\paragraph{Word charges}
labMT~1.0 gives the mean of 50 ratings of happiness on a scale from 1 to 9 for \nLabmt{} frequent English words\cite{dodds2011}. The charge of a word is $c=(h-5)/4$, so that $c=1$ at the top of the scale, 0 at its neutral point and $-1$ at the bottom. The VADER list gives the mean of 10 ratings from $-4$ to $4$ for \nVader{} words, emoticons and abbreviations\cite{hutto2014}. I put it on the labMT scale with one slope through the origin, the ratio of root-mean-square values over the \nShared{} shared words, and averaged the two values where both exist. The result has \nLexicon{} entries, of which \nCharged{} have $|c|\geq0.25$ and carry charge; the rest are buffer words, as are words outside the list. Positive charge is $h=\max(c,0)$ hedons and negative charge $d=\max(-c,0)$ dolors. Charges are stored as integers in thousandths. Adding the NRC-VAD\cite{mohammad2018vad} and Warriner\cite{warriner2013} norms ($r=\rNRCVAD$ and \rWarriner{} with labMT on shared words) raised mean agreement on development data from 0.521 to 0.534; their licences do not allow redistribution, so they are not part of the shipped lexicon and no reported result uses them.

\paragraph{Tokens and operators}
Text is lower-cased. Emoji are replaced by their names, using the list distributed with VADER, and 40 emoticons are recognised when they follow a space. Words are runs of letters and apostrophes. A full stop, exclamation mark or question mark ends a sentence; a comma, semicolon or colon ends a clause. Four closed lists of operator words carry no charge themselves: negators (\emph{not}, \emph{never}, forms ending in \emph{n't} and others), intensifiers (\emph{very}, \emph{really}, \emph{so}), diminishers (\emph{slightly}, \emph{somewhat}) and contrast words (\emph{but}, \emph{however}, \emph{yet}, \emph{nevertheless}, \emph{nonetheless}). A negator reaches the next three words or the next punctuation mark, whichever comes first; an intensifier or diminisher reaches the next two words; a contrast word reaches the end of its sentence. Every sentence end clears all operators. The scan is a finite-state transducer and uses string replacement, translation and splitting only.

\paragraph{Signature}
For a span of $L$ words with charges $h_i,d_i$ at positions $i=0,\dots,L-1$, the signature holds $L$; the number $E$ of charged words; $S^{\pm}_k$ for $k=0,\dots,3$ (equation~1); the hedons and the dolors inside the reach of each of the four operators; and $\sum_i (h_i-d_i)^2$. The fast implementation computes signatures for a batch of texts with array operations. A reference implementation loops over tokens in integer arithmetic and defines the result; the test suite checks that the two agree, and checks the propositions below for exact equality on random texts.

\prop{1} Let $A$ and $B$ be texts and let $A$ end at a sentence end. Then $\sigma(AB)=\sigma(A)\oplus\sigma(B)$, where $\oplus$ adds every entry except the position sums and applies equation~(\ref{eq:merge}) to those. The operation $\oplus$ is associative, the zero vector is its identity, and for fixed $\sigma(A)$ the map $\sigma(B)\mapsto\sigma(A)\oplus\sigma(B)$ is invertible.

\proofhead Operators do not reach across a sentence end, so each word of $B$ has the same charge and the same operator context in $AB$ as in $B$. Counts and totals therefore add. A word at position $j$ of $B$ is at position $L_A+j$ of $AB$, and expanding $(L_A+j)^k$ gives equation~(\ref{eq:merge}). Write $T_\ell$ for the linear map that equation~(\ref{eq:merge}) applies to the position sums for a shift by $\ell$. It is the matrix of the substitution $x\mapsto x+\ell$ on polynomials of degree below four in the monomial basis, so $T_\ell T_m=T_{\ell+m}$ and $T_\ell^{-1}=T_{-\ell}$, which give associativity and invertibility. \hfill$\square$

\smallskip\noindent Merging is not commutative: parts can be scored in any order and on any machine, and must be merged in reading order. With prefix signatures $\sigma_j=\sigma(s_1\cdots s_j)$ over sentences, the signature of sentences $i$ to $j$ is $T_{-L}(\sigma_j-\sigma_{i-1})$ in the position sums, with $L$ the length of the first $i-1$ sentences. In floating point this difference loses precision for high moments of short spans deep inside long texts; the integer implementation does not.

\prop{2} Let $\varphi_1,\dots,\varphi_n$ be linearly independent real sequences on positions $0,1,2,\dots$, and for a real charge sequence $c$ of length $L$ let $\Phi(c)=\bigl(L,\sum_i c_i\varphi_1(i),\dots,\sum_i c_i\varphi_n(i)\bigr)$. There is a function $F$ with $\Phi(cc')=F(\Phi(c),\Phi(c'))$ for all charge sequences $c,c'$ if and only if the span $V$ of the $\varphi_r$ is closed under the shift $(T\varphi)(i)=\varphi(i+1)$. In that case every $\varphi_r$ satisfies a linear recurrence with constant coefficients of order at most $n$, and is a combination of sequences $i^k\rho^i$, with $\rho$ a nonzero complex number, and of sequences that vanish from position $n$ onward.

\proofhead If $V$ is closed under the shift, then $T^{\ell}\varphi_r=\sum_s M^{(\ell)}_{rs}\varphi_s$ for some matrices $M^{(\ell)}$, and $\sum_j c'_j\varphi_r(L+j)=\sum_s M^{(L)}_{rs}\sum_j c'_j\varphi_s(j)$, so $\Phi(cc')$ follows from $\Phi(c)$ and $\Phi(c')$. Conversely, fix $\ell\geq1$ and let $c$ be $\ell$ zeros. Take $J$ large enough that the restrictions of the $\varphi_s$ to positions below $J$ are linearly independent, and let $c'$ range over sequences of length $J$. By assumption $\sum_j c'_j\varphi_r(\ell+j)$ depends on $c'$ only through the numbers $\sum_j c'_j\varphi_s(j)$. Adding to $c'$ any $d$ with $\sum_j d_j\varphi_s(j)=0$ for all $s$ therefore leaves it unchanged, so the linear functional $d\mapsto\sum_j d_j\varphi_r(\ell+j)$ vanishes on the common kernel of the $n$ functionals $d\mapsto\sum_j d_j\varphi_s(j)$ and is a linear combination of them. The coefficients are unique, and do not change with $J$ because the equations for $J$ are among those for $J+1$. Hence $T^{\ell}\varphi_r\in V$. The shift is then a linear map on the $n$-dimensional space $V$ and, by the Cayley--Hamilton theorem, is annihilated by its characteristic polynomial $p$, so every member of $V$ satisfies the recurrence $p(T)\varphi=0$. Its solutions are spanned by $i^k\rho^i$ for the nonzero roots $\rho$ of $p$ and, for a zero root of multiplicity $m$, by the sequences supported on positions below $m$. \hfill$\square$

\smallskip\noindent The proposition concerns summaries that are linear in the charges. It is the sequence form of the description of finite-dimensional translation-invariant function spaces\cite{anselone1964,szekelyhidi1982}, and of the fact that a function on strings has a finite-dimensional linear representation exactly when its Hankel matrix has finite rank\cite{schutzenberger1961,berstel2010}. A geometric weight $\rho^i$ gives the one-number case $S_\rho(AB)=S_\rho(A)+\rho^{L_A}S_\rho(B)$. On relative position: a weight $g\bigl((i+\tfrac12)/L\bigr)$ is available from a summary for every length $L$ only if the span of weights is closed under rescaling of position as well as under shifts. A finite-dimensional space of functions of a real variable with both properties is the space of polynomials up to some degree: closure under shifts gives exponential polynomials\cite{anselone1964}, rescaling rules out any nonzero exponent $\lambda$ because the functions $e^{\lambda sx}$, $s>0$, are linearly independent, and a shift-invariant space of polynomials contains the derivatives of its members.

\paragraph{Readouts}
Density is $1000\,(H-D)/L$. Level is equation~(\ref{eq:level}); its standard error is $1000\sqrt{\sum_i(h_i-d_i)^2-(H-D)^2/L}\,/\,(a+E+\kappa B)$, which treats the charges of a text's words as independent draws. With $x_i=(2i+1)/L-1$ and Legendre polynomials $P_k$, the moments $m_k=\sum_i (h_i-d_i)P_k(x_i)$, $k\leq3$, are linear combinations of the position sums, and the arc is $f(x)=\sum_k\frac{2k+1}{L}m_kP_k(x)$. The sampled polynomials are orthogonal up to terms of order $L^{-2}$, so $f$ is the least-squares cubic to that order; over the novels it agrees with a direct fit to $10^{-8}$. The calibrated valence is $b_0+\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{x}/(a+E+\kappa B)$, where $\mathbf{x}$ holds $H$, $D$, the moments $m_1,m_2,m_3$, the negated hedons, the negated dolors, and the net charge that is intensified, diminished, and after a contrast word.

\paragraph{Fitting and evaluation}
Ratings were standardised within each corpus with the mean and standard deviation of its training portion, and corpora were given equal total weight. For each target corpus and each pair $(a,\kappa)$ on the grid $a\in\{0,0.5,1,2,4,8,16,32\}$, $\kappa\in\{0,0.05,0.1,0.2,0.5,1,2\}$, the coefficients were fitted by ridge regression on the training portions of the other five corpora (penalty $10^{-3}$ times the mean diagonal of the weighted Gram matrix; intercept unpenalised). The pair with the highest mean Pearson $r$ on those corpora's development portions was kept, the coefficients were refitted on training plus development portions, and the target's test portion was scored once. The shipped constants ($a=\shipA$, $\kappa=\shipKappa$) are the same fit on all six corpora. Intervals are 95\% percentile intervals from 2,000 bootstrap resamples, drawing source documents for EmoBank and items elsewhere; differences between methods use the same resamples. Comparators: VADER compound score; the hedonometer, the mean labMT value of words outside its neutral band\cite{dodds2011}; TextBlob polarity; ridge regression on TF-IDF weights of words and word pairs, trained on the target's own training portion or on the other five corpora; and two transformer classifiers scored as $P(\text{positive})-P(\text{negative})$ on text truncated to 512 tokens, DistilBERT fine-tuned on SST-2\cite{sanh2019} and a RoBERTa model fine-tuned on tweets\cite{loureiro2022}. The transformers were run on a seeded sample of 5,000 Yelp test reviews, and for Yelp all methods are compared on that sample.

\paragraph{Refined charges}
With exposure $a+L$ and operator multipliers $\theta$ from a one-channel fit, the predicted rating of text $t$ is $b_0+s\sum_i \mu_i c_{w_i}/(a+L_t)$ with $\mu_i=1+\theta\cdot(\text{operator flags of word }i)$, which is linear in the vector $c$ of word charges. The refined charges minimise the weighted squared error plus $\lambda\lVert s(c-c^{0})\rVert^2$, where $c^0$ holds the norm charges and words that occur in at least 25 training texts start at zero. The penalty $\lambda\in\{0.03,0.1,0.3\}$ was chosen on development data of the fitting corpora, and the readout was then refitted with the refined charges. Each step is a ridge regression; the sequence is not a joint optimum.

\paragraph{Position and dilution}
For where charge sits, each text of at least ten words contributes $\sum_i|c_i|x_i$ and $\sum_i|c_i|$, and the statistic is the ratio of their sums over texts. Besides the six rated corpora, the units were paragraphs of the film reviews and chapters of 150 of the novels. The position kernel of a corpus is $1+(b_1P_1+b_2P_2+b_3P_3)/b_0$ from a one-channel fit to all its portions with the shipped $a$ and $\kappa$; the contrast is its mean over the first third minus its mean over the last third, with intervals from 1,000 resamples. For the treebank, every binary node of the training trees whose phrase and both parts have ratings in the phrase dictionary was used (\treeMatch\% of nodes); ratings were centred at 0.5, the phrase rating was regressed on the two part ratings, and sentences were resampled. For dilution in the treebank, a part counts as rated if its centred rating is at least 0.15 in absolute value and as neutral if at most 0.05; the share kept is the ratio of sums of the signed phrase rating to the absolute part rating. For dilution in the corpora, rated intensity is the absolute deviation of a rating from the corpus median, and the partial rank correlation with the number of buffer words removes the rank of total charge from both by linear regression. For EmoBank documents of eight or more sentences, $|V-3|$ was regressed on relative sentence position with document means removed, resampling documents.

\paragraph{Novels, fairy tales and critics}
From a seeded sample of English fiction in the Project Gutenberg catalogue, \nNovelFiles{} texts were fetched from a mirror, and \nNovels{} had at least ten chapters of 300 or more words and 20,000 words in all after removal of front matter. Chapters were found by headings and closed with a full stop. The null for chapter differences shuffles a novel's sentences 200 times and keeps the number of sentences in each chapter. Arcs were compared on 100 points; the moving window is 10\% of the words. The fairy-tale annotations\cite{alm2005} give two annotators' emotion labels for each sentence; a label of happy or positively surprised was scored $+1$, neutral 0, and any other emotion $-1$, and the two were averaged. Critic comparisons use all \nCritReviews{} reviews with the four-level rating as stratum; intervals resample reviews within critic.

\paragraph{Pre-specification}
The method, the corpora, the outcomes, the comparators and the decision rules for the hypotheses were written into an analysis plan and committed to the repository (commit \texttt{7524c58}) before any evaluation data were scored. Test portions were first scored with the plan as written. Deviations since then are listed in the plan: the grid for $a$ and $\kappa$ was widened after the first run chose its upper edge, without changing the choice; the tokeniser was rewritten for speed; a bug in the selection of constants for the uncalibrated level was fixed; refined charges, the bag-of-words ridge trained on other corpora, the treebank analyses and the check of error against exposure were added and are exploratory. Four addenda were each committed before the data they concern were generated or examined: the comparison of human and machine writing (commit \texttt{4b10b12}), the recency hypothesis and its three tests (\texttt{77d4fa2}), the rewrites with prose skills (\texttt{090411d}) and the dial (\texttt{de181ec}). Where a model declined a request, the text is missing and the essay leaves the paired comparison; no declined request was retried. A February 2026 version of the method, built on word embeddings, failed its own criterion of matching VADER on GoEmotions; the criterion carried over here is a mean $r$ over the primary corpora at least that of VADER.
