\section{Methods}
\footnotesize

\paragraph{Word charges}
labMT~1.0 gives the mean of 50 ratings of happiness on a scale from 1 to 9 for \nLabmt{} frequent English words\cite{dodds2011}. The charge of a word is $c=(h-5)/4$, so that $c=1$ at the top of the scale, 0 at its neutral point and $-1$ at the bottom. The VADER list gives the mean of 10 ratings from $-4$ to $4$ for \nVader{} words, emoticons and abbreviations\cite{hutto2014}. It was put on the labMT scale with one slope through the origin, the ratio of root-mean-square values over the \nShared{} shared words, and averaged the two values where both exist. The result has \nLexicon{} entries, of which \nCharged{} have $|c|\geq0.25$. Operator words are given no charge, which leaves \nChargedScorer{} entries that carry charge; the rest are buffer words, as are words outside the list. Positive charge is $h=\max(c,0)$ hedons and negative charge $d=\max(-c,0)$ dolors. Charges are stored as integers in thousandths. Adding the NRC-VAD\cite{mohammad2018vad} and Warriner\cite{warriner2013} norms ($r=\rNRCVAD$ and \rWarriner{} with labMT on shared words) raised mean agreement on development data from 0.521 to 0.534; their licences do not allow redistribution, so they are not part of the shipped lexicon and no reported result uses them.

\paragraph{Tokens and operators}
Text is lower-cased. Emoji are replaced by their names, using the list distributed with VADER, and 40 emoticons are recognised when they follow a space. Words are runs of letters and apostrophes. A full stop, exclamation mark or question mark ends a sentence; a comma, semicolon or colon ends a clause. Four closed lists of operator words carry no charge themselves: negators (\emph{not}, \emph{never}, forms ending in \emph{n't} and others), twenty intensifiers (\emph{very}, \emph{really}, \emph{extremely}, \emph{absolutely}, \emph{completely}, \emph{totally}, \emph{utterly}, \emph{incredibly}, \emph{so}, \emph{too}, \emph{highly}, \emph{especially}, \emph{particularly}, \emph{truly}, \emph{quite}, \emph{super}, \emph{such}, \emph{most}, \emph{deeply}, \emph{thoroughly}), diminishers (\emph{slightly}, \emph{somewhat} and others) and contrast words (\emph{but}, \emph{however}, \emph{yet}, \emph{nevertheless}, \emph{nonetheless}). A negator reaches the next three words or the next punctuation mark, whichever comes first; an intensifier or diminisher reaches the next two words or the next punctuation mark, whichever comes first; a contrast word reaches the end of its sentence. Every sentence end clears all operators. The scan is a finite-state transducer and uses string replacement, translation and splitting only.

\paragraph{Signature}
For a span of $L$ words with charges $h_i,d_i$ at positions $i=0,\dots,L-1$, the signature holds $L$; the number $E$ of charged words; $S^{\pm}_k$ for $k=0,\dots,3$ (equation~1); the hedons and the dolors inside the reach of each of the four operators; and $\sum_i (h_i-d_i)^2$. The fast implementation computes signatures for a batch of texts with array operations. A reference implementation loops over tokens in integer arithmetic and defines the result; the test suite checks that the two agree, and checks Proposition~1, the two-row prefix query and merging at cuts inside a sentence for exact equality on random texts.

\prop{1} Let $A$ and $B$ be texts, joined by white space, and let $A$ end at a sentence end. Then $\sigma(AB)=\sigma(A)\oplus\sigma(B)$, where $\oplus$ adds every entry except the position sums and applies equation~(\ref{eq:merge}) to those. The operation $\oplus$ is associative, the zero vector is its identity, and for fixed $\sigma(A)$ the map $\sigma(B)\mapsto\sigma(A)\oplus\sigma(B)$ is invertible.

\proofhead Operators do not reach across a sentence end, so each word of $B$ has the same charge and the same operator context in $AB$ as in $B$. Counts and totals therefore add. A word at position $j$ of $B$ is at position $L_A+j$ of $AB$, and expanding $(L_A+j)^k$ gives equation~(\ref{eq:merge}). Write $T_\ell$ for the linear map that equation~(\ref{eq:merge}) applies to the position sums for a shift by $\ell$. It is the matrix of the substitution $x\mapsto x+\ell$ on polynomials of degree below four in the monomial basis, so $T_\ell T_m=T_{\ell+m}$ and $T_\ell^{-1}=T_{-\ell}$, which give associativity and invertibility. \hfill$\square$

\smallskip\noindent Merging is not commutative: parts can be scored in any order and on any machine, and must be merged in reading order. With prefix signatures $\sigma_j=\sigma(s_1\cdots s_j)$ over sentences, the signature of sentences $i$ to $j$ is $T_{-L}(\sigma_j-\sigma_{i-1})$ in the position sums, with $L$ the length of the first $i-1$ sentences. In floating point this difference keeps the counts and totals and loses the higher position sums of short spans deep in long texts: for one sentence at the end of 200,000 words the cubic sum can be wrong by more than its own size. The integer implementation is exact, and in floats the position sums of such a span are obtained by merging its own rows forward, which is accurate at any depth.

\prop{2} Let $\varphi_1,\dots,\varphi_n$ be linearly independent real sequences on positions $0,1,2,\dots$, and for a real charge sequence $c$ of length $L$ let $\Phi(c)=\bigl(L,\sum_i c_i\varphi_1(i),\dots,\sum_i c_i\varphi_n(i)\bigr)$. There is a function $F$ with $\Phi(cc')=F(\Phi(c),\Phi(c'))$ for all charge sequences $c,c'$ if and only if the span $V$ of the $\varphi_r$ is closed under the shift $(T\varphi)(i)=\varphi(i+1)$. In that case every $\varphi_r$ satisfies a linear recurrence with constant coefficients of order at most $n$, and is a combination of sequences $i^k\rho^i$, with $\rho$ a nonzero complex number, and of sequences that vanish from position $n$ onward.

\proofhead If $V$ is closed under the shift, then $T^{\ell}\varphi_r=\sum_s M^{(\ell)}_{rs}\varphi_s$ for some matrices $M^{(\ell)}$, and $\sum_j c'_j\varphi_r(L+j)=\sum_s M^{(L)}_{rs}\sum_j c'_j\varphi_s(j)$, so $\Phi(cc')$ follows from $\Phi(c)$ and $\Phi(c')$. Conversely, fix $\ell\geq1$ and let $c$ be $\ell$ zeros. Take $J$ large enough that the restrictions of the $\varphi_s$ to positions below $J$ are linearly independent, and let $c'$ range over sequences of length $J$. By assumption $\sum_j c'_j\varphi_r(\ell+j)$ depends on $c'$ only through the numbers $\sum_j c'_j\varphi_s(j)$. Adding to $c'$ any $d$ with $\sum_j d_j\varphi_s(j)=0$ for all $s$ therefore leaves it unchanged, so the linear functional $d\mapsto\sum_j d_j\varphi_r(\ell+j)$ vanishes on the common kernel of the $n$ functionals $d\mapsto\sum_j d_j\varphi_s(j)$ and is a linear combination of them. The coefficients are unique, and do not change with $J$ because the equations for $J$ are among those for $J+1$. Hence $T^{\ell}\varphi_r\in V$. The shift is then a linear map on the $n$-dimensional space $V$ and, by the Cayley--Hamilton theorem, is annihilated by its characteristic polynomial $p$, so every member of $V$ satisfies the recurrence $p(T)\varphi=0$. Its solutions are spanned by $i^k\rho^i$ for the nonzero roots $\rho$ of $p$ and, for a zero root of multiplicity $m$, by the sequences supported on positions below $m$. \hfill$\square$

\smallskip\noindent The proposition concerns summaries that are linear in the charges. It is the sequence form of the description of finite-dimensional translation-invariant function spaces\cite{anselone1964,szekelyhidi1982}, and of the fact that a function on strings has a finite-dimensional linear representation exactly when its Hankel matrix has finite rank\cite{schutzenberger1961,berstel2010}. A geometric weight $\rho^i$ gives the one-number case $S_\rho(AB)=S_\rho(A)+\rho^{L_A}S_\rho(B)$. On relative position: suppose a continuous function $g$ on $[0,1]$ is available from one summary for every length, meaning that for each $L$ the sequence $g\bigl((i+\tfrac12)/L\bigr)$, $0\le i<L$, agrees on those positions with a member of a fixed shift-closed space $V$ of dimension $n$. Then $g$ is a polynomial of degree below $n$. Let $p$ be the characteristic polynomial of the shift on $V$ and write $p(z)=(z-1)^m q(z)$ with $q(1)\neq0$. For a smooth test function $\psi$ supported inside $(0,1)$ and $L$ large, summing $\psi(i/L)$ against $p(T)$ applied to the sequence gives zero, and summation by parts moves the operator onto $\psi$: $\sum_j g\bigl((j+\tfrac12)/L\bigr)\,[p(T^{-1})\psi_L](j)=0$ with $\psi_L(j)=\psi(j/L)$. Since $(T^{-1}-1)^m\psi_L=(-1)^mL^{-m}\psi^{(m)}(j/L)+O(L^{-m-1})$ and $q(T^{-1})\psi_L=q(1)\psi_L+O(L^{-1})$, multiplying by $L^{m-1}$ and letting $L$ grow turns the sum into $(-1)^mq(1)\int_0^1 g\,\psi^{(m)}=0$. So the $m$th derivative of $g$ vanishes as a distribution, and $g$ is a polynomial of degree below $m\le n$. Continuity is needed: the points $(i+\tfrac12)/L$ for odd $L$ and for even $L$ form disjoint sets, so the function equal to 1 at the points of odd lengths and 0 elsewhere gives the total charge for odd $L$ and 0 for even $L$; the summary $(L,S_0)$ supplies it, although it is not a polynomial. Conversely the signature supplies every polynomial weight of degree three or less.

\paragraph{Readouts}
Density is $1000\,(H-D)/L$. Level is equation~(\ref{eq:level}), a ratio $R=(H-D)/(a+E+\kappa B)$ whose numerator and denominator both vary from word to word: a word adds its charge $c_i=h_i-d_i$ to the first and $w_i$ to the second, with $w_i=1$ for a charged word and $\kappa$ for a buffer word. Treating words as independent draws, the delta method gives the standard error $1000\sqrt{\sum_iu_i^2-(Ra)^2/L}\,/\,(a+E+\kappa B)$ with $u_i=c_i-Rw_i$ and $\sum_iu_i^2=\sum_ic_i^2-2R(H-D)+R^2(E+\kappa^2B)$, all read from the signature. For density ($a=0$, $\kappa=1$) it is the standard error of a mean, with the variance of the charges taken over $L$ rather than $L-1$. In simulated texts of independent words it matches the spread of the level to within about 4\% at 200 words and 5\% at 50; being a first-order approximation, it is about 10\% too small at 20 words and about 20\% too small at 10. An earlier draft divided the standard deviation of the numerator by the exposure, which overstates the error when most charge has one sign. With $x_i=(2i+1)/L-1$ and Legendre polynomials $P_k$, the moments $m_k=\sum_i (h_i-d_i)P_k(x_i)$, $k\leq3$, are linear combinations of the position sums, and the arc is $f(x)=\sum_k\frac{2k+1}{L}m_kP_k(x)$. The sampled polynomials are orthogonal up to terms of order $L^{-2}$, so $f$ is the least-squares cubic to that order: in random texts the largest difference between the two is about 7\% of the cubic's range (largest minus smallest value) at 10 words, at most 12\%, about 2\% at 20 words and 1\% at 30, and the two agree to one part in $10^5$ over the novels. Below ten words the arc is a poor approximation (about 30\% of the range apart at five words), and below four words no cubic is determined; the implementation then returns no arc. The calibrated valence is $b_0+\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{x}/(a+E+\kappa B)$, where $\mathbf{x}$ holds $H$, $D$, the moments $m_1,m_2,m_3$, the negated hedons, the negated dolors, and the net charge that is intensified, diminished, and after a contrast word.

\paragraph{Fitting and evaluation}
Ratings were standardised within each corpus with the mean and standard deviation of its training portion, and corpora were given equal total weight. For each target corpus and each pair $(a,\kappa)$ on the grid $a\in\{0,0.5,1,2,4,8,16,32\}$, $\kappa\in\{0,0.05,0.1,0.2,0.5,1,2\}$, the coefficients were fitted by ridge regression on the training portions of the other five corpora (penalty $10^{-3}$ times the mean diagonal of the weighted Gram matrix; intercept unpenalised). The pair with the highest mean Pearson $r$ on those corpora's development portions was kept, the coefficients were refitted on training plus development portions, and the target's test portion was scored once. The shipped constants ($a=\shipA$, $\kappa=\shipKappa$) are the same fit on all six corpora. Intervals are 95\% percentile bootstrap intervals, drawing source documents for EmoBank and items elsewhere; differences between methods use the same resamples. Pearson $r$ and differences between methods use 2,000 resamples, as planned. To bound running time Spearman's $\rho$ uses 500, and the position, dilution, treebank and machine-writing analyses use between 100 and 2,000; the scripts give each count. The interval for the mean difference from VADER is a $t$ interval over the five primary corpora. Comparators: VADER's compound score (version 3.3.2) for the whole text, as planned, and, added after review, its mean over the sentences of the text; the hedonometer, the mean labMT value of words outside its neutral band\cite{dodds2011}; the polarity score of the TextBlob library (version 0.20.1); ridge regression on TF-IDF weights of words and word pairs, trained on the target's own training portion or on the other five corpora; and two transformer classifiers scored as $P(\text{positive})-P(\text{negative})$ on text truncated to 512 tokens, DistilBERT fine-tuned on SST-2\cite{sanh2019} and a RoBERTa model fine-tuned on tweets\cite{loureiro2022}. Both were used as distributed, with no tuning to these corpora, and text was passed to them unchanged, without the replacement of user handles that the RoBERTa model's documentation prescribes for tweets. The transformers were run on a seeded sample of 5,000 Yelp test reviews, and for Yelp all methods are compared on that sample.

\paragraph{Refined charges}
With exposure $a+L$ and operator multipliers $\theta$ from a one-channel fit, the predicted rating of text $t$ is $b_0+s\sum_i \mu_i c_{w_i}/(a+L_t)$ with $\mu_i=1+\theta\cdot(\text{operator flags of word }i)$, which is linear in the vector $c$ of word charges. The refined charges minimise the weighted squared error plus $\lambda\lVert s(c-c^{0})\rVert^2$, where $c^0$ holds the norm charges and words that occur in at least 25 training texts start at zero. The penalty $\lambda\in\{0.03,0.1,0.3\}$ was chosen on development data of the fitting corpora, and the readout was then refitted with the refined charges. Each step is a ridge regression; the sequence is not a joint optimum.

\paragraph{Position and dilution}
For where charge sits (planned, H1a), each text of at least ten words contributes $\sum_i|c_i|x_i$ and $\sum_i|c_i|$, and the statistic is the ratio of their sums over texts. Besides the six rated corpora, the units were paragraphs of the film reviews and chapters of 150 of the novels, so the eight kinds of unit come from seven bodies of text. The position kernel of a corpus (planned, H1b) is $1+(b_1P_1+b_2P_2+b_3P_3)/b_0$ from a one-channel fit to all its portions with the shipped $a$ and $\kappa$; the contrast is its mean over the first third minus its mean over the last third. For EmoBank documents of eight or more sentences (planned, H1c), $|V-3|$ was regressed on relative sentence position with document means removed, resampling documents. The planned test of dilution (H2) is the paired difference in held-out $r$ between the calibrated readout and the same readout refitted with $\kappa=0$.

The remaining analyses were not in the plan. For dilution in the corpora, rated intensity is the absolute deviation of a rating from the corpus median, and the partial rank correlation with the number of buffer words removes the rank of total charge from both by linear regression. The curves of agreement against $\kappa$ are fits to single corpora with $a$ fixed at the shipped value, on a grid extended to $\kappa=4$. For the treebank, every binary node whose phrase and both parts have ratings in the phrase dictionary was used (\treeMatch\% of nodes in the training trees); ratings were centred at 0.5, the phrase rating was regressed on the two part ratings, and sentences were resampled. With length held fixed, nodes whose parts both have two or more words were split by which part is longer, and the weights were also fitted as one step function of a part's share of the phrase's words in ten bins, shared by both parts, plus a constant for the later part; a second fit gives each part its own step function (\figref{fig:position}b). For dilution in the treebank, a part counts as rated if its centred rating is at least 0.15 in absolute value and as neutral if at most 0.05; the share kept is the ratio of sums of the signed phrase rating to the absolute part rating. The matched comparison of sides weights cells defined by the length of the rated part and the length of the neutral part (1, 2, 3 to 4, 5 to 8, 9 or more words each) by the smaller of their two counts. These analyses are reported for all trees, with the development and test trees alone where the recency tests are discussed.

\paragraph{Recency tests}
The second addendum to the plan fixed three tests. R1: in binary nodes of the treebank's development and test trees with both parts of two or more words, the weight of the later part minus that of the earlier part in the regression of the phrase rating on the two part ratings. R2: in the same trees, the share of its rating that a rated part keeps when two or more neutral words come before it, minus the share when they follow it. R3: in the \confReviews{} reviews of 100 or more words in the test portion of the IMDB corpus\cite{maas2011}, the area under the curve for telling favourable from unfavourable reviews by net charge per word of the last fifth, minus the same for the first fifth. Each was to count as support if its 95\% bootstrap interval lay above zero (2,000 resamples of sentences for R1 and R2, 1,000 of reviews for R3). All three did. The controls for length described above were not part of the addendum.

\paragraph{Novels, fairy tales and critics}
From a seeded sample of English fiction in the Project Gutenberg catalogue, \nNovelFiles{} texts were fetched from a mirror, and \nNovels{} had at least ten chapters of 300 or more words and 20,000 words in all after removal of front matter. Chapters were found by headings, sections under 300 words were dropped, and each chapter was closed with a full stop. The rule is approximate. In \emph{Moby-Dick} it finds \mobyChapters{} sections for the book's 135 chapters and epilogue: it drops the chapters shorter than 300 words, joins the epilogue to the last chapter, and keeps one block of transcriber's notes as a section. The null for chapter differences shuffles a novel's sentences 200 times and keeps the number of sentences in each chapter, so the smallest attainable $p$ is $1/201$. The comparison of chunk sizes with VADER used the first \vaderBooks{} novels in file order. Arcs were compared on 100 points; the moving window is 10\% of the words. The fairy-tale annotations\cite{alm2005} give two annotators' emotion labels for each sentence; a label of happy or positively surprised was scored $+1$, neutral 0, and any other emotion $-1$, and the two were averaged. Critic comparisons use all \nCritReviews{} reviews with the four-level rating as stratum; intervals resample reviews within critic. The film reviews were scored as distributed. Credits lines, reviewers' sign-off lines and footers were not removed; they sit at the start and the end of a review, where position is measured, and one critic's standard footer, an invitation to visit his web page, closes \footWith{} of his \footReviews{} reviews and adds \footAdded{} per thousand words to his pooled density.

\paragraph{Pre-specification}
The method, the corpora, the outcomes, the comparators and the decision rules for the hypotheses were written into an analysis plan and committed to the project's git repository (commit \texttt{0c195da}) before any evaluation data were scored. Four addenda were committed before the data they concern were generated or examined: the comparison of human and machine writing (\texttt{070d1ba}), the recency hypothesis and its three tests (\texttt{6b4ba13}), the rewrites with prose skills (\texttt{efe3fce}) and the dial (\texttt{f0f0e8f}). This is a record kept by the author and not an independent registration. The commits were made in a local repository over one evening, they carry the timestamps of the author's machine, and they were pushed to the public repository only when the study was complete. The repository's history was later rewritten to remove credentials left in two unrelated 2024 scripts and a Windows executable; no other file and no commit date changed, and the identifiers given here are those after the rewrite. Some of these commit messages say `pre-register'; the record is the author's own, as stated above. The order of file times agrees with the stated order, with one exception: the note that narrows the essay sample to eight assignments was committed together with the second addendum, after the essays had been written.

Test portions were first scored with the plan as written; the results of that first run were not kept, so the statement that the changes below left the accuracy criterion met cannot be checked from the repository. Deviations, all listed in the plan: the grid for $a$ and $\kappa$ was widened after the first run chose its upper edge, without changing the choice; the tokeniser was rewritten for speed; a bug in the selection of constants for the uncalibrated level was fixed; the VADER list was put on the labMT scale by a ratio of root-mean-square values where the plan said regression; several bootstraps use fewer resamples than the planned 2,000; the essay intervals are jackknife intervals; the planned report of how often the document score and the mean of sentence scores differ in sign was at first made for the transformers only, and VADER was added later; and the classifier for the dial was first trained on a set that left out only the essays with both arms complete, then retrained leaving out all 60 sampled essays. Refined charges, the bag-of-words ridge trained on other corpora, the partial correlations, the curves against $\kappa$, every treebank analysis, the readings of the constants, the check of error against exposure and the second question to the judge were added after the first results and are exploratory. The length-controlled treebank analysis, the jackknife intervals, the table of HC3 by source, the check of refined charges for words about groups, sentence-averaged VADER, the corrected standard error of the level, the literal last-fifth check, the check on ambiguous intensifiers and the count of informal spellings were added in response to reports by referee agents. Where a model declined a request, the text is missing and the essay leaves the paired comparison; no declined request was retried. A February 2026 version of the method, built on word embeddings, failed its own criterion of matching VADER on GoEmotions\cite{demszky2020}; the criterion carried over here is a mean $r$ over the primary corpora at least that of VADER.
