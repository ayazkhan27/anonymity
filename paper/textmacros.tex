\newcommand{\machineMethods}{\paragraph{Human and machine writing}
The ten measures are: hedons, dolors, net charge and total charge per thousand words; the percentage of words that are charged; the percentage of charge that is negated, intensified, and after a contrast word; ending lift, the mean of the cubic arc over the last fifth of the text minus the text's density; and sentence clustering, the between-sentence variance of density divided by its expected value if the text's words were dealt to its sentences at random, $\sigma^2(m-1)/L$ for $m$ sentences, $L$ words and word-level variance $\sigma^2$ of net charge. None grows with the length of a text, though all are noisier in short ones. From HC3\cite{guo2023} I took the first human answer and the first ChatGPT answer to each question and kept the \hcN{} questions where both have at least 30 words; contractions split by the corpus's tokenisation of human answers were rejoined. From PERSUADE~2.0\cite{crossley2024} I drew 20 essays of 150 to 800 words for each of the eight assignments that need no source text (\esN{} essays, seed 0). Each essay was matched by two machine essays written to the same assignment and word count, one with no further instruction and one with the instruction to write as a student of the same grade would in a timed test. Two of the 320 requests produced no essay, both under the plain instruction.

The writers were subagents of the coding assistant, one essay per agent, running Claude Fable~5.2, Opus~5.5, Sonnet~5.5 and Haiku~5.5 in rotation at low reasoning effort. Each agent's prompt held its task and a file path to write to. The harness also attached its standing context to every agent: an index of the author's project notes, a coding-style plug-in and the author's most recent message to the assistant. For the essays that message described the planned comparison of machine and human essays; for the dial revisions and the judges it stated the author's hope that steering by these measures would make machine text read as more human. The file paths named the experimental arm. The agents were therefore not blind to the project, and the revisers and the judge were not blind to the hoped-for result. The job files and the workflow scripts that dispatched them, with the judge's instruction, are in the repository; the agents' transcripts are not.

Differences are paired by essay or question and standardised by the human standard deviation. HC3 intervals are percentile bootstrap intervals over questions. For the essays the plan named a bootstrap over the eight assignments. A percentile bootstrap over eight clusters is too narrow, so the printed intervals are from a delete-one-assignment jackknife with a $t$ reference on seven degrees of freedom, and the planned verdicts are stated where they differ. Resampling essays, HL3 is refuted for essays written as a student, which cluster more than the students' and not less. The classifier is a logistic regression on the ten standardised measures, scored out of fold with all texts of an assignment or question in one fold; its intervals are bootstrap intervals over assignments or questions.

For the tone check, 60 plain-instruction drafts, 15 per model drawn with one seed, were each revised by the model that wrote them under four instructions: the control instruction, to revise the draft so that it reads as a student of that grade wrote it; the control instruction plus a report listing each measure on which the draft lay outside the middle 80\% of student essays on other assignments; and the control instruction plus an instruction to do the rewrite with one of two published prose skills for removing the marks of machine writing (\emph{humanizer} and \emph{sepia-recreate}). Distance is the mean over measures of the squared standardised difference from the mean of student essays on other assignments. The model declined \rvDeclined{} of the 240 revision requests, stating that making machine text read as a student's work would misrepresent authorship; essays with all four revisions (\rvN, from \rvAssignments{} of the eight assignments) enter the comparison. The dial used 60 further drafts drawn the same way and also covers \dlAssignments{} assignments. Its classifier was trained on the \dlTrain{} essay pairs outside that sample. The judge was Claude Opus~5.5, or Sonnet~5.5 for drafts written by Opus; shares chosen by the judge are given with exact binomial intervals that treat pairs as independent.}

\newcommand{\dialParagraphs}{Can the measures steer a revision? For \rvN{} plain machine essays, the model that wrote each draft revised it under four instructions: a control instruction, to make it read as a student of that grade wrote it; the control instruction plus a report naming each measure on which the draft lay outside the middle 80\% of student essays on other assignments; and the control instruction plus one of two published prose skills written to remove the marks of machine writing (Methods). The planned outcome was a text's mean squared standardised distance from the centre of student essays. It turned out to measure how typical a text is, and not how student-like. The student essays themselves average \rvHuman{} on it, because real essays scatter around their own centre, while drafts average \rvDraft, control revisions \rvControl, revisions made with a skill \rvHumanizer{} and \rvSepia, and revisions that saw the report \rvReport{} (\figref{fig:machine}c). A smaller distance means a more average essay. On the planned test the report is not distinguishable from the control (difference \dRvRC{} \dRvRCCIjack), and neither are the skills (\dRvHC{} \dRvHCCIjack{} and \dRvSC{} \dRvSCCIjack). On the secondary outcome, the mean machine score of a classifier trained on other essays, the drafts are at \rvScoreDraft, the four kinds of revision between \rvScoreLo{} and \rvScoreHi, and the students at \rvScoreHuman{} (\figref{fig:machine}d). Every kind of revision moved a little toward the students, and none came close. The report did move what it named. It cut the mean ending lift from \rvLiftDraft{} to \rvLiftReport{} standard deviations, against \rvLiftControl{} for the control, and left intensifier use where it was (\rvBoostDraft{} to \rvBoostReport), because a draft low on intensifiers is still inside the wide human range and was not flagged.

A report of ranges is a weak instrument. It acts only on drafts outside a wide band, and if it worked perfectly it would pull every draft to one centre. A dial is stronger. Each of \dlRequested{} further drafts was assigned the measured profile of one real student essay, drawn at random from other assignments, and was revised twice by its author model with a sheet giving the current and the target value of each measure; a control arm revised the same drafts twice with the plain instruction. The smallest of the four models declined \dlMissingHaiku{} of its 15 dial requests, so the result rests on three models and on the \dlN{} essays with both arms complete. The dial was followed. Achieved values correlate with assigned targets at a mean of \dlCorrDial{} over the ten measures in the dial arm and \dlCorrControl{} in the control arm (difference \dDlCorr{} \dDlCorrCIjack; \figref{fig:machine}e), at \dlCorrModelLo{} to \dlCorrModelHi{} for each of the three models, and from \dlCorrMax{} for the best-followed measure to \dlCorrMin{} for sentence clustering. The mean squared gap to the target fell from \dlGapDraft{} to \dlGapDialOne{} after one round and \dlGapDial{} after two, while the control arm stayed at \dlGapControl.

Following the dial did not make the essays more like student writing than a plain revision did. The classifier's mean machine score is \dlScoreDraft{} for the drafts and \dlScoreHuman{} for the matched student essays; two plain revisions bring it to \dlScoreControl{} and two rounds with the dial to \dlScoreDial{} (difference \dDlScore{} \dDlScoreCIjack, undecided). A model judge read both revisions of each draft in random order without being told which was which, though it was not blind to the study (Methods). It chose the dial revision as the better written in \dlJudgeWins{} of \dlJudgeN{} pairs (exact 95\% interval \dlJudgeExact\%). That passes the planned test, that the dial revision not be chosen less than half the time, a test lenient enough that passing it says little. The preference came from two of the writers: \dlJudgeOpus{} pairs for drafts by Opus and \dlJudgeFable{} for Fable, against \dlJudgeSonnet{} for Sonnet and \dlJudgeHaiku{} for Haiku. Asked a second question that the plan did not list, which revision reads more like a student's own work, the judge chose the dial revision in \dlJudgeStudentWins{} of \dlJudgeN{} pairs (\dlJudgeStudentExact\%), and never on both questions. The dial revisions read as more polished, and polish is not what a student's essay has. Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:excerpt} shows the essay at the median improvement, chosen by rule and not by eye. To raise its positive charge the dial revision closes on \emph{a fun, wonderful summer}, and its ending lift moved away from the target.

The measures therefore give a model a working control over how its text carries feeling, as far as a target can be reached by word choice in two passes. That is narrower than writing as a person does. A plain instruction to write as a student did as much by the classifier and more by the judge.

One more observation belongs here. These experiments ask a model to make machine text resemble student writing, and the model sometimes refused: \rvDeclined{} of 240 requests in the first revision experiment, and \dlMissing{} of \dlRequested{} essays in the dial arm were left unfinished for that reason, all \dlMissingHaiku{} by the smallest of the four models. The refusals said that passing machine text off as a student's work misrepresents authorship. The tools released with this paper report tone against a human range for writers who disclose their use of a model, and the separations measured above are too weak for these measures to serve as a detector or to defeat one.}
