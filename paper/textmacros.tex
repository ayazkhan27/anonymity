\newcommand{\machineMethods}{\paragraph{Human and machine writing}
The ten measures are: hedons, dolors, net charge and total charge per thousand words; the percentage of words that are charged; the percentage of charge that is negated, intensified, and after a contrast word; ending lift, the mean of the cubic arc over the last fifth of the text minus the text's density; and sentence clustering, the between-sentence variance of density, weighted by words, divided by its expected value if the text's words were dealt to its sentences at random, $\sigma^2(m-1)/L$ for $m$ sentences, $L$ words and word-level variance $\sigma^2$ of net charge. None grows with the length of a text, though all are noisier in short ones. Two have caveats. The cubic arc weighs words up to the end of the text, the last ones most, so ending lift is not the density of the last fifth; that literal quantity is reported beside it. Clustering is 1 under the null for sentences of any length, but where charge is correlated within sentences it grows with sentence length, so a difference in clustering can reflect a difference in sentence length. From HC3\cite{guo2023} the first human answer and the first ChatGPT answer to each question were taken, keeping the \hcN{} questions where both have at least 30 words; contractions split by the corpus's tokenisation of human answers were rejoined. From PERSUADE~2.0\cite{crossley2024}, 20 essays of 150 to 800 words were drawn for each of the eight assignments that need no source text (\esN{} essays, seed 0). Each essay was matched by two machine essays written to the same assignment and word count, one with no further instruction and one with the instruction to write as a student of the same grade would in a timed test. Two of the 320 requests produced no essay, both under the plain instruction: the agents set the task aside as unrelated to the message the harness had relayed to them (below).

The writers were subagents of the coding assistant, one essay per agent, running Claude Fable~5.2, Opus~5.5, Sonnet~5.5 and Haiku~5.5 in rotation at low reasoning effort. Each agent's prompt held its task and a file path to write to. The harness also attached its standing context to every agent: an index of the author's project notes, a coding-style plug-in and the author's most recent message to the assistant. For the essays that message described the planned comparison of machine and human essays; for the dial revisions and the judges it stated the author's hope that steering by these measures would make machine text read as more human. The file paths named the experimental arm. The agents were therefore not blind to the project, and the revisers and the judge were not blind to the hoped-for result. The job files and the workflow scripts that dispatched them, with the judge's instruction, are in the repository, as is the final message of each of the \dcTotal{} requests that left no text (\texttt{machine\_text/declined\_requests.json}); full transcripts are not.

Differences are paired by essay or question and standardised by the human standard deviation. HC3 intervals are percentile bootstrap intervals over questions. For the essays the plan named a bootstrap over the eight assignments. A percentile bootstrap over eight clusters is too narrow, so the printed intervals are from a delete-one-assignment jackknife with a $t$ reference on seven degrees of freedom (six for the tone check and the dial, which cover seven assignments), and the planned verdicts are stated where they differ. A bootstrap over essays, which is neither the planned rule nor the printed one, would call HL3 refuted for essays written as a student, which cluster more than the students' essays. The classifier is a logistic regression on the ten standardised measures, scored out of fold with all texts of an assignment or question in one fold; its intervals are bootstrap intervals over questions for HC3 and jackknife intervals over assignments for the essays. The check on intensifiers recomputes that measure with \emph{so}, \emph{too}, \emph{such} and \emph{most} taken off the list of intensifiers.

For the tone check, 60 plain-instruction drafts, 15 per model drawn with one seed, were each revised by the model that wrote them under four instructions: the control instruction, to revise the draft so that it reads as a student of that grade wrote it; the control instruction plus a report listing each measure on which the draft lay outside the middle 80\% of student essays on other assignments; and the control instruction plus an instruction to do the rewrite with one of two published prose skills for removing the marks of machine writing (\emph{humanizer}, built on Wikipedia's page on signs of AI writing, and \emph{sepia-recreate}; both are public plug-ins for the coding assistant). Distance is the mean over measures of the squared standardised difference from the mean of student essays on other assignments. The smallest model declined \rvDeclined{} of the 240 revision requests, stating that making machine text read as a student's work would misrepresent authorship; essays with all four revisions (\rvN, from \rvAssignments{} of the eight assignments) enter the comparison. The dial used 60 further drafts drawn the same way; its \dlN{} complete pairs come from \dlAssignments{} assignments in clusters of \dlClusters{} essays. One control revision was reported as written and left no file. The dial's classifier was trained on the \dlTrain{} essay pairs outside the dial sample. The judge was Claude Opus~5.5, or Sonnet~5.5 for drafts written by Opus; shares chosen by the judge are given with exact binomial intervals that treat pairs as independent, which the split by writer shows they are not. The judge saw no real student essay, and the key to the arms was a file in the same folder as its job files. Informal spellings are counted from a fixed list: \emph{alot} and seventeen contractions written without their apostrophe.}

\newcommand{\dialParagraphs}{A tone check tested whether the measures can steer a revision. The model that wrote each of \rvN{} plain machine essays revised it under four instructions: a control instruction, to make it read as a student of that grade wrote it; the control instruction plus a report naming each measure on which the draft lay outside the middle 80\% of student essays on other assignments; and the control instruction plus one of two published prose skills written to remove the marks of machine writing (Methods). The planned outcome was a text's mean squared standardised distance from the centre of student essays. It measures how close a text is to the average student essay, and real student essays are not close to it: they average \rvHuman{}, because they scatter around their own centre, while drafts average \rvDraft, control revisions \rvControl, revisions made with a skill \rvHumanizer{} and \rvSepia, and revisions that saw the report \rvReport{} (\figref{fig:machine}c). A smaller distance means a more average essay. On the planned test the report is not distinguishable from the control (difference \dRvRC{} \dRvRCCIjack), and neither are the skills (\dRvHC{} \dRvHCCIjack{} and \dRvSC{} \dRvSCCIjack). On the secondary outcome, the mean machine score of a classifier trained on other essays, the drafts are at \rvScoreDraft, the four kinds of revision between \rvScoreLo{} and \rvScoreHi, and the students at \rvScoreHuman{} (\figref{fig:machine}d). Every kind of revision moved a little toward the students, and none came close. The report did move the measures it named: it cut the mean ending lift from \rvLiftDraft{} to \rvLiftReport{} standard deviations, against \rvLiftControl{} for the control, and left intensifier use where it was (\rvBoostDraft{} to \rvBoostReport), because a draft low on intensifiers is still inside the wide human range and was not flagged.

A report of ranges acts only on drafts outside a wide band, and if it worked perfectly it would pull every draft to one centre. In a second experiment, the dial, each of \dlRequested{} further drafts was assigned the measured profile of one real student essay, drawn at random from other assignments, and was revised twice by its author model with a sheet giving the current and the target value of each measure; a control arm revised the same drafts twice with the plain instruction. The smallest of the four models declined \dcDialFirst{} of its \hkDialFirst{} first-round dial requests and \dcDialSecond{} of the \hkDialSecond{} that reached a second round, so only \hkPairs{} of its drafts are among the \dlN{} essays with both arms complete (\dlPairsOpus{} by Opus, \dlPairsFable{} by Fable and \dlPairsSonnet{} by Sonnet), and the result rests mainly on three models. The models followed the dial part of the way. Achieved values correlate with assigned targets at a mean of \dlCorrDial{} over the ten measures in the dial arm and \dlCorrControl{} in the control arm (difference \dDlCorr{} \dDlCorrCIjack; \figref{fig:machine}e), at \dlCorrModelLo{} to \dlCorrModelHi{} for each of the three models with 14 or more essays. By rank correlation the means are \dlRhoDial{} and \dlRhoControl, from \dlRhoMax{} for dolor density, the best followed, to \dlRhoMin{} for sentence clustering. The mean squared gap to the target fell from \dlGapDraft{} to \dlGapDialOne{} after one round and \dlGapDial{} after two, about half way, while the control arm stayed at \dlGapControl.

No gain in resemblance to student writing over a plain revision was detected. The classifier's mean machine score is \dlScoreDraft{} for the drafts and \dlScoreHuman{} for the matched student essays; two plain revisions bring it to \dlScoreControl{} and two rounds with the dial to \dlScoreDial{} (difference \dDlScore{} \dDlScoreCIjack, undecided). The test had little room to show a gain. After two plain revisions the classifier could barely tell the essays from the matched students' (area under the curve \dlAucControl, against \dlAucDraft{} for the drafts and \dlAucDial{} after the dial), so on this measure the plain instruction to write as a student had already done what the dial was meant to do.

A model judge read both revisions of each draft in random order without being told which was which, though it was not blind to the study (Methods). It chose the dial revision as the better written in \dlJudgeWins{} of \dlJudgeN{} pairs (exact 95\% interval \dlJudgeExact\%). That passes the planned test, that the dial revision not be chosen less than half the time, a test lenient enough that passing it says little. The preference came from two of the writers: \dlJudgeOpus{} pairs for drafts by Opus and \dlJudgeFable{} for Fable, against \dlJudgeSonnet{} for Sonnet and \dlJudgeHaiku{} for Haiku. Asked a second question that the plan did not list, which revision reads more like a student's own work, the judge chose the dial revision in \dlJudgeStudentWins{} of \dlJudgeN{} pairs (\dlJudgeStudentExact\%), and never on both questions. That answer is confounded with spelling: the plain revisions wrote informal spellings such as \emph{alot} and \emph{dont}, \dlSlipsControl{} per thousand words against \dlSlipsHuman{} in the students' own essays, and the dial revisions wrote none. Extended Data Table~\ref{tab:excerpt} shows the essay at the median improvement, chosen by rule. To raise its positive charge the dial revision closes on \emph{a fun, wonderful summer}, and its ending lift moved away from the target.

The measures therefore give a model a partial control over how its text carries feeling, as far as a target can be reached by word choice in two passes. That is narrower than writing as a person does, and a plain instruction to write as a student did as much by the classifier.

The models sometimes refused these requests. Of \dcTotal{} requests that left no text, \dcAuthorship{} were refusals by the smallest model: \rvDeclined{} in the tone check and \dlMissing{} in the dial arm, \dcDialFirst{} of them in its first round. The refusals said that tuning machine text to pass as a student's work, or to move a scoring tool's numbers, misrepresents authorship. The same model followed the plain instruction to write as a student in \hkControlDone{} of its \hkControlAll{} plain requests (all \hkControlDial{} in the dial and \hkToneControlDone{} of \hkToneControl{} in the tone check), and declined \hkReportDeclined{} of its \hkReport{} revisions with the report and \dlMissingHaiku{} of its \hkDialRequests{} dial requests, so it was mainly the steering sheets that it declined. The remaining requests failed for other reasons (Methods). The tools released with this paper report tone against a human range, for writers who disclose their use of a model. On these measures the plain instruction to write as a student did the most to make machine essays hard to tell from students', and the measures are too weak to serve as a detector.}
