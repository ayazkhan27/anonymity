Phones Belong in the Glovebox

Almost everyone I know carries a phone everywhere, and a lot of us feel nervous and unhappy when it is out of reach. That habit comes with us into the car. People think a quick look at a text or a short call is harmless, but it is a dangerous mistake. Driving is different. It is the one thing we do every day where a few seconds of distraction can kill someone. That is why I believe drivers should not use cell phones while driving, except for emergencies and a map that is set up before the trip starts.

The biggest problem is attention. When you drive, your brain has to keep track of speed, distance, other cars, people walking, signals, and the road all at once. Reading a text takes your eyes off the road for several seconds. On the highway, a car goes about the length of a football field in five seconds. A driver looking down that long is basically driving blind. A kid stepping off a curb or a car braking in front of you will not wait for you to finish typing.

Some people say hands-free calls fix this. Having both hands on the wheel is good, and it helps a little. The real issue is your mind, though. Studies show that people talking on the phone react slower and miss things right in front of them, even while looking ahead. Talking to a passenger is safer, because a passenger can see the road and usually goes quiet when traffic gets scary. The person on the phone has no clue what you are dealing with, and that is not safe.

Others say a rule like this would be hard to enforce, or that adults should be trusted to know their limits. That sounds fair, but trust does not protect a family hit by a careless driver. We already limit freedom on the road when one person's choices put other people in danger. Drunk driving laws and speed limits exist for the same reason. Most drivers who text think they are the exception, and that confidence is exactly what makes it dangerous.

There are fair exceptions. Calling 911 to report a crash or a fire should always be allowed. A map on a mounted phone, set before the car moves, is fine and no worse than a dashboard screen. Other than that, the phone should stay quiet and in the glovebox. When we put it away, we stay safe and protect the people we love. Getting home in one piece is the best reward, and it is worth more than any text.
