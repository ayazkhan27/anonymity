I agree with the principal that every student should join at least one extracurricular activity. School is about more than passing tests, and the time after the last bell is when students learn things a classroom can teach only a little.

First, activities help students build skills. A student on the yearbook has to meet deadlines, take photos, and work with a team of editors. If one person is late, the whole book is late, and that is a problem. A student in the band learns to show up on time and practice a part until it fits with the others. Student council is another example. They plan the fall dance, set a budget, and argue about whether to spend money on a DJ or on decorations. Adults use these skills at work every day, so it makes sense to practice early.

Second, activities give students a place to belong. Lots of kids feel lost at a big school, especially in ninth grade. When I was new, I knew nobody, and I sat alone at lunch for weeks. Then a friend talked me into joining the cross-country team. Within a month I had friends to sit with, and I liked going to practice. Students who feel connected at school come more often and care more about their grades. A required activity gives every student that chance, even the shy ones who would stay home instead of signing up.

Some people say a requirement is unfair because certain students have jobs or have to watch their little brothers and sisters. That is a fair worry, but we can handle it without losing the rule. The school can offer clubs during lunch or short sessions, and it can count community service done outside school. Students could pick a club by interest, like chess, art, or robotics. The rule should be flexible, but it should stay. Without it, the students who need friends the most are the ones who stay away.

Others say students have too much homework already. However, I think busy students often manage their time better. A full schedule forces a person to plan. Many athletes and band members finish homework faster because practice is coming. I have seen this on my own team, where the fastest runners are often on the honor roll.

In the end, one required activity is a small ask. It pushes students to try new things, meet people, and practice teamwork. For these reasons, I support the principal's decision, even though some students will complain at first, and the school will be a different place with it.
