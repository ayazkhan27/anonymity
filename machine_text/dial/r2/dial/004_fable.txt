Dear Principal,

I heard that you are thinking about changing the cell phone policy. I am writing to ask you to pick Policy 1. Students could bring phones and use them at lunch and during free time, and the phones would stay off in class.

My first reason is about getting home. A lot of us do not go straight home after school. I have practice until five on some days, and my mom works a shift that changes every week. If practice gets cancelled or my ride does not show up, I have to tell her somehow. The office has a phone, but it is one line for a few hundred kids. Parents also call us to say when plans change. A total ban would not make that go away. It would be unsafe and scary for kids who walk or wait outside alone, and it would not be fair to worried parents.

My second reason is that Policy 2 would be hard to enforce. Phones are small. Kids who want them will keep them in a pocket or a locker. Teachers would have to search and take phones and argue, and that is a waste of a class period. Most kids would break the rule anyway, and then the rule is just a joke. Policy 1 asks for less, and teachers would only have to deal with the few who do not listen.

Third, Policy 1 still covers class time. The problem with phones is that they distract people during lessons, and both policies deal with that. Under Policy 1 a phone that is seen or heard in class can be taken until the end of the day. Kids would not want to lose it, so they would keep it put away.

Fourth, we are going to have to learn this at some point. At a job or in college nobody is going to lock up our phones for us. We will have to turn them off in a meeting and check them on a break. I do not think a ban teaches that. It just hides the thing for a few years.

I know you want students to pay attention in class, and I want that too. Policy 2 sounds strict, but Policy 1 is the better and fairer choice. It gives you quiet classrooms, and it lets families trust that they can reach us when they need to. I hope you will choose it. Thank you for reading my letter.

Sincerely,
A student
