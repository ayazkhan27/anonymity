When people have a hard choice to make, they usually go to one person they trust for advice. That is fine, but one person can only see things from one side. I think asking a bunch of different people is a good idea, because everyone has different experiences and ways of thinking, but together they help you make a better and happier choice.

First, people know different things. Say a high school senior is picking a college. Her parents might care about cost and how far away it is, but they might not know much about the classes. A teacher might know which programs are great for the subject she loves. An older friend at one of the schools could tell her what the dorms are like, but also the fun and exciting parts of college. Nobody has the whole picture, but together they give her a lot of helpful and useful information. Each talk fills in something the others missed, and she feels proud and hopeful about her choice.

Second, asking more than one person helps you notice when advice is one-sided. Everyone's advice comes from their own life. A man who lost money on a restaurant might tell everyone not to start a business, but a friend who did great might make it sound easy and fun. If you only listened to one of them, you would get a weird idea of what it's like. But if you hear both, you can see that success depends on planning, location, and luck, and you can make a smart and fair decision.

Third, when people agree, that means something, but when they disagree, that is useful too. If three doctors say the same treatment is best, a patient can feel confident and safe about it. If the doctors say different things, that isn't a disaster, but it tells the patient to ask more questions and learn more. Either way, he gains something good and helpful that he would have missed with only one doctor.

Last, explaining your problem to different people helps you think. Every time you tell your story, you have to put your thoughts in order. Sometimes you figure out what you want just by talking, but you can also notice which advice makes you feel relieved and calm and which makes you nervous.

Of course, asking for opinions doesn't mean other people get to decide for you. The choice is still yours, and you are the one who has to live with it, but you will not be alone. A choice made after hearing lots of kind and caring voices is usually smarter, fairer, and more confident than one made after hearing just one, and that makes it worth it.
