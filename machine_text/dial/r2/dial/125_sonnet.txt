Why Several Opinions Are Better Than One

When I have a big decision to make, I usually want to ask my favorite person and just do what they say. It is quick and simple. My teacher says people should ask around first, and I think she has a point. One adviser can help, but I believe that asking several people gives you a stronger choice.

My first reason is that everybody sees things in their own way. An adviser only knows their own life. Say a student is picking between two colleges. Her mom went to a big state school, so she says big campuses are great. Her cousin went to a small school, so he says small ones are best. Neither one is lying, they just have different stories. When the student hears both, she can see the real question, which is what place fits her. With only one opinion she would not have asked it.

My second reason is that different people know different things. Think about a guy buying a used car. His friend drives a lot, so he talks about how the car feels on the road. A coworker who fixes cars checks the repair history and finds an issue with the transmission. An aunt who works at a bank sees that the insurance costs a lot. Each person gives one piece of the picture. Together they make a full answer, and the buyer does not end up with a car he can't afford.

Another reason is that you can see how much to trust the advice. If five people say a job offer sounds too good to be true, that is a sign to listen. If they split, then the choice is close, but your own values can pick the winner. That is not a failure, it is a clue about the tradeoffs. For example, my brother asked three friends about joining the band, and they all said to try it, so he signed up. It also helps to know that more than one person agrees with you.

Talking to many people is also practice for explaining yourself. Every time you tell your story you notice what matters most, and some worries fade away. Last year a friend asked a question I never thought of, and I understood my own plan better. I did not expect that from a person I talk to every day.

Of course, there are limits. If you ask too many people you can get tired, and you might keep asking until someone says what you wanted to hear. However, the answer is simple. Pick a few people with different lives, listen with an open mind, and then make the choice yourself. In the end, you are the one who lives with it.

So I would pick three honest voices over one sure voice any day. I know a lot of kids who just copy their best friend, and they never ask anyone else. It takes time, and sometimes the talking is slow, but a few extra talks are worth it when the choice is big. My plan is to ask my mom, my teacher, and one friend next time. I think that is a fair way to decide, and I feel ready to try it myself.
