Put the Phone Down While Driving

I really think drivers should put their cell phones away while they drive. Phones are great and I love mine, so I get why people want to keep them close. A car is a big machine though, and staying safe is the best thing a driver can do. A good driver keeps both eyes and a clear mind on the road, and that is a very smart way to keep everybody safe and happy.

First, driving takes a lot of focus. A good driver watches the road, checks the mirrors, and keeps track of the other cars and the people walking nearby. It is really hard to do all of that and read a text too. Smart people can do many nice things, but the brain is so busy with two big jobs at once. When you look at your phone for five seconds on the highway, you travel the length of a football field. That is a lot of road to cover with your eyes somewhere else, and nobody has that much good luck every day.

Some people say hands-free calling is a great fix, and that sounds nice. However, the mind is the part that matters most. Studies show that drivers who talk hands-free still miss easy things like a red light or a stopped car. A fun, long talk with a friend is a lovely thing, but it pulls your attention off the road. If the law says hands-free is fine, people will think it is totally safe and talk for a long time. That is a risky plan, and it is really unfair to the other drivers who want to be careful.

Other people say we need phones for maps and for help. I understand that, and those are good points. However, there is a very easy answer. You can set up your map before you start the trip, and if you need help you can pull over in a safe spot and call. Parking is a simple, safe, and smart choice. Almost everything can wait a few minutes, and the people you love will be glad you did. I would much rather get there a little late and happy than get a perfect route and a sad surprise.

A clear rule is also better and easier for the police. If some phone uses are okay and some are wrong, an officer cannot tell if you were dialing, texting, or looking at a map. A rule for everything is simple and fair, and everybody knows what to do. Seat belts are a great example. Long ago many people skipped them, and now almost everyone buckles up with a smile. A strong rule with good education can help people build better habits, and I think that would be a really great thing for the whole country.

Also, this is about more than just the driver. Passengers, kids walking to school, bike riders, and workers by the road all trust drivers to take care. They have a quiet hope that the person in the next lane is paying attention, and that is a very kind thing to give them. When one person picks the road over the phone, someone else's family can stay safe and happy.

In conclusion, phones are very useful and I enjoy them, so I am not saying they are evil. I am saying the car is the right place to be a careful, kind, and smart person. Wait until you are parked, because every text can be answered later. A safe trip is a wonderful gift, and that is a great reward for one little pause. I really love that idea.
