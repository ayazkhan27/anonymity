When my cousin broke her leg in tenth grade, she missed almost five weeks of school. Her teachers sent home packets of worksheets, and she tried her best with them. Nobody was there to explain the chemistry, though, so she ended up taking the class again over the summer. If her school had let her join classes by video, she could have kept up with her friends. Her story is the main reason I believe students would benefit from having the option to attend classes from home.

The key word is option. Most students like being in the building, and I am not against that at all. Some kids are healing from a long illness or injury. Others live an hour's bus ride from the nearest high school, and they could use those two hours to sleep or play a sport they love. Some feel calm at home in a way they never feel in a crowded cafeteria. For these students, learning at home is a way to keep learning at all.

Distance learning also lets students work in a way that fits them. Online lessons are often recorded, so a student who did not understand a math problem can watch the explanation again. Small schools could even offer classes they don't have now, like a third year of a foreign language, by connecting students to a teacher somewhere else. I think that is a really smart use of technology.

People who oppose this idea usually say that students at home will get distracted, and they have a point. It is not easy to pay attention when your bed and your phone are three feet away. Friends are also easier to make in person, where you can laugh together at lunch. Those worries are real, but schools can handle them with a few clear rules. They can require attendance checks, cameras on during discussions, and a solid grade to stay in the program. A student whose grades slip can be called back into the building, but one who does well should be trusted to keep going.

School is supposed to be about learning, whatever chair you sit in. Right now, a student who gets hurt for a month can lose a whole semester, the way my cousin did. That does not have to happen. However, with an option to attend from home, a student like her could keep up, stay close to her friends, and enjoy a normal summer. I think that is a very good reason to give students the choice.
