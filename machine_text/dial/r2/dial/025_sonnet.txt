Dear Principal,

I am writing about the idea that every student must do community service. I do not agree with making it a rule. I think the school should set up ways for students to volunteer if they choose to.

Some of my friends tutor kids at the elementary school on Tuesdays. Other students spend a Saturday picking up trash at the park. I see them at school on Monday and they talk about what they did that weekend. Those students do something for the town, and people notice it.

A requirement changes things. Many of my classmates already have homework, sports, jobs, and younger brothers or sisters to watch after school. One girl I know works nights at a grocery store so her family can pay rent. If the school gives her a number of hours to finish, that is one more item on her list. When a student volunteers just to check a box, they usually do the minimum. The people at the site can tell when a student is there only for the hours.

There is also the planning. Tutoring and park cleanups need adults to organize them. The adults who run these groups already have a lot to manage. If hundreds of students need a place to volunteer at the same time, the school and the local groups might run out of spots. Some students would end up doing busywork that serves no purpose.

My plan is to encourage service instead of ordering it. The school could hold a volunteer fair where local groups set up tables and students sign up. Teachers could announce the names of volunteers at assemblies. Service done at school events could count too, so students do not need to find extra time. A student who picks an activity on their own will probably keep doing it after graduation.

I am asking the school to consider this plan. It would give students a choice and still send more of them to volunteer sites.

Sincerely,
A Concerned Student
