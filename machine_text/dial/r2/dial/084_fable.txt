One Activity Is Not Too Much to Ask

When I first heard that our principal wants every student to join at least one activity after school, I groaned. I already have homework, chores, and a little brother I have to watch on Thursdays. To me it sounded like a terrible, unfair, annoying rule. Then I thought about it for a while and changed my mind. I agree with the principal, as long as the rule has some room in it.

The biggest reason is that kids find out what they like when somebody makes them try it. Last year my friend Marcus signed up for yearbook because his mom made him. He was angry, he complained and whined for two weeks, and he called it boring, stupid, and a waste. By October he was staying late to fix the photo pages. Now he says he wants to study graphic design. Without his mom he would have gone home at 3:00 and played video games.

Activities also teach you things that classes leave out. In math, if I mess up, it only goes on my grade. On the soccer team, if I skip practice, ten other people have to do my part. Kids on student council have to listen to angry complaints about the awful, gross lunch menu. They still have to plan something with a budget. Adults do that every day. A worksheet is only a piece of paper.

There is a reason with friends too. Our school has about nine hundred students. You can go three years and only know the people in your classes. That is lonely, sad, and kind of scary. A club mixes the grades up, and it is fun, friendly, and a great way to laugh with new people. A sixth grader in chess club sits across from an eighth grader. After that the hallway is a different place.

I know what people will say against this. Some kids have jobs, long bus rides, or have to help at home, and some are shy. So the school should have clubs that meet at lunch or in advisory. It should count small things, like helping in the library once a week. A student who has a serious problem at home should be able to talk to a counselor and get out of it.

With those changes, one activity is an hour or two a week. Most of us will stay home unless somebody pushes us. We are lazy, scared of looking dumb, and worried that everybody will ignore us. I was afraid of that in sixth grade. I sat in my room every afternoon that whole year. The principal is right, and I am glad about the rule. I only wish I had been pushed when I was eleven, because I think I would have loved having a team and good friends.
