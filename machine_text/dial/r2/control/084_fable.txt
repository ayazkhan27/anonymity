One Activity Is Not Too Much

When I first heard our principal wants every student to join at least one extracurricular activity, I was kind of mad. I already have homework and chores and on Thursdays I have to watch my little brother. But then I thought about it some more and I changed my mind. I agree with the principal, but only if the rule is flexible for kids who need it to be.

My first reason is that alot of kids don't know what they like until somebody makes them try it. Last year my friend Marcus joined yearbook only because his mom made him. He complained about it for like two weeks straight. Then by October he was staying after school to fix the photo pages and now he says he wants to do graphic design when he grows up. He never would of found that out if he just went home at 3:00 and played video games. Some kids are quiet or not sure of themselfs, so a rule like this gives them a push.

Another reason is activities teach you stuff that classes don't. If I mess up in math it only hurts my grade. But if I skip soccer practice the whole team has to deal with it, and my coach makes sure I know it. Also the kids on student council have to listen to everybody complain about the lunch food and then they have to actually plan things with real money. Adults have to do things like that all the time and you can't learn it from a worksheet.

It also helps you meet people. Our school has around nine hundred kids, and you can go all three years only knowing the people in your classes. In a club there are kids from different grades and different friend groups. A sixth grader in chess club might play against an eighth grader, and then the hallway isn't so scary anymore. I think when kids feel like they belong somewhere they come to school more and they get in less trouble too.

I know some people are going to disagree with me. Some students have jobs or a really long bus ride, or they have to help out their family, and some kids are just shy. Those are good points. That is why the rule shouldn't be too strict. The school should have clubs that meet during lunch or advisory so nobody has to miss the bus. Small things should count too, like helping in the library once a week. And if a student really can't do anything at all, they should be able to go talk to a counselor and get excused.

In conclusion, one activity is not that much to ask for. It is only an hour or two a week and you could get a new skill or a new friend out of it. Most of us would not sign up on our own, so I think the principal is right.
