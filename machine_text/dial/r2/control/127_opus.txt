Last year my older cousin got a job offer in a different state and she didnt know if she should take it or not. She didn't just ask one person though. She called her parents, she talked to one of her old teachers from college, and she texted her friend who moved across the country like two years ago. Everybody told her something different. But by the end she understood her situation a lot better than when she started. I think this shows why asking more than one person for advice helps you make a better choice. When you ask more people you get more information, you don't get stuck with one person's bias, and you figure out what you actually want.

My first reason is that nobody knows everything. Everybody only has their own life to go off of. My cousins parents know her really well and they know about her money and stuff, but they never lived far away from home. Her professor knew about her job field and he could tell her if the company was good or not. Her friend could tell her what its really like to move to a new city where you dont know anybody. She said the first few months were really lonely but then she made new friends. None of them could of given my cousin the whole picture by themselves. But all together they covered money, her career, and what her normal life would be like. Its kind of like a puzzle. Each person gives you a piece, and the pieces one person doesn't have, somebody else fills in.

My second reason is that everybody has their own bias, even when they are trying to help you. Parents usually want their kids to stay close so they might only talk about the bad parts of moving. A friend who loves adventure might just tell you to go for it and not think about what could go wrong. It's like my soccer coach, he always tells kids to care about sports more than anything else because sports is what he cares about. These people aren't trying to trick you or anything. It's just that what they want and what they are scared of changes what they say to you. If you only listen to one person you might end up making a choice that fits what they want and not what you want. But if you hear a couple different opinions you can see where people disagree and ask them why. That helps you figure out which advice is based on facts and which advice is just feelings.

My last reason is that hearing different opinions helps you find out what is important to you. Sometimes you don't even know what you care about until someone says something you don't agree with. When my cousin's dad told her the job wasn't worth leaving her family for, she really wanted to argue with him. Thats how she knew she really wanted the job. Then when her friend warned her about being lonely, she knew she needed a plan to keep in touch, so she set up video calls with her family every week before she even signed anything. Every conversation kind of showed her something about herself that she didn't really think about before. In the end she took the job, but she had a plan and she knew what she was doing. She didn't just do it out of nowhere.

Some people might say that asking alot of people just makes you more confused. And thats true if you ask like twenty people and they all say different stuff and you don't think about any of it. But the point isn't to count votes and do whatever most people tell you. The point is to get information, think about it, and then make your own choice. Usually asking a few people who think different is enough. That way you get the good parts and you don't get totally confused.

In conclusion, asking more than one person for advice helps people make better choices because you get more information, you balance out one persons bias, and you learn what you care about. Big choices like picking a college, getting a new job, or moving somewhere new should get more than one opinion. Just like my cousin did, anybody with a hard choice can listen to a bunch of people and then decide for themself.
