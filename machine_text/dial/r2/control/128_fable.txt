Last spring my cousin Danielle had a big decision to make. She could take a job at a hospital two states away, or she could stay at the clinic where she worked for four years. The first person she asked was her mom and her mom said to stay. If Danielle stopped there she would of had her answer, but I don't think it would of been a good one. She asked three more people after that and her decision came out a lot better. I think asking a few people for advice is better then asking just one person.

My first reason is that one person only knows part of the story. Danielle's mom was thinking about how far away the new city was and how lonely Danielle could get there. Her boss at the clinic told her something different. He said there was no way for her to move up at the clinic and that the hospital would teach her things the clinic never could. He didn't even have a reason to want her to leave, he would have to find somebody new, so I think that says a lot. Then she asked a friend who moved for a job a few years ago. Her friend told her about stuff she never thought about, like paying a security deposit and how car insurance costs more there and how much it costs to fly home for the holidays. None of them were wrong. They each knew a different part, and Danielle needed all of the parts before she could see the whole thing.

My second reason is that people put their own feelings in their advice. A lot of the time they don't even know they are doing it. Danielle's mom really did think staying was the smart thing to do. But she also didn't want her daughter living six hours away from her. If you only ask one person, you can't tell what part is facts and what part is just what that person wants. When you ask a few people you can tell. Danielle noticed that her mom was the only one who said being lonely was the biggest problem. Being lonely still mattered, but now she could think about it in a fair way and not like it was the only thing.

My third reason is that when you compare what people say, you find out what you really have to decide. If everybody says the same thing then it is probably right. All four people said the hospital was better for her career, so Danielle didn't have to worry about that part anymore. The part they didn't agree on was if moving was worth it for the rest of her life, like her family and friends. So that was the part she had to figure out by herself. It is kind of like getting a second opinion from a doctor. If two doctors both say you need the same surgery, you feel better about getting it. If they say different things, then you know you should ask more questions first.

Some people say that asking too many people will just confuse you. I think that can be true sometimes. There are people who keep asking and asking so they never have to pick. But that doesn't mean you should only ask one person. It means you should ask a normal amount of people, and they should be people who know something about it. Also getting advice is not the same thing as doing what everybody tells you. Danielle didn't count up votes. She listened to all of them and then she picked for herself. That made it her own decision. It wouldn't of been her own if she just did what her mom said.

In the end Danielle took the hospital job. She asked if she could start later so she could save up money for the move, and that was her friend's idea. She has been there eight months now and she just started the training her boss told her about. One conversation could not of gotten her to that plan. It took four of them. That is why I think you should always ask more than one person when you have a big choice to make.
