Drivers Should Put the Phone Down

Almost everyone has a cell phone now, and a lot of people feel like they have to have it with them all the time, even in the car. I think that is a problem. A car is a big heavy machine that goes really fast, and one small mistake can change somebody's life. I believe drivers should not be allowed to use their phones at all while driving, because looking away for just a second can kill someone, and no text is worth that.

The biggest reason is how the brain works. Driving takes a lot of attention. You have to watch the road, check the mirrors, keep track of how fast the other cars are going, and watch out for people walking or riding bikes. Studies show that people are not good at doing two hard things at once. When people think they are multitasking they are really just switching back and forth, and each time they switch there is a moment where they are not paying attention to the road. At highway speed, the time it takes to read one text is about the length of a football field. A lot can go wrong in that much space. I have seen this myself. My aunt looked down at her phone for a second while I was in the car and she almost ran into the back of a truck that was stopping. She said sorry, but we were both really scared.

Some people say hands-free calling is a safe middle ground. I get why they think so, because both hands are still on the wheel. But the problem is not only your hands, it is your mind. Studies of drivers using hands-free systems found they still react slower and miss things right in front of them, like a stopped car or a red light. Talking to someone pulls your brain into the conversation even when your eyes are pointed at the road. Also, if the law says hands-free is okay, people will think it is safe, and then they will talk on the phone for the whole drive.

Another argument is that a full ban is not realistic since people need their phones for directions and emergencies. These are fair points, but there are easy answers. You can type in the directions before you start driving, and if you get lost you can pull over and check the map. If there is a real emergency, like a crash or somebody getting sick, the driver can stop in a safe place and call for help. The rule is about using the phone while the car is moving, so a car parked on the side of the road is different. Most phones also have a driving mode that can turn off notifications, so there is even less of an excuse. Almost everything people use their phone for can wait a few minutes.

A simple rule is also easier to understand and to enforce than a complicated one. If the law allows some things but not others, a police officer can not really tell if the driver was dialing a number, reading a text, or looking at a map. The driver could just say it was the allowed one. A complete ban takes care of that. In my opinion the law should be clear so nobody has to guess what is allowed. It can also change how people act. Think about seat belt laws and the ads against drunk driving. A strong rule with education behind it can change what people think is normal. Not that long ago lots of people did not wear seat belts, and now most people would feel weird driving without one.

Finally, this is not just about the driver. A distracted driver puts passengers in danger, plus other drivers, kids walking to school, and workers on the side of the road. Those people never chose to take that risk. Using a phone is a choice that one person makes, but someone else might be the one who pays for it.

Cell phones are really useful, but the road is not the place for them. Drivers should wait until the car is stopped, because every message can be answered later, but a life that is lost can never be brought back. I know it is hard to ignore a phone when it buzzes, but it is a lot harder to live with hurting someone.
