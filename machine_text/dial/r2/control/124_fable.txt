Last year my cousin wanted to buy a used car from his neighbor. Before he bought it he asked three people what they thought. His dad said the price was fair. His friend who works at a repair shop told him to look under the hood first. His older sister asked him if he could still pay for insurance after. None of them gave him the whole answer, but when you put all three together he pretty much had it. That is why I think asking more than one person for advice helps you make a better choice. Each person only sees a part of the problem.

First, people give advice from what they have been through, and nobody has been through everything. My cousin's dad has bought alot of cars so he knows about prices. He doesn't know much about engines though. The friend knows engines, but he had no idea how much money my cousin makes at his job. If my cousin stopped after talking to his dad he would of bought the car that same day. Because he kept asking, he found out the timing belt needed to be replaced. Then he used that to get the price down four hundred dollars. So having only one opinion would have cost him money.

Another reason is that people are biased, and you can't really tell when you only ask one person. Lets say a girl is picking between two high schools and she only asks her best friend. Her friend is probably going to say the school that she goes to, because she likes it there and she wants her friend to be with her. The friend isn't lying or anything. Her advice just leans one way. But if the girl also talks to a teacher, her mom, and a kid who goes to the other school, then she can see how much her friend was leaning. She can tell which parts are really about the school and which parts are just about the person talking.

It also helps when everybody agrees. If I ask four people if I should quit the soccer team so I can work on my grades, and all four say yes but for different reasons, I'm going to feel alot more sure then if only one person said it. Doctors do this too. When somebody gets told they need surgery they usually go get a second opinion. If the second doctor says the same thing and he didn't even know what the first one said, the patient feels better about doing it. And if the doctors don't agree, that is good to know too. It means the question is harder than it looked and you should think about it more.

Some people say that asking too many people just makes you confused. I get that, and it can happen, mostly when you are hoping somebody else will just decide for you. But I don't think the answer is to ask less people. You just have to remember that advice is only information and you are the one who has to live with what you pick. My cousin didn't do exactly what any one person told him. He checked the engine, he got the price lowered, he added up the insurance, and then he bought the car. He still has it and it runs fine.

In conclusion, asking a bunch of people does take longer than asking one. Sometimes they tell you stuff you don't want to hear. But when you decide after hearing from different people, you know more and you miss less, and you have a better idea of how sure you should be. To me that is worth having a few more conversations.
