Asking more than one person for advice is a good way to make a better decision. I think this because nobody can see a problem from every side, and one person might miss something important.

Say a girl is trying to pick between two colleges. Her parents might care the most about the price and how far away the school is. Her teacher might tell her that one school has a better program for the subject she likes. Her cousin goes to one of the schools, so he can tell her what it is really like, like the dorms being crowded and how cold it gets in winter.

Another reason is that people are biased. Everyone giving advice has their own life and their own wants, and that changes what they say. A mom might want her kid to stay close to home, and a friend might want someone to hang out with at the same school. But if a bunch of different people who don't know each other all say the same thing, it is easier to trust them. If they disagree, then she knows the choice is hard, and she can take her time and think about what matters most to her.

Disagreement is also helpful because it makes you explain why you think what you think. Say one person tells you to take the safe job and another person says to take the risk. Now you have to figure out which worries are real for you. Doing that can show you what you really care about.

The choice is still yours, and too many opinions can get confusing. But a few good ones can help you pick.
