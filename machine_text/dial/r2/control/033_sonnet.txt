Dear Principal,

I am writing about whether our school should make every student do community service. I think it should not be required, but the school should encourage it and make it easy to do.

Some of my friends volunteer and they are proud of it. One helps third graders with reading after school, and she says their faces light up when a hard word finally makes sense. Another goes to a creek cleanup near our neighborhood on weekends. They both feel that helping others matters, and I agree. Service teaches you to be responsible and patient, and it shows you that you are part of something bigger than yourself.

However, a requirement would cause problems. Many students already have very full schedules. Some have part-time jobs to help their families pay bills. Others take care of their younger brothers and sisters or play on teams that practice every day. For them, a service quota would be one more thing on time they barely have. My friend works at a grocery store four nights a week. If you told him to find extra hours for service, it would stress him out and it could hurt his grades.

Another problem is the reason people do it. When something is forced, a lot of people do the bare minimum and watch the clock. A student who has to sign a log sheet might start to hate volunteering instead of liking it. Service that comes from your own choice usually means more, both to the student and to the people who get helped.

Instead of a requirement, I think the school should create more chances for students to help. We could have a volunteer fair where local groups like food banks, libraries, and animal shelters tell us what they need. We could also have service projects during lunch or on school grounds so busy students can join without traveling far. Another idea is to give volunteers certificates or say their names at assemblies. If students see their classmates having fun with service and getting honored for it, more of them will want to join too.

I believe this plan will get more students involved on their own, and with better attitudes, than a rule ever could. Thank you for reading my opinion.

Sincerely,
A Concerned Student
