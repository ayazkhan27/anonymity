Last summer I had to choose between two summer programs. At first I just wanted to ask my mom and do whatever she said. She told me the robotics camp was the safe choice because it would look good on my college applications. But then I talked to my cousin, who went to a camp like it, and he said it was mostly lectures. Then a girl in my class told me the art workshop was small and everybody got to make stuff. By the end of the week I had three different opinions, and I think I made a way better choice than if I only asked one person.

The first reason asking more than one person helps is that everybody only knows part of the story. My mom knew what colleges like, but she never went to either program. My cousin did go to a camp, but he is not the same kind of student as me. Each person knew something the others didn't, so when I put it all together I got a pretty good idea of what each camp was really like.

Another reason is that people have biases and they don't always notice them. A parent might want the safest thing, and a friend might want you to do the same thing they are doing. Someone selling something just wants you to buy it. When people disagree, you can see what they are leaning toward. If three people who don't even know each other all warn you about the same problem, it is probably a real problem. If only one person says it, you can think about it more before you believe it.

Also, hearing what other people think makes you think about your own reasons. When my cousin said the robotics camp was mostly lectures, I had to ask myself what I actually wanted to do with my summer. I figured out that I wanted to build things with my hands and not just sit there listening to somebody talk. If I hadn't had that conversation, I might never have figured that out.

Of course, more opinions is not always better. If you ask too many people it can get confusing, and in the end it is your decision because you are the one who has to live with it. Still, if you ask a few people you trust, especially people who have had different experiences, you get more information and you can spot bias. That is why I would always ask a few people instead of just one, even if the first answer sounds really good.
