Phones and Driving

Almost everyone I know has a phone, and almost everyone I know has looked at it while driving. I have done it too, I'm not going to lie. It doesn't feel like a big deal because most of the time nothing happens. You look down for a second and when you look back up the road is still there. But I think that's why phones are so dangerous in a car. People get used to nothing going wrong so they keep doing it. I think drivers should not be allowed to use there phone while the car is moving, except for emergencies and for directions that you set up before you leave.

The main reason is that driving takes way more focus than people think it does. You need your eyes on the road, your hands on the wheel, and your brain paying attention. Texting takes away all three at once. In drivers ed they told us that if you are on the highway and you look at a text for five seconds, you go about the length of a football field without looking. Thats basically driving with your eyes closed. A little kid could run out in the street, or the car in front of you could slam on the brakes, and you wouldn't even see it because you were typing "on my way." Thousands of people die every year in the US from distracted driving and a lot of that is because of phones.

Some people say the answer is hands free. If your phone is on the dashboard and you just talk instead of typing, then your hands and eyes are where they are supposed to be. I agree that this is better than texting but it doesn't fix everything. Your mind is still on the conversation and not on the road. I have heard that people who are on a call can look right at something and not really notice it because their brain is busy with the call. Talking to someone in the passenger seat is different though. A passenger can see what's going on, so if traffic gets bad they usually stop talking. The person on the phone has no idea you are trying to merge onto a busy freeway so they just keep talking.

Another reason is that it's not fair to other people. When a driver decides to use their phone they aren't the only one who could get hurt. People walking, people on bikes, the passengers, and the other cars on the road never agreed to that risk. We already think this way about drunk driving. Nobody says its okay to drive after six beers as long as you feel fine, because if you are wrong then somebody else pays for it. A person reading a text is kind of the same as a drunk driver for those couple seconds.

I don't think the rule should count for every single situation though. If you see a crash or you think someone is following you, you should be able to call 911. GPS is useful too. A voice telling you "turn left in half a mile" is probably safer then trying to read every street sign. But you should type in the address while the car is still parked, and if you need to change it you should pull over. It's the same with music. You can pick your playlist before you start driving.

Some people will say a law like this is too hard to enforce, and that is true because cops can't see inside every car. But laws aren't only about giving out tickets. My mom told me that when she was a kid a lot of people didn't wear seat belts, and now it's just normal and everybody does it. I think a law against phones could do the same thing after a while. It would show new drivers, especially teenagers like me who grew up with a phone, that the text can wait.

In conclusion, drivers should not be using their phones while the car is moving. The text or the call will still be there when you park. But if someone dies in a crash you can't take it back. So putting your phone away is really not a lot to ask, and I think it should be the law.
