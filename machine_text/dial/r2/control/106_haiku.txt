Drivers should not be allowed to use cell phones at all while they are driving. Driving takes a lot of attention, and a phone takes attention away no matter how you use it. Holding the phone in one hand and texting with the other is an obvious problem, but the bigger danger is in the mind. A lot of studies on distracted driving have shown that talking on the phone, even hands-free, pulls your attention off the road. A driver on a call might not notice the brake lights in front of them or a person stepping off the curb. These lapses might only last a few seconds, but at highway speed that is long enough to travel the length of a football field.

Some people say hands-free devices solve the problem. They get rid of the physical distraction, but they do not get rid of the mental one. The brain cannot fully focus on two hard things at the same time, so a driver listening to a message or thinking about a reply has less focus left for the road. A strict rule with no exceptions is easier to follow and easier to enforce than a bunch of allowed uses that drivers have to decide on by themselves in the moment. Police also have a hard time telling if someone was really on a call or just holding their phone.

The strongest argument against a full ban is emergencies, but emergencies can be handled without letting people use their phones whenever they want. A driver can pull over safely to call for help, and passengers can make calls or check directions for the driver. Those options keep people safe without making every driver decide in a split second whether a conversation is safe. Think about how many accidents happen every year because someone looked down for one second.

Cell phones have made life more connected and easier, but a moving car is not the place for that. Banning phone use completely while driving protects drivers, passengers, and everyone else on the road, and it does not ask much in return.
