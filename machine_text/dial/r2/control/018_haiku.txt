Dear Principal,

I think we should go with Policy 1, which lets students bring phones to school and use them at lunch and during free time as long as they stay off during class. I know a full ban seems like the easy answer but I dont think it fixes the real problem and it makes new ones.

First, phones are for more than games. A lot of kids carry their phone so they can reach their parents if a bus gets canceled or practice ends late or something happens at home. If Policy 2 passes those students wont have a good way to contact anybody during the day, and the office is going to get flooded with people asking us to pass messages along. Policy 1 keeps that connection open and doesnt get in the way of learning.

Second, the no phones in class rule already takes care of the biggest worry. If phones are off during lessons then the distraction teachers complain about most is gone. Lunch and passing periods are supposed to be a break. A kid who scrolls on their phone at lunch is not really doing worse than a kid who plays chess or cards.

Third, a total ban would be hard to enforce. Students who are used to having their phones will keep them in backpacks or hide them in their jackets or use them in the bathroom where nobody can see. Then teachers and staff spend all their time taking phones away and arguing with students instead of helping them. A simple rule students can actually follow is easier to enforce than a strict rule everybody breaks.

Finally, Policy 1 teaches responsibility. Students learn to manage their own devices and turn them off when its time to focus. We will need that skill in high school, in college, and at a job. If we ban phones completely we never get the chance to practice it, and we wont be ready for a world where phones are everywhere.

For these reasons I think Policy 1 is the better choice. It keeps students connected, protects class time, is realistic to enforce, and helps us build self control. I would be happy to help write the guidelines or talk with teachers about how it could work.

Respectfully,

A Concerned Student
