Should Drivers Be Allowed to Use Cell Phones While Driving?

Nowadays almost everybody has a cell phone and they keep it with them all the time. It is really convenient to be able to talk to anyone at any time, but it becomes a big problem when people are driving. So should drivers be allowed to use their phones at all while they drive? In my opinion, no. Drivers should not be allowed to use phones while driving, even hands-free, because the real danger is that your attention gets split, not just that your hands are busy.

Driving uses your eyes, your hands, and your brain at the same time. A car going on the highway can travel about the length of a football field in just a few seconds, so one second of not paying attention can end really badly. Many studies on distracted driving have found that talking on the phone slows down reaction time, even when the call is hands-free. Texting, scrolling, and reading messages are even worse because they take your eyes off the road completely. A lot of serious crashes happen when somebody looks down at their phone for just a few seconds.

Some people say hands-free devices fix the problem. I don't really think they do. Voice commands and earpieces get your hand off the wheel, but your mind is still stuck in the conversation. If you are dealing with something stressful on a call, you might not notice a kid stepping off the curb or the car in front of you slamming on its brakes.

A full ban would also be easier to enforce than a partial one. If the law lets people use their phones a little, police have to figure out whether someone is texting or just tapping a screen to open a map app, and drivers will push the limits to see what they can get away with. A clear rule that says no phone use while driving is simple and nobody has to guess. If there is an emergency, you can pull over safely and make the call.

Some critics might say a ban is annoying and that adults should be trusted to make their own choices. However, driving is not something you do by yourself. Every phone conversation on the road affects pedestrians, cyclists, and other drivers who never agreed to take that risk. Personal convenience should not matter more than the safety of people who aren't even on the call.

For these reasons, drivers should not be able to use cell phones in any way while they are driving. The phone can wait until the car is parked, and that small delay is a fair price to pay for safer roads.
