Dear Principal,

I think every student should have to do community service. School is supposed to prepare us for life outside of school, and service is one way to do that. You learn stuff in service that you can't learn from a worksheet or a teacher just talking at you. Right now only the kids who already volunteer get that. I think everybody should get the chance.

My friend Marcus tutors a first grader at Lincoln Elementary every week. At first he said he didn't know what to do, and the kid wouldn't even look at the book. But after a few weeks the kid started reading to him. Marcus had to get patient and figure out how to explain things so they made sense. The little kid got someone who shows up every single week. A worksheet can't do that.

Also, think about the kids who pick up trash in the park or on the street. It sounds small, but it teaches you that the places we all share are kind of your responsibility too. People notice when the park is clean, and the kids who did the cleaning start to feel like they can actually change something. I bet a lot of them won't walk past trash the same way again.

I know some students are worried that service will take time from homework, sports, jobs, or family stuff. Those are real problems and the school should plan for them. It doesn't have to be a lot, though. Ten or fifteen hours during the whole school year is enough to start a habit without messing up anybody's schedule. The school could also let kids do weekend projects, do some during school hours, or pick a service project that connects to a class they're already taking.

Some people say service should be a choice, because you can't really care about something you're forced to do. But a lot of kids who start because they have to end up liking it, and some keep doing it after graduation. Sometimes a requirement makes you walk through a door you never would have opened on your own. Every student should get the chance to find out what they can give to other people.

So I'm asking you to require community service for all students. The benefits for the students and for the community are too big to leave up to chance. If we plan it well and keep the hours reasonable, this requirement can help our school graduate kids who are capable, caring, and ready to contribute.

Respectfully,

A Concerned Student
