Asking more than one person for advice is a good way to make a better decision. I think this because nobody sees a problem from every side, and one person can easily miss something important.

Say a girl is trying to pick between two colleges. Her parents might care most about how much it costs and how far away it is. Her teacher might tell her that one school has a better program for the subject she likes. Her cousin goes to one of the schools, so he could tell her what it is really like there, like the crowded dorms and how cold the winters get. Together that tells her way more than one person could.

Another reason is that people have bias. Everybody giving advice has their own life and wants that change what they say. A mom might want her kid to stay close to home, and a friend might want somebody to hang out with at the same school. If a lot of different people who don't know each other all say the same thing, then it is easier to trust. If they disagree, then she finds out the choice is hard, and she can take her time and think about what matters most to her.

Disagreement helps too, because it makes you explain why you think what you think. Say one person tells you to take the safe job and another says to take the risk. You have to figure out which worries are real for you. Doing that can show you what you actually care about.

The choice is still yours, and too many opinions get confusing. But a few good ones help you pick.
