Drivers Should Put the Phone Down

Almost everybody has a cell phone these days, and I think a lot of people feel like they need it with them all the time, even when they are driving. But a car is a huge heavy machine going really fast, and there is not much room for mistakes. I believe drivers should not be allowed to use their cell phones at all while they are driving, because even one second of not paying attention can kill someone, and no text or phone call is worth that.

The main reason is how our brains work. Driving takes a lot of attention. You have to watch the road, check your mirrors, see how fast the other cars are going, and think about what a person walking or on a bike might do. Studies have shown that people are bad at doing two hard things at the same time. When people think they are multitasking, they are really just switching back and forth really fast, and every time they switch there is a moment where they are not looking at what is in front of them. On the highway, the few seconds it takes to read a text is about the length of a football field. A lot of bad things can happen in that much space.

Some people say that hands-free calling is a safe middle ground. I can see why they think that, since both hands are still on the wheel. But the problem with phone calls is not just about your hands, it is about your mind. Studies of drivers who use hands-free systems found that they react slower and miss things that are right in front of them, like a stopped car or a light that turned red. Talking to someone makes your brain focus on the conversation even if your eyes are on the road. If the law says hands-free is okay, it tells people that it is safe, and then they will talk for a long time while driving.

Another argument is that a full ban is not realistic because people use their phones for directions and for emergencies. I think those are fair points, but there are easy answers to them. You can put in the directions before you start driving, and if you get lost you can pull over and look at the map. If there is a real emergency like a crash or someone getting sick, the driver can stop somewhere safe and call for help. The rule is about using the phone while the car is being driven, so a car parked on the side of the road is different. Almost everything people need their phone for can wait a few minutes.

A simple rule is also easier to understand and to enforce than a complicated one. If the law allows some things but not others, a police officer cannot really tell if the driver was dialing a number, reading a text, or looking at a map. The driver can just say they were doing the allowed one. A complete ban gets rid of that problem. It can also change how people act. Look at seat belt laws and the campaigns against drunk driving. A strong rule with education behind it can change what people think is normal. Not that long ago lots of people did not wear seat belts, and now most people would never even think about driving without one.

Finally, this is not only about the driver. When someone is distracted they put their passengers in danger, along with other drivers, kids walking to school, and workers on the side of the road. Those people never chose to take that risk. Using a phone while driving is a choice one person makes, but someone else might be the one who pays for it.

Cell phones are really useful, but the road is not the place to use them. Drivers should wait until the car is stopped, because every message can be answered later, but a life that is lost cannot be brought back.
