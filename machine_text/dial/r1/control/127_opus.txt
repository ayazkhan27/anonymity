Last year my older cousin got a job offer in another state and she had no idea if she should take it. She didn't just ask one person. She called her parents, she talked to one of her old professors from college, and she texted her friend who moved across the country two years ago. Everybody told her something different. But by the end she understood her situation way better than when she started. I think that shows why asking more than one person for advice helps you make a better choice. When you ask a bunch of people you get more information, you don't get stuck with just one person's bias, and you figure out what you actually want.

The first reason is that nobody knows everything. Every person only has their own experiences to go off of. My cousin's parents know her really well and they know about her money stuff, but they have never lived far away from home. Her professor knew about her field and he could tell her if the company was a good one or not. Her friend could tell her what it is really like to move to a new city where you don't know anyone. She said the first few months were super lonely, but then she made new friends. None of them could have given my cousin the whole picture by themselves. Put together, they covered money, her career, and what her everyday life would be like. It's kind of like a puzzle. Each person gives you a piece and the pieces one person is missing, someone else fills in.

The second reason is that everyone has their own bias, even if they are trying to help. Parents usually want their kids to stay close, so they might only talk about the bad parts of moving. A friend who loves adventure might tell you to just go for it and not think about what could go wrong. Like my soccer coach, who always tells kids to focus on sports more than anything because sports is what he cares about. These people aren't trying to trick anybody. It's just that what they hope for and what they are scared of changes what they tell you. If you only listen to one person, you might end up making a choice that fits what they want instead of what you want. But if you hear a few different opinions, you can see where people disagree and ask why. That helps you tell which advice is based on facts and which is just based on feelings.

The last reason is that hearing different opinions helps you find out what is important to you. Sometimes you don't even know what you care about until somebody says something you disagree with. When my cousin's dad told her the job wasn't worth leaving her family for, she really wanted to argue with him. That's how she knew she really wanted the job. Then when her friend warned her about being lonely, she knew she needed a plan to stay in touch, so she set up video calls with her family every week before she even signed anything. Every conversation was kind of like a mirror that showed her something about herself she hadn't really thought about before. In the end she took the job, but she had a plan and she knew what she was doing. She didn't just do it randomly.

Some people might say that asking a lot of people just makes you more confused. And that's true if you ask like twenty people and they all say different things and you don't think about any of it. But the point isn't to count votes and do whatever most people say. The point is to get information, think about it, and then make your own choice. Usually asking a few people who think differently is enough. You get the good parts without getting totally confused.

In conclusion, asking for more than one opinion helps people make better choices because you get more information, you balance out one person's bias, and you learn what you care about. Big choices like picking a college, getting a new job, or moving somewhere new deserve more than one opinion. Just like my cousin, anyone with a hard choice can listen to a lot of people and then decide for themselves.
