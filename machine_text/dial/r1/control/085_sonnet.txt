I agree with my principal's decision that every student should have to do at least one extracurricular activity. School is not just about passing tests. A lot of the real learning happens after the last bell, and it's stuff you can't get from a classroom.

My first reason is that activities help students build skills they will actually use. Like if you are on the yearbook, you have to meet deadlines, take pictures, write captions, and work with the other editors. If one person is late, the whole book is late. You can't really learn that kind of responsibility from a worksheet. Student council is the same way. They have to plan a fall dance, make a budget, and argue about whether to spend the money on a DJ or on decorations. Those are the same things adults do at their jobs every day.

My second reason is that activities help students feel like they belong somewhere. A lot of kids feel lost at a big school, especially when they are new. When I started at my school I didn't know anybody, and I ate lunch by myself for weeks. Then a friend talked me into joining the cross-country team. In about a month I had people to sit with and I looked forward to practice. Kids who feel connected to their school usually come more often and care more about their grades. If the activity is required, then every student gets that chance, even the shy ones who would never sign up on their own.

Some people say a requirement is not fair because some students have jobs or have to take care of their little brothers and sisters. I think that is a good point, but we can fix it without getting rid of the rule. The school could have activities during lunch or short meetings, and it could count community service or clubs outside of school. The rule can be flexible but it should still be there. If it isn't, the students who need a place to belong the most will probably be the ones who stay away.

Other people say students already have too much homework. But I think kids who stay active manage their time better, not worse. When you have a busy schedule you have to plan. Lots of athletes and band members get their homework done faster because they know practice is coming up.

In conclusion, requiring one activity is a small thing to ask for a big reward. It makes students try something new, meet new people, and practice teamwork and responsibility. For these reasons I support the principal's decision, and I think our school will be a better community because of it.
