Last spring my cousin Danielle had a really big decision to make. She could take a job at a hospital two states away or she could stay at the clinic where she had worked for four years. The first person she asked was her mom, and her mom told her to stay. If Danielle stopped right there she would of had an answer, but I don't think it would have been a very good one. Instead she asked three more people, and her decision came out way better because of that. I think that asking a few people for advice is better then just asking one person, and here is why.

My first reason is that each person only knows part of what is going on. Danielle's mom was thinking about how far away the new city was and how lonely Danielle might get. Her boss at the clinic told her the truth, which was that there was no way for her to move up at the clinic and that the hospital would teach her things the clinic never could. He didn't even have a reason to want her to leave, so that says a lot. Then she asked a friend who had moved for a job a few years ago. Her friend told her about stuff she never thought of, like paying a security deposit, car insurance costing more, and how expensive it is to fly home for the holidays. Nobody was wrong. They each just knew a different part, and Danielle needed all the parts to understand the whole thing.

My second reason is that people put their own feelings into their advice, and a lot of the time they don't even know they are doing it. Danielle's mom really did think staying was smarter. But she also didn't want her daughter living six hours away from her. If you only ask one person, you can't tell which part is facts and which part is just what they want. When you ask a few people you can see it. Danielle noticed her mom was the only one who said being lonely was the biggest problem. That helped her think about it in a fair way and not act like it was the only thing that mattered.

My third reason is that when you compare what people say, you find out what the real question is. If everybody agrees, then that is probably right. All four people said the hospital was better for her career, so she didn't have to worry about that part anymore. What they didn't agree on was if moving was worth it for her personal life. So that was the part she had to figure out herself. It is kind of like when somebody gets a second opinion from a doctor. If both doctors say you need the same surgery, you can feel better about getting it. If they say different things, then you know you need to ask more questions first.

Some people say that asking too many people just makes you confused. That can be true, because some people keep asking and asking so they never have to decide. But that doesn't mean you should only ask one person. It means you should ask a normal amount of people who actually know something about it. Also, getting advice is not the same as doing whatever people tell you. Danielle didn't just count up votes. She listened to everyone and then she picked for herself. That made it her own decision, and it wouldn't have been if she just did what her mom said.

In the end Danielle took the hospital job. She asked to start later so she could save up money to move, which was her friend's idea. She has been there eight months now and she just started the training her boss talked about. One conversation could not have gotten her to that plan. It took four, and that is why I think you should always ask more than one person.
