I think it is a good idea to make every student join at least one extracurricular activity. School is supposed to get us ready for life, and life is not only about tests. In life you have to work with other people, keep your promises, and figure out what you like. If everyone has to join something, then everyone gets a place to learn those things.

Think about a kid who starts high school and is really shy. He might go home every day and not talk to anybody, and at lunch he eats alone in the library. If someone asks him to pick something, even something small, he might choose yearbook. There he meets a photographer who teaches him how to frame a picture, and an editor who needs captions written by Friday. After one semester he has jobs to do, friends, and a reason to come to school. Without the rule, he probably never would have tried.

Some people say that making students join things takes away their freedom. I get why they feel that way, but the rule does not tell you which activity to pick. You can choose chess club, debate, robotics, or the school garden. What the rule takes away is only the option to do nothing at all. Most adults would not call that freedom. It is more like drifting around without a plan.

Activities also teach responsibility in a way classes sometimes cannot. If a soccer player skips practice, the whole team feels it. If a student council member misses a meeting, the committee might not have enough people to vote. Other kids are counting on you, not just a teacher grading a worksheet, so you learn that your effort matters to other people. That lesson sticks around long after the season is over.

Finally, activities help students find out what they are good at. A test grade can show that you can solve equations, but it does not show if you are a good leader or a good listener. Student council can show leadership. Theater can show confidence. Sports can show you how to keep going after a bad loss. Some kids find a passion that becomes their job someday, and others just find out they like being part of a group that is bigger than them.

Some people will argue that a rule like this cannot make anyone really interested, and that kids will just be mad about being forced. Some kids will be mad at first, I know that. But interest usually comes after you start doing something, not before. Nobody loves piano before their first lesson. The requirement opens the door, and then the student decides what to do once they are inside.

That is why I agree with the principal. Every student deserves a chance to belong, to contribute, and to figure out who they are outside the classroom. One required activity is a small thing to ask, and it can make a big difference that lasts for years.
