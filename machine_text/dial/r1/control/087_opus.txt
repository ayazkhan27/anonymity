I agree with the principal that every student should have to do at least one extracurricular activity. Some people think it will take away time from homework or make kids do stuff they don't like. But I think it will help students more than hurt them. Activities teach you things you can't learn in class, they make you feel like part of the school, and they help you find out what you actually like.

First, activities teach you stuff you can't really get from a textbook. If you play on the soccer team you learn that you have to show up to practice even when it's freezing and nobody wants to run. If you work on the yearbook you learn about deadlines because the pages have to go to the printer on a certain day no matter what. Kids on student council learn how to talk in front of people, listen when people complain, and compromise when everybody doesn't agree. Colleges and jobs want people who can do these things, and a lot of kids would never practice them if they just went to class and then went home.

Second, activities make students feel like they belong. A big school can be lonely, especially if you're new or shy. When you join something like chess club or the drama crew, all of a sudden there are people who expect to see you and notice if you're not there. My cousin moved to a new town in ninth grade and didn't know anybody. She only joined cross country because a teacher kept pushing her to, but by October she had a whole group of friends to sit with at lunch. Also kids who feel connected to their school usually keep their grades up more and don't skip as much.

Third, if it's required, kids might find out they like things they never knew about. A lot of teenagers don't try new things because they're nervous or they think they'll be bad at it. If everyone has to pick something, a kid who thinks he hates writing might find out he loves taking pictures for the school newspaper. That could even change what he wants to study later on.

But the rule should be flexible. Some students have jobs or have to help their family, so they should get options that fit their schedule, and there should be enough choices so everyone can find something. If the school does that, I think the principal made a good decision, and I hope other students will support it too.
