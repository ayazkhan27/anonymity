Dear Principal Morales,

I am writing to you about the idea of making every student do community service. I think you should do it, but it shouldn't be too many hours and we should get to pick how we do them.

My first reason is that most kids won't ever try community service unless somebody makes them. Last spring my friend Dana signed up to tutor second graders at Lincoln Elementary, but only because her older sister made her go. She thought it was going to be really boring. But then she spent ten weeks helping this boy named Marcus sound out his words, and by May he was reading chapter books to her! Now Dana says she wants to be a teacher when she grows up. She never would of found that out by herself, and I bet a lot of us are the same way.

Another reason is that it helps the community and you can actually see it. Some people in my neighborhood pick up trash on Creek Road one Saturday every month. Before they started the ditch was full of bottles and fast food bags and it was gross. Now families go on walks there. Our school has around nine hundred students. If everybody did just twenty hours a year that would be eighteen thousand hours of tutoring and cleaning and visiting nursing homes. Our town doesn't have the money to pay for all that.

I know some people are against this because my own friends say it. They have sports and jobs and little brothers and sisters to watch, plus homework that keeps them up late already. I think that's fair, and that is why the rule has to be flexible. Twenty hours in a whole school year is only like half an hour a week. Kids should be able to finish in the summer if they want. Also the school should count lots of things, like helping at a church food pantry or coaching little kids soccer. If someone babysits every day after school they could just do one weekend project and be done.

Some people also say if you are forced to do it then it's not real service. I don't agree. We have to take math and nobody says the stuff we learn in math is fake. The rule just gets us in the door, and after that it's up to us.

I hope you will say yes to this and keep it flexible.

Sincerely,
An eighth grade student
