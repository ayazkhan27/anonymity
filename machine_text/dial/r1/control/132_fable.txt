Why One Opinion Is Not Enough

Last year my cousin got offered a job in a different city and she didn't know if she should take it. The first person she asked was her dad. He told her to stay because the pay was only a little bit more and she would be far away from the family. If she stopped right there she probably would of said no to the job. But she kept asking people, and three other people told her things that changed her mind. I think this shows that asking more than one person helps you make a better choice. One person only knows part of the story, people have their own feelings mixed in, and hearing different opinions helps you figure out what you really want.

My first reason is that nobody knows everything. My cousin's dad knew about money and about family, but he never worked in the kind of job she does. Then she talked to a lady she used to work with who had moved for a job before. That lady said the new company was really good at training people and after two years there she could get way better jobs. She also asked a friend who lives in that city. Her friend told her how much rent really costs and which neighborhoods are too far to drive from. None of them had the whole answer by themself. But when you put it all together she knew about the pay, the career and what living there would be like. Her dad could not have told her all that.

My second reason is that people's feelings get into their advice. Her dad loves her and he didn't want her to move away. So of course he said stay, even though he thought he was just being practical. I don't think that is lying. It's just how people are. It's kind of like doctors. A doctor who does surgery all day might tell you to get surgery faster than another doctor would, and that's why people get a second opinion before a big operation. If you only ask one person you can't tell what part of the advice is about you and what part is really about them. If you ask a few people it is easier to notice, because the other people don't have that same feeling.

My third reason is that listening to other people teaches you about yourself. When her friend was telling her about the city, my cousin noticed she was getting excited. When her dad was listing reasons to stay, she noticed she kept trying to think of ways he was wrong. Nobody told her that, she just saw how she was reacting. Also it helps when people disagree. If everybody says the same thing then you can feel pretty sure. If they say different things, then you know which part is the hard part and you can think about that the most.

I'm not saying you should just count votes and do what most people say. And if you ask too many people it gets confusing. It is still your choice in the end because you are the one who has to live with it. My cousin took the job and she decided that herself. But she knew a lot more than she did after just talking to her dad, and that is why you should ask more than one person.
