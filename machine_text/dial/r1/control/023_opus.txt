Dear Principal Morgan,

I am writing to you about if every student should have to do community service. I think you should make it required, because it helps students and it helps our town, and that is worth the time.

First of all, community service teaches you stuff you can't learn in a classroom. Last spring my friend Danielle started tutoring second graders at Lincoln Elementary two times a week. She told me that explaining subtraction to a seven year old actually made her get math better, and she got way more patient too. My friend Marcus joined a group that picks up trash at Riverside Park. He said he never really thought about where trash goes until he pulled like forty plastic bottles out of the creek in one afternoon. Things like that change how kids think about being responsible. A lot of kids would never try stuff like this unless they had to.

Second, our town really needs the help. The elementary school has a lot of kids who are behind in reading and the teachers can't help all of them one on one. The food bank on Main Street doesn't have enough volunteers on the weekends. If every student at our school did even a few hours a semester, that would be thousands of hours of help. That would make a big difference for the people who need those places.

I know some of my friends don't like this idea. They have homework, sports, jobs, and stuff they have to do for their families, and they are worried they won't have time. I get that, but I think there is a way to fix it. The requirement could be small, like ten hours a semester, and kids could pick projects that work with their schedules. Some of it could even be during school, like reading to little kids during a free period. That way it is a little bit for everyone but not too much for anyone.

Last, if it is required, then service becomes part of our school. When everybody does it, it isn't just a few kids anymore, it is something we can all be proud of together.

So that is why I hope you will make community service required for all students. A few hours from each of us can teach us things we will remember and help the people in our town who need it the most.

Sincerely,
Jordan Ellis
