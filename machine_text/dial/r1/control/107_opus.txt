Phones and Driving Don't Mix

Pretty much everyone has a phone on them all the time, and a lot of people get nervous when they can't find it. That habit comes with us into the car too. It's easy to think that looking at one text or taking a quick call is no big deal, but driving is the one thing we do every day where a few seconds of not paying attention can actually kill someone. That's why I think drivers should not use their phones while driving, except for emergencies and for GPS that's set up before you start driving.

The biggest problem is attention. When you drive, your brain has to keep track of how fast you're going, how close other cars are, people walking, traffic lights, and the road all at the same time. Reading a text takes your eyes off the road for a few seconds. On the highway, a car goes about the length of a football field in five seconds. If you look down that long, you're basically driving with your eyes closed. A little kid running into the street or a car slamming on its brakes isn't going to wait for you to finish typing.

Some people say hands-free calls fix this. Having both hands on the wheel does help, but it doesn't fix the real problem, which is that your mind is somewhere else. Studies have shown that people who are really into a phone conversation react slower and miss stuff right in front of them, even when they're looking straight ahead. Talking to someone in the passenger seat is different, because they can see the road too and they'll usually stop talking if something scary is happening. The person on the phone has no clue what you're dealing with.

Other people say a rule like this couldn't be enforced, or that adults should be able to decide for themselves. But we already have rules on the road when one person's choices can hurt other people. That's the whole reason we have drunk driving laws and speed limits. Most people who text and drive think they're good at it and nothing will happen to them, and that's exactly why it's so dangerous.

There are some exceptions that make sense. You should always be allowed to call 911 if you see a crash or a fire. And using a map on a phone that's mounted on the dashboard, if you set it up before you leave, isn't any worse than a screen built into the car. Other than that, your phone should stay quiet and out of your hands. Nothing on it is worth someone's life, whether it's yours or a stranger's.
