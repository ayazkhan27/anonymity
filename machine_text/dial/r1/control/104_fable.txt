Phones and Driving

Pretty much everybody I know has a phone, and pretty much everybody I know has looked at it while driving. I have done it too, I'm not going to lie. It feels like no big deal because most of the time nothing happens. You look down for a second, you look back up, and the road is still right there. But I think that is the reason phones are so dangerous in a car. People get used to nothing going wrong. In my opinion drivers should not be allowed to use their phone while the car is moving, except for emergencies and for directions that you set up before you leave.

The main reason is that driving takes a lot more focus than people think. You need your eyes on the road, your hands on the wheel, and your brain paying attention. Texting takes away all three of those at the same time. In drivers ed they told us that if you are on the highway and you look at a text for five seconds, you go about the length of a football field without looking. That is basically like driving with your eyes closed. A little kid could run into the street, or the car in front of you could slam on the brakes, and you would not even see it because you were typing "on my way." Thousands of people die every year in the US from distracted driving, and a lot of that is from phones.

Some people say the solution is hands free. If your phone is on the dashboard and you just talk instead of type, then your hands and eyes are where they should be. I agree this is better than texting, but it doesn't fix everything. Your mind is still on the conversation and not on the road. I have heard that people who are on a call can look right at something and not really notice it, because their brain is busy. Talking to someone in the passenger seat is different. A passenger can see what is going on, so if traffic gets bad they usually stop talking. The person on the phone has no clue you are trying to merge onto a busy freeway, so they just keep going.

Another reason is that it is not fair to other people. When a driver decides to use their phone, they aren't the only one who could get hurt. People walking, people on bikes, the passengers, and the other cars on the road never agreed to take that risk. We already think this way about drunk driving. Nobody says it's okay to drive after six beers as long as you feel fine, because if you are wrong somebody else pays for it. A person reading a text is kind of the same as a drunk driver for those few seconds.

I don't think the rule should be for every single situation though. If you see a crash or you think someone is following you, you should be able to call 911. GPS is useful too. A voice telling you "turn left in half a mile" is probably safer then trying to read every street sign. But you should type in the address while the car is still parked. If you need to change it, pull over. Same with music. Pick your playlist before you start driving.

Some people will say a law like this is too hard to enforce, and that's true, cops can't see inside every car. But laws aren't only about giving tickets. My mom told me that when she was a kid a lot of people did not wear seat belts, and now it's just normal and everyone does it. A law against phones could do the same thing over time. It would tell new drivers, especially teenagers like me who grew up with a phone, that the text can wait.

In conclusion, the text or the call will still be there when you park. If someone dies in a crash you can't undo it. So putting the phone away is really not a lot to ask.
