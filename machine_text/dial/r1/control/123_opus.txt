Why You Should Ask More Than One Person

Last spring I had to pick an elective for high school. First I asked my older cousin and he said take computer science because "thats where the jobs are." I almost just signed up right then. But I didnt. I also asked my art teacher, my best friend, and my mom. They all told me something different, and by the end I understood my choice way better than if I only talked to one person. So I think asking more than one person for advice usually helps you make a better decision. It helps because other people can see stuff you miss, it balances out peoples bias, and you get the whole picture of what your choosing.

The first reason is that every person only sees part of the situation. Nobody has done everything, so their advice comes from their own life. My cousin loves computer science because he is good at math and he likes puzzles. His advice was honest but it fit him, not really me. Then I talked to my art teacher. She said I spend like all my free time drawing, and there is a digital design class that mixes art and technology. I never even thought about that class. My cousin showed me one door and my teacher showed me a door I didnt know was there. When you ask a bunch of people, all their little pieces kind of fit together and you get closer to the real answer.

The second reason is that everybody has some bias, even if they mean well. Parents want you to pick the safe thing because they worry about you. Your friends might want you to pick whatever keeps you with them. A salesperson just wants you to buy what they are selling. They are not lying exactly, but what they want can change what they tell you. Like say someone is buying a used car. If they only ask the dealer, the dealer will say the car is great and its a good deal. But if they ask a friend who is a mechanic to look under the hood, and they read reviews online from people who own that car, they might find out the car has a transmission problem a lot. When you hear from people who want different things, you can tell when one person is leaning too far one way.

Third, when people tell you different things it makes you think harder about what you actually care about. If two people you trust disagree, you cant just do what they say. You have to figure out whose reasons make more sense and what matters to you. My mom told me to take the class with the easiest grading so my GPA stays good. My friend told me to take whatever sounded the most fun. So I had to decide if I cared more about grades or having fun. That was kind of uncomfortable to think about, but after I figured it out I felt a lot more sure about my choice. If you only get one piece of advice the decision feels easy, but you might skip the thinking you really need to do.

Asking more than one person also stops you from making dumb mistakes. Doctors know this, thats why people who need a big surgery are told to get a second opinion. The second doctor might look at the same tests and see something different, or they might have a treatment thats less risky, or they might just agree with the first doctor. Either way the patient wins. If both doctors agree, the patient can feel better about going ahead. If they dont agree, the patient found out about a big question before it was too late. This works for smaller stuff too, like having two teachers read your essay before you turn it in. One of them might catch a confusing sentence the other one missed.

Now, asking too many people can get overwhelming, and at the end of the day it is still your decision. Advice is like a tool, it doesnt replace your own thinking. The point isnt to count up votes and go with whatever most people said. The point is to get enough different views so you know what your doing. It is better to ask people who know different things, like an expert, someone who went through the same thing, and someone who knows you really well, than to ask ten people who are all the same.

In the end I picked the digital design class and it ended up being my favorite class all year. I never would of found it if I just listened to my cousin. Talking to a few different people helped me see more choices, notice when people were biased, and figure out what I really wanted. Whether your picking a class, a car, or what to do about a medical problem, listening to more than one person gives you the best chance to choose right.
