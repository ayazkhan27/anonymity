Put the Phone Down While Driving

I really think drivers should not use cell phones while they drive. Phones are great and I love mine, so I get why people want to keep them close. A car is a big heavy machine though, and staying safe is the most important thing on the road. A good driver keeps both eyes and a clear mind on the road, and that is the best way to keep everybody safe and happy.

First, driving takes a lot of focus. A good driver watches the road, checks the mirrors, and keeps track of the other cars and the people walking nearby. It is really hard to do all of that and also read a text. Smart people can do many nice things, but the brain is not so good at two big jobs at once. When you look at your phone for just five seconds on the highway, you travel the length of a football field. That is a lot of road to cover with your eyes somewhere else, and nobody has that much luck every day.

Some people say hands-free calling is a great fix, and that sounds nice. Both hands stay on the wheel, which is good. However, the mind is the part that matters most. Studies show that drivers who talk hands-free still miss things that are easy to see, like a red light or a stopped car. A fun, long talk with a friend is a lovely thing, but it pulls your attention away from the road. If the law says hands-free is fine, people will think it is totally safe and talk for a long time. That is not a smart plan, and it is not fair to the other drivers who are trying to be careful.

Other people say we need phones for maps and for help. I understand that, and those are good points. But there is an easy answer. You can set up your map before you start the trip, and if you need help you can pull over in a safe spot and call. Parking is a simple, safe, and smart choice. Almost everything can wait a few minutes, and the people you love will be glad you did. I would much rather get there a little late and in one piece than get a perfect route and a really big problem.

A clear rule is also better and easier for the police. If some phone uses are okay and some are not, an officer cannot tell if you were dialing, texting, or looking at a map. A rule for everything is simple and fair, and everybody knows what to do. Rules can change what people think is normal too. Seat belts are a great example. Long ago many people did not wear them, and now almost everyone buckles up without even thinking. A strong rule with good education can help people build better habits, and I think that would be a great thing for the whole country.

Also, this is about more than just the driver. Passengers, kids walking to school, bike riders, and workers by the road all trust drivers to take care. They get no say in the choice. They just hope the person in the next lane is paying attention. When one person picks a phone over the road, someone else's family can be the one that pays for it.

In conclusion, phones are very useful and I enjoy them, so I am not saying they are evil. I am saying the car is the wrong place for them. Wait until you are parked, because every text can be answered later. A hurt person is a much harder thing to fix, and that is a sad price to pay for one message.
