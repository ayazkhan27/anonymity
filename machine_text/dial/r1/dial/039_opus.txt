Dear Principal,

I am writing about whether every student should have to do community service. I think our school should make it a requirement, but it has to be flexible because students are already really busy.

Some of my friends already volunteer, and I have seen what it does for them. My friend Maya tutors second graders in reading every Tuesday after school. At first she only signed up because her mom made her. Now she talks about her students all the time, and she tells everyone when one of them finishes a chapter book. She is more patient now and she can talk in front of people without getting nervous. Another friend, Daniel, joined a group that cleans trash out of the creek behind the shopping center on weekends. He told me he never noticed how much garbage was in our town until he spent a morning pulling bottles and plastic bags out of the water. Now he picks up trash even when nobody is watching. You can learn responsibility from a lecture in class, but doing it yourself teaches it better.

I also get why other students are against the idea. A lot of us have jobs, sports practice, music lessons, or little brothers and sisters to watch at home. Homework already takes hours every night. If the school made us do fifty hours a semester, some kids would fall behind or stay up too late, and the service would feel like a punishment. That would make the whole thing pointless.

That is why I think the requirement should be reasonable. Maybe twenty hours a year would be enough, and students could pick when and how to do them. Someone who works on weekdays could help at a food bank on Saturday. An athlete could coach a kids team during the off-season. The school could even count things students already do, like working at a church event or reading to patients at the hospital. If students get to choose, the hours would feel very real instead of forced.

A requirement would also help kids who would never volunteer on their own because they do not know where to start. Some people only find out they like helping after they try it, but they never try it unless someone pushes them.

Our town gives us a lot, like parks, libraries, and teachers. A small requirement that works with our schedules would teach students to give something back. I hope you will think about it.

Sincerely,
A concerned student
