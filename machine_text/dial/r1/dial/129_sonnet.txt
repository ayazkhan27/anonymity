When I had to choose between two summer programs last year, my first instinct was to ask my mom and just do whatever she said. She thought the robotics camp was a safe bet because it looked really good for college. Then I talked to my older cousin, who did a similar program, and he said it was mostly boring lectures. A friend from my class told me the art workshop was fun, with a small group where everyone got to make things. By the end of the week I had three opinions, and I made a much better choice than I would have with only one.

The first reason multiple opinions help is that every person sees only part of the picture. My mom knew what colleges like, and she was so sure about it, yet she had never gone to either program. My cousin had real experience, though he is a different kind of student than I am. Each person had good information the others did not have, and putting it together gave me a great look at the whole story.

A second reason is that people have bias they often cannot see. A parent may want what feels safe, a friend may want you to join the same thing they picked, and a salesperson just wants your money, which is kind of sad. When several people disagree, you notice those leanings. If three people who do not know each other all warn you about the same problem, that is a really bad sign and worth taking seriously. If only one person says it, you can think about it more carefully.

Finally, hearing other views makes you think about your own reasons. When my cousin said the robotics camp was dull, I had to ask myself what I truly wanted from the summer. I wanted to build things with my hands, not sit and listen all day. Without that talk, I might never have figured it out, and I would have hated it.

Of course, more opinions are not always better. Too many voices are confusing and stressful, and in the end the choice belongs to the person who has to live with it. Asking a few trusted people gives you more information and a smarter plan. Still, I worry about kids who only ask one person. They can end up stuck in a program they hate, with a wasted summer and a terrible feeling that they never got a real chance to choose.
