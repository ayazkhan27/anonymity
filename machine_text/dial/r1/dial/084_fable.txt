One Activity Is Not Too Much to Ask

When I first heard that our principal wants every student to join at least one activity after school, I groaned. I already have homework, chores, and a little brother I have to watch on Thursdays. It sounded like a terrible, unfair rule to me. But after I thought about it for a while, I decided it was a really good idea. I agree with the principal, as long as the rule has some room in it.

The biggest reason is that kids don't know what they like until somebody makes them try it. Last year my friend Marcus signed up for yearbook because his mom made him. He was so angry about it. He complained and whined for two weeks and said it was boring and stupid. By October he was staying late to fix the photo pages, and now he says he wants to study graphic design. He would have just gone home at 3:00 and played video games.

Activities also teach you things that classes can't. In math, if I mess up, it only goes on my grade. On the soccer team, if I skip practice, ten other people have to do my part. Kids on student council have to listen to complaints about the awful lunch menu, but they still have to be polite and plan something with a budget. Adults do that every day. A worksheet does not teach it.

There is a reason with friends too. Our school has about nine hundred students. You can go three years and only know the people in your classes. That is lonely and kind of sad, but a club mixes the grades up and makes it really fun and friendly. A sixth grader in chess club sits across from an eighth grader, and then the hallway is not so scary.

I know what people will say against this. Some kids have jobs, long bus rides, or have to help at home, and some are very shy. So the school should have clubs that meet at lunch or in advisory. It should count small things, like helping in the library once a week. A student who has a serious problem at home should be able to talk to a counselor and get out of it.

With those changes, one activity is an hour or two a week. Most of us will never sign up by ourselves, and that is the sad part. We are lazy, or scared of looking dumb, or worried that nobody will talk to us. I was so afraid of that in sixth grade, and I wasted a whole year sitting alone and bored at home. The principal is right, but it is too bad that kids have to be forced before they stop being afraid.
