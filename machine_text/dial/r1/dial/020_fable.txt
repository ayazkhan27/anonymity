Dear Principal Morales,

I am writing about the plan to make every student do community service. I think you should do it, but with a small number of hours and a lot of choices for how we finish them.

My main reason is that most kids will never try service unless somebody makes them. Last spring my friend Dana signed up to tutor second graders at Lincoln Elementary. She didn't want to. Her older sister dragged her there, and Dana told me it was going to be boring and a waste of ten weeks. Then she got a boy named Marcus who could not sound out words. By May he was reading chapter books to her. Now Dana says she might be a teacher. She would not have found that out by herself, and I don't think the rest of us would either.

The town needs it too. Some people from my street pick up trash on Creek Road one Saturday a month. Before that the ditch was full of bottles and fast food bags, and nobody walked there because it was gross. Now families do. Our school has about nine hundred students. If each one did only twenty hours a year, that is eighteen thousand hours of tutoring, cleaning, and visiting nursing homes. Our town can't pay for those things, so right now a lot of it just doesn't get done.

I know what kids will say, because my own friends complain about it. They have jobs, sports, little brothers and sisters to watch, and homework that keeps them up late. They are tired already. That is not a dumb worry, and it is why the rule can't be strict. Twenty hours in a school year is about half an hour a week. Students should be able to finish in the summer. The school should count lots of things, like working at a church food pantry or coaching little kids in soccer. A kid who babysits every afternoon could do one weekend project and be done.

Some people say forced service is not real service. I don't agree. We are forced to take math, and nobody says the math we learn is fake.

If the rule is too hard, kids will hate it and cheat on their hours, and then the whole thing fails. Please don't let that happen.

Sincerely,
An eighth grade student
