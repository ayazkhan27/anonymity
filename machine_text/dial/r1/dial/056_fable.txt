When my cousin broke her leg in tenth grade, she missed almost five weeks of school. Her teachers were kind and sent home packets of worksheets, and she tried her best with them. Still, nobody was there to explain the chemistry, and she ended up taking the class again over the summer. If her school had let her join classes by video, she would have kept up with her friends and enjoyed a normal summer. Her story is the main reason I believe students would benefit from having the option to attend classes from home.

The key word is option. Most students like being in the building and learn well there, and that is great. The choice should exist for the students who need the help. Some kids are healing from a long illness or injury. Others live an hour's bus ride from the nearest high school, and they could use those two hours to sleep, read, or play a sport they love. Some feel calm and safe at home in a way that a crowded cafeteria makes hard. For these students, learning at home is a good way to keep learning at all.

Distance learning also lets students work in a way that fits them. Online lessons are often recorded, so a student who wants a clear explanation of a math problem can watch it again and feel sure about it. A student who works a part-time job to help the family can finish assignments in the evening with a fresh mind. Small schools could even offer exciting classes, like a third year of a foreign language or advanced physics, by connecting students to a talented teacher somewhere else. I think that is a smart and fair use of technology.

People who oppose this idea usually say that students at home will get distracted, and I agree that they have a fair point. It takes real discipline to pay attention when your comfortable bed and your phone are three feet away. Friends are also easier to make in person, where you can laugh together at lunch. Schools can handle this with simple rules that everyone can trust. They can require attendance checks, cameras on during discussions, and a solid grade to stay in the program. A student whose grades slip can be called back into the building.

School is supposed to be about learning, whatever chair you sit in. Right now, a student who gets hurt or sick for a month can fall behind and lose a whole semester, the way my cousin did. An option to attend from home would keep that from happening to someone else.
