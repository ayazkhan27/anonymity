Dear Principal Morgan,

I am writing about whether every student should have to do community service. I think you should make it a requirement, because what students and our town get out of it is worth the hours.

First, community service teaches things you can't learn in a classroom. Last spring my friend Danielle started tutoring second graders at Lincoln Elementary two times a week. She told me that explaining subtraction to a seven year old made her get math better herself, and she learned patience she never had before. My friend Marcus joined a group that picks up litter along Riverside Park. He said he never thought about where trash goes until he pulled forty plastic bottles out of the creek in one afternoon. Stuff like this changes how kids see responsibility, and a lot of kids would never try it if they didn't have to.

Second, our town actually needs the help. The elementary school has more kids who struggle with reading than the teachers can work with one on one. The food bank on Main Street doesn't have enough volunteers on weekends. If every student at our school gave even a few hours each semester, that would add up to thousands of hours. That makes a real difference for the people who count on these places.

I get why some of my friends don't like the idea. They have homework, sports, jobs, and stuff at home, and they think service will take time they don't have. That is fair, but it can be fixed with a plan that makes sense. The requirement could be small, like ten hours a semester, and students could pick projects that fit their schedules. Some of it could even happen during school, like reading to younger kids during a free period. A flexible rule would ask a little of everyone and wouldn't be too much for anyone.

Finally, a requirement makes service part of who we are as a school. When everyone does it, it stops being something only a few kids do and turns into something we are all really proud of together.

So I hope you will make community service required for all students. A few hours from each of us can teach us lessons we will keep, help the neighbors who need it most, and make our whole town a better and happier place.

Sincerely,
Jordan Ellis
