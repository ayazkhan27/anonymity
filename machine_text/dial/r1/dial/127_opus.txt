When my older cousin had to decide if she should take a job in another state, she didn't just ask one person. She called her parents, talked to an old professor, and texted a friend who moved across the country two years ago. Everyone told her something different, but by the end she understood her own situation way better. Her story shows why asking more than one person is a good idea. Getting lots of opinions gives you more information, protects you from one person's bias, and helps you figure out what you really want.

The first reason is that nobody knows everything. Every person only has their own experiences. My cousin's parents knew a lot about her personality and her money, but they never lived far from home, so they couldn't really help with that part. Her professor knew her field and could tell her if the company was good and respected. Her friend was the most helpful about real life. She told my cousin the first few months were lonely and hard, but she also said she eventually made great new friends and loved her new city. None of these people could have given her the whole picture alone. Together they covered money, career, and everyday life. When you get advice from different people, it fits together like a puzzle, and one person fills in what another one missed.

The second reason is that everybody has a bias, even when they care about you and mean well. Parents usually want their kids to stay close, so they might only talk about the risks of moving. A friend who loves adventure might push you to go for it without thinking about the bad parts. A coach might tell a student to focus only on sports because that's what the coach cares about. None of them are trying to trick you, but their own hopes and fears change what they say. If you only listen to one person, you might make a choice that fits their values instead of yours. That isn't fair to you. When you hear several opinions, you can notice where they clash and ask why. Comparing them helps you see which advice is based on facts and which is just feelings.

Finally, hearing different opinions helps you find out what matters most to you. Sometimes you don't know what you want until somebody says something you disagree with. When my cousin's dad told her the job wasn't worth leaving her family, she felt like arguing back, and that showed her how much she really wanted this chance. When her friend warned her about being lonely, she was a little scared, but it helped her make a smart plan. She set up weekly video calls with her family before she even signed the offer. Every conversation was like a mirror that showed her a part of herself she hadn't looked at closely. She took the job, but she did it with a clear plan and not on a whim.

Some people might say that asking too many people just makes you confused. It's true that you can get overwhelmed if you collect a ton of opinions and don't think about them. However, the point isn't to count votes or just do what most people say. The point is to gather information, think about it, and then make your own choice. Asking a few thoughtful people with different backgrounds is usually enough to stay calm and not get confused.

In conclusion, asking for more than one opinion helps people make better choices. It gives you more information, balances out one person's bias, and helps you understand what you value. Big decisions like picking a college, changing jobs, or moving somewhere new are hard, and they deserve more than one point of view. Even with all that good advice, my cousin still says leaving was painful, and she misses her family a lot. But listening to different people didn't make the hard part go away. It just made sure that when she felt sad or homesick, she knew the choice was really hers and not someone else's mistake.
