I agree with the principal's decision that every student should join at least one extracurricular activity. School is about more than passing tests, and the time after the last bell is where students learn good things a classroom can teach only a little.

First, activities help students build great skills. A student on the yearbook has to meet deadlines, take nice photos, and work with a team of editors. If one person is late, the whole book is late, and that is a big problem. That kind of responsibility is hard to learn from a worksheet. Student council is a good example too. They plan the fall dance, set a budget, and argue about whether to spend money on a fun DJ or on pretty decorations. Adults use these same skills at work every day, and it helps to practice early.

Second, activities help students find a place to belong. Lots of kids feel lost at a big school, especially in ninth grade, and that feeling is hard. When I was new, I knew nobody, and I sat alone at lunch for weeks. It was a sad time. Then a friend talked me into joining the cross-country team. Within a month I had friends to sit with, and I loved going to practice. Students who feel happy at school come more often and care more about their grades. A required activity gives every student a chance at that, even the shy ones who would hide at home instead of signing up.

Some people say a requirement is unfair because certain students have jobs or have to watch their little brothers and sisters. That is a fair worry, but we can fix it without losing the rule. The school can offer fun clubs during lunch or short sessions, and it can count community service done outside school. The rule should be flexible, but it should stay. Without it, the kids who need friends the most are the ones who stay away, and that is a bad result.

Others say students have too much homework already. However, I think active students often manage their time better. A busy schedule forces a person to plan, which is a smart habit. Many athletes and band members finish homework faster because they know practice is coming, and they want to go.

In the end, one required activity is a small ask with a big reward. It pushes students to try new things, make new friends, and practice teamwork. For these reasons, I support the principal's decision, and I think our school will be a better community with it, even if some students complain at first.
