Dear Principal,

I am writing about the plan to make students have a B average to play sports or join activities. I think this rule is a mistake. I know you want kids to work harder in class. Still, I think this rule would hurt the same students it is supposed to help.

First, a lot of students at our school have a C average, and a C is a passing grade. Those students do their work and meet the standard. If you take away the one thing that keeps them coming to school, they might stop trying in their classes. For a lot of kids, practice after school is the reason they get out of bed in the morning. Without it, school would feel like a chore.

Second, sports and clubs teach things that a report card does not show. On the soccer team or in drama club, students learn teamwork and responsibility. They learn how to deal with losing a game or messing up a line. They also have to manage their time because they have homework and practice on the same night. These skills help with jobs later, and a kid with a C needs them as much as a kid with an A.

Third, activities give students adults who pay attention to them. A coach or club advisor can tell when a student is struggling and push them to get help. If we kick those students off the team, we also cut them off from that help. That would leave them alone with their problems.

My idea is to keep the C average rule and add a tutoring program for players whose grades drop. That way students can stay on the team while they work on their grades.

Thank you for asking what we think. I am worried that if this rule passes, a lot of kids will lose the one part of school they care about, and some of them will fall further behind.

Sincerely,
A Concerned Student
