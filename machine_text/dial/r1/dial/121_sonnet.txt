I think asking more than one person for advice is a good way to make a better choice. One person can't see a problem from every side, and that is a big reason why it helps.

Say a girl is picking between two colleges. Her parents might worry about the cost and how far away it is. Her teacher might say one school has a really good program for the subject she loves. Her cousin goes to one of the schools and can tell her what it is like, even the bad parts like the crowded dorms and the cold winters. Each person knows something the others don't. When she puts it all together, she gets a much better picture than she would from one talk.

Another reason is bias. Every adviser has their own wants and worries. A mom might want her kid to stay home, and a friend might want company. If several people who don't know each other say the same thing, she can trust it more. If they disagree, she learns the choice is hard, so she can slow down and think about what matters to her. That is not a bad thing at all.

Disagreement also helps because it makes her explain her own reasons. If one person says take the safe job and another says take the risk, she has to figure out which fears are real. That is a very good way to find out what she values.

Sure, the choice still belongs to her, and too many voices can be confusing. Advice is only a tool. Still, a few caring opinions, weighed with care, help a person avoid mistakes and feel happy and sure about a great decision.
