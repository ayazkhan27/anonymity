When my cousin wanted to buy a used car from a neighbor, he asked three people what they thought. His dad said the price was fair. A friend who works at a repair shop told him to look under the hood first. His older sister asked if he could still pay for insurance after he bought it. Each of them only knew one part of it, but together they gave him a really good answer. That is why I think asking more than one person for advice helps you make a better choice.

First, people give advice from what they know, but nobody is smart about everything. His dad had bought a lot of cars, so he knew prices, but he was bad with engines. The friend knew engines, but he had no idea how much money my cousin made at his job. If my cousin had stopped after talking to his dad, he would have bought the car that afternoon, and it would have been a mistake. He kept asking, and he found out the timing belt was worn out. That was a problem, but it helped him get the price down by four hundred dollars. One opinion would have cost him money.

Second, people lean toward the things they like, and it is hard to tell when you only ask one person. Say a girl is picking between two high schools and she only asks her best friend. The friend will probably tell her to pick the school she goes to, because she likes it there and wants company. That advice is honest, but it is unfair to the other school. If the girl also asks a teacher, a parent, and a kid from the other school, she can see where her friend is coming from. Her friend still counts, but now she can tell what is true about the school and what is only true about her friend.

Third, more opinions help when they agree. If I ask four people whether I should quit the soccer team to work on my grades, and all four say the same thing for their own reasons, I feel very sure about it. Doctors do this too. A patient who is told she needs surgery is probably scared, but she can get a second opinion. If the second doctor says the same thing, she can trust it more. Sometimes the doctors disagree, but that is helpful too, because it shows the question is hard and she should think about it longer.

Some people say that too many opinions just make you confused. That can happen, but I think the real mistake is wanting someone else to decide for you. Advice is only information. The choice still belongs to the person who has to live with it. My cousin listened to all three people, but he made the choice himself. He checked the engine, talked the price down, added up the insurance, and then he bought the car.

Asking a lot of people takes more time, and sometimes you hear things that hurt. It can be annoying and a little scary to find out you were wrong. But a bad choice made alone is worse, because then you are stuck with the mistake and the regret. I would take a few hard talks over that.
