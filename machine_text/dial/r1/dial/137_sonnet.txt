Why More Than One Opinion Is Better

Last spring my cousin Dana had a really hard choice to make. She had to pick between two colleges. One was a big state university close to home with a great scholarship. The other was a small private school three states away with an awesome marine biology program, which is the thing she has loved since she was ten. She asked her parents and they said take the money. She asked her best friend and she said follow the ocean. Then she asked her old science teacher, and he said something nobody else thought of. The state university has a summer program with a coastal lab, so she could maybe get both. Every opinion changed the choice. Dana's story shows why asking more than one person is better than asking only one.

The first reason is that every person sees a problem from a different spot. Dana's parents were worried about debt because they had a terrible time paying off loans when they were young. Her friend cared about happiness because she knew how much Dana loved talking about the ocean. Nobody was wrong, but each one only saw a small part. If you ask just one person you get one piece and you might think it is the whole thing. When you ask a few people the pieces start to fit together, and you can tell which worries are big and which ones are just one person's fear.

The second reason is that people know different facts. Advice is not just opinion, it is information too. Dana's teacher knew about the lab because he sent two old students there. Her parents had no clue it was real, and her friend never looked it up. If Dana quit after the first talk she would have missed something huge. It is like buying a used car. A friend who loves driving says it feels so smooth and fun, but a mechanic sees ugly rust under the frame. A buyer who hears both is way less likely to get a bad surprise later, and that saves a lot of money and pain.

The third reason is that many opinions protect you from bias. Everybody giving advice wants something, even if it is little. Dana's parents wanted her close because they would miss her. Her friend was leaving town too, so she liked the idea of Dana being brave like her. That is natural and not mean, but it pushes the advice one way. When people disagree the pushes cancel out and you get a better answer. Doctors know this. That is why people with a scary diagnosis are told to get a second opinion. The second doctor does not have to protect the first one, and if they agree the patient can feel really safe going ahead.

Last, hearing different views helps you understand yourself. When Dana listened to her parents she felt angry and defensive, and that told her something. When she heard her friend she felt happy and relieved. Those feelings showed her what she truly wanted. Disagreement made her explain her reasons out loud, and she sorted out what mattered most to her. One adviser is easy to just obey or ignore. A group makes you do the hard work of thinking, and that work is worth it.

I have seen this in my own life too. Last year I could not decide whether to join band or the soccer team, and I was so stressed that I felt sick. My mom said band, my coach said soccer, and my older brother asked me which one I would miss more if I quit. That question was the best one, and I never would have heard it if I only talked to one person. I joined both and I love it.

None of this means more is always better. If you ask twenty people you get a loud messy pile of answers and it can be stressful and confusing. It is smart to pick people who know the topic or know you well. In the end the choice is yours because you are the one who has to live with it. But three or four different voices give you facts you were missing, views you did not see, and a shield against bias.

Dana went to the state university and joined the coastal lab the next summer. She says she is so glad she asked around, because she would have chosen worse if she listened to only the first person. Asking did not make the choice for her. It made the choice smart, and that is what great advice should do. Now she loves her lab, her new friends there are wonderful, and she is really happy and proud of her choice. I think anyone can have that kind of good luck if they just ask more people.
