Why Several Opinions Are Better Than One

When I have a big decision to make, I always want to ask my favorite person and just do what they say. It feels easy and safe. My teacher says smart people always ask around first, and I think she is right. I think one good adviser is nice, but I really believe that asking several people gives you a much better choice.

My first reason is that everybody sees things in their own way. A good adviser still only knows their own life. Say a student is picking between two colleges. Her mom loved a big state school, so she says big campuses are great. Her cousin had a wonderful time at a small school, so he says small is best. Neither one is wrong, they just have different stories, and both stories are so interesting. Both of them are kind and smart, and both are trying to help. When the student hears both, she can see the real question, which is what place makes her happy. With one opinion she would never have asked it.

My second reason is that different people know different things. Think about a guy buying a used car. His friend loves driving, so he talks about how smooth and fun the car feels. A coworker who fixes cars for fun checks the repair history and finds a problem with the transmission. An aunt who is great with money sees that the insurance is so expensive. Each person gives one good piece of the picture. I think this is a really great example of how teamwork works. Together they make a full and helpful answer, and the buyer gets a really good deal instead of a mess.

Another good thing is that you can see how much to trust the advice. If five people say a job offer sounds too good to be true, that is a strong sign to listen. If they split, then the choice is close and your own values can pick the winner. That is not a failure. It is a helpful clue, and I love that it shows the real tradeoffs. It also feels good to know that more than one person agrees with you.

Talking to many people is also great practice for explaining yourself. Every time you tell your story you notice what matters most, and some worries just fade away. I have had happy moments when a friend asked a question I never thought of, and suddenly I understood my own plan much better. It was so nice to learn something new from a person I know well.

Of course, there are limits. If you ask too many people you can get confused and tired, and you might keep asking until someone says what you wanted to hear. The best way is to pick a few people with different lives, listen with an open mind, and then make the choice yourself. In the end, you are the one who lives with it.

So I would pick three honest voices over one sure voice any day. I know a lot of kids who just copy their best friend, and that can end badly. It is a hard thing to do, and sometimes the talking is stressful and slow, and sometimes the advice hurts. Even so, a few extra talks are worth the trouble when the choice is big.
