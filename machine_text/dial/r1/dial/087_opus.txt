I agree with the principal's decision that every student has to do at least one extracurricular activity. Some people worry it will take time away from homework or make kids do stuff they don't want to do. That is a real problem, but I still think it helps students more than it hurts them. Activities teach skills that classes can't, they connect students to the school, and they help kids figure out what they care about.

First, activities teach things you can't get from a textbook. On a soccer team you learn that showing up to practice matters even when it is freezing and nobody wants to run. If you work on the yearbook, you learn to meet deadlines because the pages have to go to the printer on a certain date no matter what. Student council members have to speak in front of a group, listen to complaints, and compromise when people disagree. Colleges and jobs look for these skills. A lot of students would never practice them if they just went to class and went home.

Second, activities make students feel like they belong somewhere. A big school can be really lonely, especially if you are new or shy. When you join the chess club or the drama crew, you have a group of people who expect to see you and notice when you are gone. My cousin moved to a new town in ninth grade and didn't know anyone. She was miserable for the first few weeks. She only joined the cross country team because a teacher pushed her, but by October she had friends to sit with at lunch. Students who feel connected to school usually keep their grades up and skip less.

Third, a rule like this makes kids try things they would never pick on their own. Lots of teenagers stay away from new things because they are nervous or think they will fail. If everyone has to pick something, a kid who thought he hated writing might end up taking photos for the school newspaper and liking it. That could change what he studies later.

Still, the rule has problems if it is too strict. Some students have jobs or have to babysit their siblings after school, and forcing them into a club would be unfair. There also need to be enough choices so nobody gets stuck in something they dread. If the school fixes those issues, then I think the principal made the right call.
