Why Asking More Than One Person Helps

When people have a hard decision to make, they often ask for advice. Maybe they want to know which high school to choose, whether to join the school play, or if they should buy a used bike or save up for a new one. Asking for advice is a good idea, but talking to only one person can be risky. Seeking multiple opinions can help someone make a better choice because each person sees the problem differently.

One reason multiple opinions are helpful is that every person has different experiences. Let's say a student named Maya wants to try out for the travel soccer team, but she is worried it will take too much time away from her homework. If she only asks her best friend, her friend might say, "Go for it, it will be so much fun!" That is nice to hear, but her friend is not the one who has to stay up late finishing math. Maya's older cousin, who played in high school, might say that she can manage practice if she plans her homework ahead of time. Her coach might tell her how many hours the team really practices each week. With three different points of view, Maya can see the whole picture instead of just the fun part or just the hard part.

Another reason is that one person can be biased without meaning to be. A friend may want to keep you close, so they might tell you to stay in the same class even if a new class would be better for you. A parent may worry too much and say no to everything new. These people love you, but their feelings can change their advice. When you ask several people, you can notice when one answer seems to come from worry or from wanting something for themselves. If most people agree on one choice, you have a stronger reason to believe it is right.

Multiple opinions also help when the answers are different. Imagine a student named Jared who is deciding between two summer programs. One is a science camp, and the other is a job at the public library. His science teacher says the camp will help him get into a good high school. His librarian says the job will teach him responsibility and how to talk to adults. His dad says to pick whichever one he will enjoy more. Jared may not get one clear answer, but he gets a list of things to think about. He can then compare the good parts and the costs of each choice and decide what matters most to him. Hearing different opinions makes him think harder, and that usually leads to a better decision.

Some people think that asking many people is confusing or that it takes too long. It is true that too many opinions can be overwhelming. However, a person does not have to ask everyone they know. Picking three or four people who know different things is usually enough. For example, if you want to pick a new phone, you could ask someone who knows about technology, someone who has the same budget as you, and someone who has used the phone you are thinking about. Each of them can give you a useful piece of information in just a few minutes.

In the end, the person asking for advice is still the one who has to make the choice. Other people can share their ideas, but they cannot live your life for you. Getting multiple opinions helps you learn more about your options, notice your own biases, and feel more confident about what you decide. A good decision is not one that everyone agrees with. It is one that you understand well because you listened to more than one voice before choosing.
