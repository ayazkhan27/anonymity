Have you ever had a hard decision to make and didn't know what to do? Most people have. When that happens, a lot of people ask someone for advice. But asking only one person is not always enough. I think seeking multiple opinions helps someone make a better choice because different people know different things, one person can be wrong or biased, and hearing more ideas helps you figure out what you really want.

First, different people have different experiences. No one person knows everything. For example, last year I was trying to decide if I should join the school band or try out for the soccer team, because both of them had practice at the same time. I asked my older cousin, who played trumpet in band for four years, and he told me about the concerts and the trips they got to go on. Then I asked my friend Marcus who plays soccer, and he told me how much running there is and that games are on Saturdays. If I only asked my cousin, I would only know about band. Since I asked both, I had more information about each choice and could compare them.

Second, one person can be wrong or have their own reasons for saying something. People give advice based on what they like, not always whats best for you. My cousin loves band, so of course he said to pick band. Marcus wanted me on his team so he said soccer. Neither of them were lying, but they both were a little bit biased. When you ask more people, you can notice when someone is just telling you what they would do. I also asked my mom, and she didn't care which one I picked. She just asked me which one I would still want to do in the winter when its cold and I'm tired. That was a question nobody else thought of.

Third, hearing a lot of opinions helps you understand your own feelings. Sometimes when a person gives you advice, you feel happy about it or you feel kind of dissapointed. That feeling tells you something. When my cousin talked about band I was interested, but when Marcus talked about the games I got excited and started asking a bunch of questions. I realized I already wanted to play soccer, I just needed to hear it out loud from other people to know. If I had only talked to one person, I might not have noticed that.

Some people might say that asking too many people is confusing because everybody says something different. That can be true. But you don't have to do what everyone says. You just listen, think about it, and then make the choice yourself. The opinions are like tools, and you are still the one building.

In conclusion, seeking multiple opinions helps people make better choices. You get more information, you can tell when someone is biased, and you learn what you really want. So next time you have a big decision, don't just ask one person. Ask a few, and then decide.
