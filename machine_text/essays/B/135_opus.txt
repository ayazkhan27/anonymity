Last year I had to decide if I wanted to try out for the soccer team or join the school band. I couldn't do both because practices were at the same time. At first I just asked my best friend, and he said soccer because he was on the team. But then I realized he might just want me there with him. That's when I figured out that asking more than one person is a smart idea. Seeking multiple opinions can help someone make a better choice because different people see things differently, they have different experiences, and it helps you notice if someone is being biased.

First, different people look at a problem in different ways. When I asked my mom about soccer or band, she didn't talk about which one was more fun. She talked about my grades and how much time each one would take. My band teacher told me that learning an instrument helps your brain and that I could keep playing for the rest of my life. My best friend only cared about having fun on the field. Each person gave me a piece of the picture. If I had only listened to one of them, I would have missed a lot of important stuff.

Second, people have different experiences that can help you. My older cousin played soccer all through middle school and high school. She told me that the season gets really tiring and that she hurt her ankle twice. She still loved it, but she wanted me to know the hard parts too. My neighbor plays the trumpet and he told me that band is fun but you have to practice at home every single day or you fall behind. Hearing from people who actually did these things gave me real information instead of just guessing what it would be like.

Third, asking more than one person helps you spot bias. Like I said before, my best friend wanted me on the team because he would have someone to hang out with. That isn't a bad reason, but it's about him, not me. When you only ask one person, you might not notice that their advice is really about what they want. When you ask a bunch of people, you can compare their answers and figure out which advice is actually about you.

In the end I chose band. It wasn't because one person told me to, it was because after talking to everybody I realized I cared more about music and I liked that I could do it forever. My friend was a little disappointed but he got over it. Asking for lots of opinions doesn't mean you let other people decide for you. It just means you have more information so you can make the choice that is actually right for you. Next time I have a big decision, I'm going to ask more than one person again.
