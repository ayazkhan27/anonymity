Almost everybody I know has a phone, and most of them can't go ten minutes without looking at it. That is fine when you are sitting on the couch, but it is a totally different thing when you are driving a car that weighs two tons. I think drivers should not be able to use their cell phones while operating a vehicle, except for a few small things like GPS that is set up before the car moves.

The biggest reason is safety. When you look down at a text, your eyes are off the road for about five seconds. That does not sound like a lot, but if you are going 55 miles per hour you basically drive the length of a football field without looking. A kid could run into the street or the car in front of you could slam on its brakes, and you would never see it. My cousin rear ended somebody last year because she was changing a song on her phone. Nobody got hurt, but her insurance went up a lot and she was really shaken up about it.

Some people say that they are good at multitasking and can text and drive just fine. I don't believe that. Your brain can only really focus on one thing at a time, and driving already takes a lot of attention. Even talking on the phone takes your mind away from the road, because you are thinking about the conversation instead of the stop sign coming up. People who say they are good at it just haven't crashed yet.

Another reason is that it isn't only the driver who gets hurt. If I decide to text while driving, I am putting my passengers, other drivers, and people walking on the sidewalk in danger too, and they didn't get a choice. That isn't fair to them.

I do think there should be some exceptions. Using a map that talks to you is helpful and actually keeps you from getting lost and driving badly. Also, if there is an emergency, a person should be able to call 911. But those are different from scrolling or texting your friends.

In conclusion, phones are a big part of our lives, but no text is worth somebody's life. Drivers should put the phone down and wait until they are parked.
