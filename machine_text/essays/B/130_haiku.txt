Why Talking to More Than One Person Helps

When people need advice, they sometimes ask more than one person. This is a smart thing to do because every person has a different point of view. One person might know a lot about the subject, while another person might notice something that the first person missed. When I put all of the advice together, I can make a better choice than I could make by listening to just one voice.

One reason multiple opinions help is that each person has different experiences. For example, last year I wanted to join a soccer team, but I was not sure if I should pick the travel team or the rec league. My coach told me that the travel team would help me get better fast, but it would take a lot of time on weekends. My older cousin, who played in high school, said that the rec league was more fun and that I would still learn a lot there. My mom looked at my schedule and said that I already had a lot of homework and that the rec league would be easier to manage. Each person helped me see a different part of the choice. The coach taught me about skills, my cousin told me about enjoying the game, and my mom reminded me about my time. Without all three, I would have only thought about one side.

Another reason is that different people can catch mistakes. Sometimes a person is so excited about an idea that they forget about the problems. When I wanted to buy a new video game with my savings, I only thought about how fun it would be. My friend Marcus asked me how much money I had left after I bought it, and I realized I would not have enough for the school trip. My dad also asked whether I had already played the game I was thinking about, which showed me that I had enough games already. Because I asked two people, I did not spend my money on something I would regret.

Multiple opinions can also help when the choice is very important. When my class had to pick a topic for the science fair, I asked my teacher, my partner, and my neighbor who works as a nurse. My teacher suggested a project about plants because it had clear steps. My partner liked an idea about the ocean, but it seemed hard to test at home. My neighbor said that a project about sleep would be useful because it would help people, and she gave me some ideas about how to measure it. In the end, I chose a project about how light affects plant growth. It used the teacher's clear steps, it was easy to test, and it had a real purpose like my neighbor wanted. I would not have found this mix of ideas if I had only asked one person.

Of course, asking many people can also be confusing. Sometimes the opinions do not match, and it can be hard to know which one to trust. To handle this, I make a list of the reasons each person gave and then I think about which reasons are strongest. I also think about which person knows the most about the subject. Even when it is hard, having different views is still better than having only one, because I can make my own decision with more information.

In conclusion, seeking multiple opinions can help someone make a better choice because each person brings different experiences, can catch mistakes, and can offer ideas that one person might not think of. When I ask for advice, I try to talk to a few people I trust, listen carefully to what they say, and then decide for myself. This helps me feel more confident that I have considered all of the important parts of my choice.
