Every June, teachers hand out a packet right before the last bell, and every August most of us are finishing it the night before school starts. If schools really want students to keep learning over the summer, I think the projects should be designed by the students, not the teachers. Students work harder on something they picked, and they actually remember it afterward.

The first reason is motivation. When a teacher assigns a project, it feels like homework that followed you home for two months. Last summer my English class had to read a certain novel and fill out a chart for each chapter. I did it, but I did it in three days at the end of August and I could not tell you much about that book now. On the other hand, my cousin's school let students choose their own project, and he decided to rebuild a lawn mower engine with my uncle. He had to read the manual, watch videos, and write about what went wrong. He worked on it for weeks because he wanted the thing to run, not because of a grade.

Another reason is that summers are not the same for everybody. Some students have jobs, some watch their little brothers and sisters all day, and some travel to see family in another state or country. A teacher designed project assumes everyone has the same time and the same supplies. A student who works at a grocery store forty hours a week could design a project about how the store orders food and how much gets thrown away. That is still math and writing, but it fits into the life they already have.

Some people would say that students will just pick something easy, or that they won't cover what the teacher needs them to know. That is a fair point, and I don't think students should have zero rules. The teacher can give a list of requirements, like the project has to include research, a written part, and a presentation. The teacher could also approve the idea before summer starts. That way the student designs it, but nobody turns in a poster about their favorite video game with no real work behind it.

In the end, the point of a summer project is to keep learning, not to keep students busy. People learn more when they care about what they are doing. Schools should let students design their own projects and let teachers guide them instead of control them.
