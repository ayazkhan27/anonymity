Drivers Should Not Use Cell Phones Behind the Wheel

Almost everyone today has a cell phone, and most people use theirs constantly. Phones are great for talking to friends, getting directions, and listening to music. But a car is not the place for them. Drivers should not be allowed to use cell phones in any capacity while operating a vehicle, because phones take attention away from the road, because even "hands-free" use is risky, and because the cost of a mistake is a human life.

The biggest problem is distraction. Driving takes a lot more focus than people think. A driver has to watch the cars around them, check mirrors, read signs, and be ready to stop suddenly. When someone looks down to read a text, their eyes are off the road for about five seconds. At highway speed, that is like driving the length of a football field with your eyes closed. A lot can happen in that time. A kid could run into the street, or the car ahead could slam on its brakes. By the time the driver looks up, it may be too late.

Some people argue that using a phone is fine as long as it is hands-free. They say that talking on speaker or using voice commands keeps both hands on the wheel. However, the problem is not only where your hands are. It is where your mind is. Studies have shown that talking on the phone, even hands-free, makes drivers slower to react and more likely to miss things right in front of them. The brain cannot fully focus on a conversation and on driving at the same time. It just switches back and forth, and that gap can cause a crash. This is why a total ban makes more sense than a partial one. If only some uses are banned, drivers will keep making excuses for the ones that are still allowed, and the rules will be hard for police to enforce.

Another argument is that phones are needed for emergencies or for navigation. That is a fair point, but it does not mean drivers need to use their phones while the car is moving. A person can pull over to a safe spot to make an emergency call or to type in an address. Setting up the GPS before leaving the driveway only takes a minute. If a call or message is not an emergency, it can wait. Nothing that shows up on a screen is more important than getting home safely.

Finally, we should think about who gets hurt. When a driver chooses to use a phone, they are not only risking themselves. They are risking passengers, other drivers, cyclists, and people walking on the sidewalk. Thousands of people die every year in crashes involving distracted drivers, and many of them did nothing wrong. Those deaths could have been prevented. Families lose loved ones because someone wanted to answer a text. Seatbelt laws and drunk driving laws were once argued over too, but now most people agree they save lives. Cell phone laws should be treated the same way.

In conclusion, drivers should not be able to use cell phones while driving in any way. Phones are a distraction, hands-free devices do not fix the problem, and the risks are too high for everyone on the road. A short text or call is never worth a life. If people would just wait until they are parked, the roads would be safer for all of us.
