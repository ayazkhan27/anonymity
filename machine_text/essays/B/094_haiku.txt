Every Student Should Join Something

My principal has decided that every student must take part in at least one extracurricular activity. Some people think this is unfair because students should be free to choose what they do after school. I disagree. I think this rule is a good idea because school is not only about classes and tests. It is also about finding out who you are and who you want to be, and extracurricular activities are one of the best ways to do that.

First, activities help students make friends. In a regular classroom, you sit in the same seat with the same people, and you might never talk to the kids on the other side of the room. But when you join a team or a club, you spend time with people who share your interests. For example, my cousin did not know anyone in her grade when she started middle school. She was quiet and nervous. Then she joined the yearbook club, and within two months she knew almost everyone who worked on it. Now she has friends she would never have met otherwise. Friends like that make school feel like a place where you belong.

Second, activities teach skills that are hard to learn from a book. On a sports team, you learn how to work with others even when you do not like them, and you learn that you have to show up on time and do your part. In student council, you learn how to speak in front of people and how to listen to ideas that are different from yours. These skills matter in high school, in a job, and in life. A student who has never been part of a group might struggle the first time he has to work on a project with people he does not know.

Third, activities can help students who are struggling. Some kids do not feel good at math or reading, but they might be great at drawing, building things, or playing an instrument. If they never get the chance to try something else, they may think they are not good at anything. When my neighbor was in seventh grade, his grades were low and he did not seem happy. The school made him join the robotics club. At first he complained, but after a few weeks he was building machines and talking about them all the time. His grades started to improve too, because he finally had a reason to care about school.

Some people might argue that students who are already busy with jobs, family duties, or other responsibilities should not be forced to join something. That is a fair point. However, the principal's rule does not say students have to join a certain activity or spend a huge amount of time on it. Students can pick something that fits their schedule, like a club that meets once a week. A short commitment is still a chance to learn something new. Also, if a student truly cannot manage it, the school can work with that student to find an option that makes sense.

Another argument is that students should be allowed to choose freely because forcing them makes it less fun. I understand this idea, but most students do not know what they will enjoy until they try it. Many people who love a hobby today only found it because someone encouraged them to give it a chance. Choosing is still part of the rule, because students get to pick which activity they join. The rule only makes sure they do not skip the experience completely.

For these reasons, I agree with the principal. Extracurricular activities help students make friends, build real skills, and discover talents they did not know they had. Every student deserves those benefits, and a small push to try something new can lead to a much better school year. Schools exist to help students grow, and joining one activity is one simple way to grow.
