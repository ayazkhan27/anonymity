Dear Principal,

I have heard that the school is thinking about a new rule that would stop students from playing sports or doing other activities unless they have at least a B average. I think this is a bad idea, and I want to tell you why. I know grades are important, but I do not think sports and clubs should be taken away because of them.

First, sports and activities help students with their grades. When I am on the basketball team, I have to go to practice after school, and I have to keep track of my time. I learned how to plan my homework so I can finish it before practice. Without the team, I think I would just come home and watch TV all afternoon. Many students in my class say the same thing. Activities give us a reason to stay busy and focused on school.

Second, a lot of students who have a C average would lose the chance to be on a team. Some of them are not doing well in math or reading, but they are really good at soccer or drama or the school band. If we take away their activities, they will have even less reason to come to school. The students who need help the most would be the ones kicked off the team. That does not seem fair. Instead of taking away sports, the school could give those students extra tutoring or a homework club so they can get their grades up.

Third, activities help students make friends and learn how to work with other people. In a team, you have to listen to your coach and your teammates, and you have to help each other when someone is having a bad day. These are skills that matter for the rest of your life. Many students get to know kids from other grades through clubs, and that makes the school feel more like a community. If students cannot join anything, they might feel left out and lonely.

Some people might say that students should focus on school first, and that a C is not good enough. I understand that point. Grades really do matter, and I want my classmates to do well. But a grade is only one way to measure a student. Some kids are better at hands-on things, like building a robot in science club or painting a set for the school play. Those skills are also important, and the school should value them too.

Finally, I think there is a better way to help students who are struggling. The school could set up a study period during the day, or it could ask teachers to check in with students who are falling behind. The school could also make a rule that students who are failing a class have to get help before they can play in a game. This would still push students to work hard, but it would not punish them by taking away the things they love.

For these reasons, I think the school should not require a B average to join sports or other activities. Students who are struggling need support, not punishment. I hope you will think about our views and find a way to help every student succeed, both in the classroom and outside of it.

Thank you for reading my letter. I would be happy to talk with you or with the school board about this idea.

Sincerely,

A Concerned Eighth Grade Student
