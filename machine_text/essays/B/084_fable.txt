Our principal has decided that every student has to be in at least one extracurricular activity. A lot of kids in my class are mad about it, but I actually agree with the decision. I think making students join one activity is a good idea because it helps us make friends, it teaches us things we don't learn in class, and it gives us something better to do after school.

First of all, activities help you meet people. In sixth grade I didn't really know anybody because I came from a different elementary school than most of the kids. I ate lunch with the same two people every day. Then my mom made me sign up for cross country, and I didn't want to at all. But after a couple weeks of running with the team, I knew like fifteen new people, and some of them are still my best friends now. If nobody pushed me I would of never done it. Some students are shy and they need that push, and the principal's rule gives it to them.

Second, you learn stuff in activities that you can't get from a textbook. On student council you have to plan events and talk in front of people. On yearbook you learn how to take pictures and meet a deadline. In sports you learn how to lose, which is honestly harder than it sounds. My cousin was on the robotics team and now he wants to be an engineer, and he said he never would have known that without the club. These are skills that help you in high school and in jobs later.

Another reason is that it keeps kids busy. A lot of students just go home and play video games or scroll on their phone for four hours. I know because I do it too sometimes. If you have practice or a meeting, you are doing something active and your with other people instead of sitting alone in your room.

Some people say this rule isn't fair because students already have too much homework, or they have to babysit their little brothers and sisters. I understand that, and I think the school should help with it. But the rule only says one activity. Some clubs only meet once a week during lunch, so it doesn't have to take up your whole life. There are enough choices that everybody can find something that fits.

In conclusion, I agree with the principal. One activity is not that much to ask, and it could change how a student feels about school. It did for me. I think students should give it a chance before they complain.
