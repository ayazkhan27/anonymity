Almost everybody I know has a cell phone, and most of us have it in our hand or our pocket all day long. Phones are useful for a lot of things, but I do not think the driver's seat is the place for them. In my opinion, drivers should not be able to use their phones while they are operating a vehicle, except for a few limited situations like hands free directions or an emergency call. Driving already takes all of your attention, and a phone takes it away.

The first reason is that texting and driving is dangerous. When you look down at a text, your eyes are off the road for a few seconds. That does not sound like very much, but a car going sixty miles an hour covers a lot of ground in a few seconds. A kid could run out into the street, or the car in front of you could slam on its brakes, and you would not even see it happen. We learned in drivers ed that distracted driving causes thousands of crashes every year, and a lot of them involve teenagers. I believe it, because I have been in the car with friends who text while they drive, and the car always starts drifting toward the other lane before they notice.

Another reason is that the driver is not the only person who gets hurt. If somebody decides to answer a message and crashes, they could hit another car with a whole family in it, or somebody walking on the sidewalk. Those people did not make the choice to look at a phone. It is not fair for them to pay for somebody else's bad decision. People say "it's my car and my life," but the road is shared by everyone, so the rules should protect everyone.

Some people argue that they are good at multitasking and can handle it. I don't think that is true for most people. My older cousin used to say the same thing, and then he rear ended a truck at a stop light because he was changing a song. Nobody got hurt, thankfully, but his insurance went up and he had to pay for the damage. He was only going about fifteen miles an hour. If that can happen at a stop light, imagine what happens on the highway. Even talking on the phone while holding it means you only have one hand on the wheel and half your brain on the conversation.

However, I do not think phones need to be banned completely in every capacity. A lot of people use their phone for GPS, and getting lost can also be dangerous because you are confused and making last minute turns. If the phone is in a mount and the directions are set up before the car moves, that seems fine to me. The same goes for hands free calls and for calling 911 if there is an emergency. The difference is that these things do not make you look away from the road or take your hands off the wheel for very long.

In conclusion, drivers should not be allowed to text, scroll, or hold their phones while driving. The risk is too big for the driver and for everybody around them, and no message is so important that it can't wait until you park. Phones should only be used in a car when they are hands free or when it is a real emergency. If more people followed that rule, I think the roads would be a lot safer for all of us.
