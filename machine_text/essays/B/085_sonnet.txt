I agree with the principal's decision that every student should have to join at least one extracurricular activity. Some kids might think it is unfair to be forced, but I think it would help students a lot, and I have a few reasons why.

First of all, extracurricular activities help students make friends. At the beginning of the year in my school, a lot of kids stick with the same small group and never talk to anyone else. If you are in the yearbook club or on a sports team, you have to work with people you may not know. Last year I joined the track team, and I ended up becoming friends with kids from other grades that I would never have met in class. Being forced to join something might make a shy person step out of their comfort zone, and they could end up really glad about it.

Another reason is that these activities teach skills that you can't always learn in a regular classroom. For example, serving on the student council teaches you how to speak in front of people and how to work out problems as a group. Working on the yearbook teaches you about deadlines, taking photos, and writing captions. Sports teach teamwork and how to handle losing without giving up. These skills will help students later in high school and when they get a job. Employers and colleges also like to see that a person did more than just go to class.

Some people say that students already have too much homework and don't have time for another thing. I understand this, because some nights I have a lot of work too. However, extracurriculars can actually help students manage their time better. When I have track practice after school, I know I have to get my homework done fast, so I don't waste time on my phone. Kids who have nothing to do after school often just go home and play video games or watch videos for hours. Having somewhere to be keeps them busy in a good way. Also, the principal said at least one activity, so a student can pick something small that only meets once a week.

Another argument against the rule is that students should get to choose for themselves. But the rule still lets them choose, because there are so many different options. A student who hates sports can do the yearbook, and a student who likes art can join the art club. There is something for everybody, so nobody has to do something they really can't stand.

In conclusion, I agree with the principal. Extracurricular activities help students make friends, learn new skills, and use their time wisely. Even if some students complain at first, I believe most of them will be happy they joined. School should be about more than just classes and tests, and this rule gives every student a chance to be part of something.
