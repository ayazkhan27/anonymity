Last winter my cousin Dana missed almost three weeks of school because she had mono. When she came back she was so far behind in chemistry that she ended up dropping to the regular level class, even though she had an A before she got sick. If her school had let her log in to her classes from home, I think that whole situation could have been avoided. For reasons like this, I believe students would benefit from having the option to attend classes from home. It would help students who can't physically be at school, it would teach responsibility, and it would give students more control over how they learn.

The first and most obvious reason is that some students just can't make it to the building every day. Kids get sick, they break legs, they have family emergencies. Right now when that happens you basically get a packet of worksheets and are told to figure it out. A worksheet is not the same as hearing your teacher explain something. In math especially, if you miss the day where the teacher shows how to do a new kind of problem, the homework looks like another language. With distance learning a student who is stuck at home could still watch the lesson, ask a question, and stay with the class. This also helps students who live far away. In my district some kids ride the bus for over an hour each way. When the roads are icy the school either closes for everybody or those kids miss the day. An online option would fix that.

Second, attending class from home would teach students to be responsible for themselves. At school there is always somebody telling you where to be. A bell rings, a teacher says to take out your notebook, and you do it. At home nobody is standing over you. You have to get yourself up, log in on time, and actually pay attention when your phone is sitting right there. Some people will say that is exactly the problem, and that students will just sleep through class or play video games. I agree that some would. But those same students are the ones who sit in the back of a real classroom with there heads down anyway. For everybody else it is good practice for what comes after high school. College students have to manage their own time, and a lot of jobs now are done from home too. My dad works from our kitchen table three days a week, and he says the hardest part was learning to stay focused without a boss around. I would rather learn that at sixteen then at twenty five.

Third, distance learning lets students learn in a way that fits them. Not everyone does well in a room with thirty other people. I have a friend who has bad anxiety, and she says half of her energy at school goes to worrying about who is looking at her instead of the lesson. At home she could concentrate on the work. Also, a lot of online classes are recorded, so if you don't understand something the first time you can go back and watch it again. In a regular class once the teacher says it, it's gone, and many students are too embarrassed to ask them to repeat it. Being able to pause and rewind a lesson would have saved me in geometry last year.

Of course, there are downsides. Students at home miss out on seeing their friends, and things like labs, band, and sports can't really be done over a video call. Not every family has good internet or a quiet place to work either. Those are real problems, and that is why I think distance learning should be an option and not a requirement. Schools could loan out laptops and hotspots like some already do, and students could still come in for the activities that need to be in person. Nobody is saying to close the buildings.

In conclusion, giving students the choice to attend class from home would help more than it would hurt. It keeps sick and far away students from falling behind, it builds responsibility that we will need later, and it lets people learn in the way that works best for them. School is supposed to be about learning, not just about sitting in a certain chair. If a student can learn just as well from home, the school should let them.
