Dear Principal,

I think Policy 1 is the better choice. Students should be allowed to bring their phones to school and use them during lunch and other free times, as long as the phones are turned off during class. Here are my reasons.

First, phones can be useful in an emergency. If a student gets sick, has an accident, or needs to call a parent after school plans change, a phone lets them reach help quickly. If phones are banned completely, students have to ask the office for permission to call home, and that can take a long time. Parents also feel better knowing their kids can call or text them during the day. This matters a lot in a big school where it is easy to get lost.

Second, students need a break from class. Lunch and free time are when students relax, talk with friends, and recharge so they can focus when class starts again. Many students use their phones during those times to listen to music, check messages from family, or look up a fact they were curious about. Taking that away would make the school day feel longer and more stressful. Students who feel relaxed usually pay better attention in class.

Third, phones can help students learn outside of class. Some students use their phones to check a bus schedule, read a news story, or look up a word they do not know. These are small things, but they show that phones can be tools as well as distractions. If students are trained to use them responsibly, they learn an important skill they will need later in life.

Some people say phones will be a distraction even at lunch, and that students will sneak them into class. I understand that worry. However, Policy 1 already requires phones to be turned off during class time, so the rule is clear. If a student breaks the rule, the school can take the phone for the day and call the parent, which is a fair consequence. A complete ban is not needed to stop the problem. Most students will follow a clear rule if they are trusted with it.

Policy 2 might seem simpler, but it takes away useful tools and does not teach responsibility. Students who never have phones at school will not learn how to balance them with their other work. Policy 1 treats students like they can make good choices, and I think they can.

For these reasons, I believe Policy 1 is the better policy for our school. Thank you for considering my letter.

Sincerely,
A Student
