Distance Learning: Helpful, But Not for Everyone

Ever since the pandemic, a lot of people have had an opinion about online school. Some students loved it and some students hated it. Now some schools offer distance learning as a regular option, where students can take classes from home through video calls. I think students would benefit from having this option, as long as it stays an option and not something everyone is forced into.

The first reason is that distance learning helps students who can't always make it to school. Some students have health problems, like chronic illnesses, that make them miss weeks of class at a time. Others have to take care of younger siblings or work to help their families. When I was in ninth grade, a girl in my English class had surgery and was out for almost a month. She fell so far behind that she had to retake the class over the summer. If she could have logged in from home, she could have kept up with the lessons and not lost all that progress. For students like her, distance learning isn't a lazy shortcut, it's the only way they can stay on track.

Another reason is that some students actually learn better at home. School can be loud and distracting. There are people talking during work time, phones going off, and drama in the hallways. Some students, especially ones with anxiety, have a hard time focusing when they're stressed about the people around them. At home, they can work in a quiet place and go at their own pace. My cousin did online school for his junior year and said his grades went up because he could rewatch recorded lessons when he didn't understand something the first time. You can't rewind a teacher in a normal classroom.

Distance learning also teaches responsibility and time management. When you're at home, nobody is standing over you making sure you do your work. You have to log in on time, turn in assignments, and keep yourself motivated. These are skills that students will need in college and in jobs, since a lot of companies let people work remotely now. Learning how to handle that in high school could make the adjustment easier later.

Of course, there are some real problems with distance learning. Not every student has good internet or a quiet space at home. Some students also need the social part of school, like seeing friends, joining clubs, and playing sports. And some kids would just sleep through class with their camera off. These are fair points, but they are reasons to keep distance learning optional, not reasons to get rid of it. Students who do better in person can still go to school, and schools could lend laptops or hotspots to students who need them.

In conclusion, I believe students would benefit from being able to attend classes from home. It gives sick or busy students a way to keep up, helps students who focus better alone, and builds skills they will use in the future. School isn't one size fits all, and giving students more choices about how they learn just makes sense.
