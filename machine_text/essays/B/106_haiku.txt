Today most people have a cell phone, and many of us use them every day. Because of this, some people think drivers should be allowed to use their phones in any way while they drive. I disagree. Drivers should not be able to use cell phones in any capacity while operating a vehicle.

The first reason is safety. Driving takes full attention. When a driver reads a text or looks at a map, their eyes leave the road for several seconds. At 55 miles per hour, a car travels almost the length of a football field in that time. A small distraction can cause a big accident, and people can get hurt or killed.

Some people say that hands-free phones are safe because the driver does not have to hold the phone. However, studies have shown that talking on the phone, even hands-free, makes it harder for the brain to focus on driving. The driver may miss a stop sign or a child running into the street because their mind is somewhere else.

Another reason is that laws already limit other dangerous behaviors. We do not allow drivers to drink alcohol or drive while very tired because those things make driving less safe. Phone use is similar. It is a choice that puts everyone on the road at risk.

Finally, some people argue that phones are needed in emergencies. That is true, but a driver can pull over safely to make a call. Pulling over takes only a minute, and it is a small price to pay for protecting lives.

For these reasons, drivers should not use cell phones in any way while driving. Safety has to come before convenience.
