Our principal has decided that every student has to join at least one extracurricular activity. A lot of kids in my grade are mad about it, and at first I was too. But after thinking about it more, I agree with the decision. I think it will help students make friends, learn new skills, and feel like they belong at school.

First of all, activities help you meet people you would never talk to otherwise. In sixth grade I only hung out with the same three kids from my elementary school. Then my mom made me join the cross country team even though I am not a fast runner at all. I came in close to last at almost every meet. But by the end of the season I knew people from every grade, and some of them are my best friends now. If nobody had pushed me, I would of stayed in my little group and never met them. Some students are shy and they are not going to sign up for something on their own. A rule like this gives them a reason to try.

Second, extracurricular activities teach things that you don't learn in regular classes. On the yearbook you learn how to take good pictures and how to finish something by a deadline. On student council you have to speak in front of people and plan events like the dance or the canned food drive. In sports you learn how to lose without throwing a fit, which is harder than it sounds. These are skills that people use in high school and in jobs. My older brother said that when he applied for his first job at the grocery store, they asked him what teams or clubs he had been on. He was glad he had something to say.

Some people argue that students are already too busy. They have homework, chores, and some have to watch their little brothers and sisters after school. I understand that, and I think it is a fair point. But the principal only said one activity, not five. There are clubs that only meet once a week, and some meet during lunch, like the chess club and the art club at our school. A student who has to go home right after the bell could pick one of those. The school should make sure there are enough choices like that so nobody gets left out or punished for something they can't control.

Other people say you can't force someone to have fun. That is true, but you also can't know if you like something until you try it. I thought running was going to be the worst thing ever, and I was wrong. Even if a student ends up not liking their activity, they can switch to a different one next semester. One semester of trying something is not that big of a deal.

Finally, I think school is better when more people are involved. When more kids are in clubs and on teams, more people show up to the games and the concerts, and the school feels more like a community instead of just a building we have to go to.

In conclusion, I agree with the principal. Making students join one activity will help them make friends, build skills, and care more about our school. It might feel annoying at first, but I think most students will be glad they did it.
