Drivers Should Not Use Cell Phones While Driving

Almost everybody has a cell phone now, and most people check theirs all day long. But a car is not a place for a phone. I believe drivers should not be able to use cell phones in any way while they are operating a vehicle, because it is dangerous, it hurts other people, and the rules are simpler when there are no exceptions.

The biggest reason is safety. Driving takes a lot of attention. You have to watch the road, the cars around you, the signs, and the people who might step out in front of you. When someone looks down to read a text, their eyes leave the road for a few seconds. At highway speed, a car can travel the length of a football field in that time. A lot can go wrong in that distance. According to the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, thousands of people die every year in crashes caused by distracted driving, and phones are one of the main causes.

Some people say that hands-free calling is okay because both hands stay on the wheel. This sounds reasonable, but the problem is not only about hands. It is about the brain. Studies have shown that talking on the phone, even with a headset, makes a driver slower to react and more likely to miss things right in front of them. Your mind is busy with the conversation instead of the road. So a hands-free call is not really much safer than holding the phone.

Another point is that drivers do not only risk themselves. A person who chooses to text while driving puts passengers, other drivers, cyclists, and walkers in danger too. Those people did not agree to take that risk. If someone wants to take a chance with their own life, that is one thing, but they should not be allowed to take a chance with everyone else's lives.

Some people argue that there are emergencies and that a phone can be useful, for example to use a map or call for help. I understand this, but the answer is simple. A driver can pull over to a safe spot and stop the car first. Nothing on a phone is so urgent that it cannot wait a couple of minutes. A missed text will still be there when the car is parked, but a person who is hurt in a crash may not get a second chance.

In conclusion, drivers should not use cell phones at all while driving. It keeps everyone safer, it protects people who did nothing wrong, and it is an easy rule to follow and enforce. If we want fewer accidents on the road, the best thing to do is put the phone away until the car is parked.
