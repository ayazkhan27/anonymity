I agree with my principal's decision that all students should have to join at least one extracurricular activity. School is not only about classes and tests. It is also about finding out who you are and what you care about, and extracurricular activities help with that.

First, being in an activity teaches responsibility. When you are on a sports team, you have to show up to practice even when you are tired or do not feel like going. If you miss too many practices, the coach might not let you play in the big game. My cousin plays on the middle school soccer team, and last year he almost got kicked off because he kept forgetting his cleats. After that, he started writing his practice times in his planner and setting an alarm on his phone. Now he is one of the most reliable players on the team. That kind of responsibility is hard to learn just from homework.

Second, activities help students make friends. Lunch can be a lonely place if you do not know anyone. But when you are in the yearbook club or on the student council, you spend time with other kids who like the same things you do. I know a girl named Maria who was really shy when she started sixth grade. She did not talk much in class. Then she joined the yearbook club and got to take photos at the school events. Now she talks to everyone and even runs a photography table at the book fair. Without that club, she might have stayed quiet all year.

Third, extracurricular activities can help students find out what they are good at. Some kids think they do not have any talents, but they just have not tried the right thing yet. A student who never thought about singing might discover a great voice in the school choir. Another student might find out that he likes building things when he joins the robotics club. If everyone is required to try something, then nobody gets left out of finding their strengths.

Some people argue that students already have enough work with homework and that adding an activity is too much pressure. I understand that concern. Some kids have jobs or have to take care of younger siblings after school. However, the principal could let students pick any activity they want, including short ones that only meet once a week. An activity does not have to take over your whole life. It just has to give you one place where you feel like you belong.

In conclusion, I think the principal made a good choice. Responsibility, friendship, and discovering new talents are all things that students need, and extracurricular activities give them all of these. Every student deserves the chance to find something they love, and this rule helps make sure that happens.
