When you have a big decision to make, it is usually smart to ask more than one person for advice. Different people know different things, so hearing several opinions can help you see the whole picture.

For example, last year I was trying to decide which elective to take, band or art. My mom said I should pick band because I already play the trumpet and it would be easy. My older cousin told me art would look better on a high school application. My friend Marcus said band was more fun because you get to go on trips. If I had only listened to one of them, I would have only seen one side of the choice.

Another reason to ask more than one person is that it keeps you from being tricked by one person's bias. Sometimes a person gives advice that is really about what they want, not what is best for you. If three people all say the same thing, you can be more sure it is good advice. If they disagree, that tells you the decision is harder and you need to think about it more.

Also, people have different experiences. A teacher might know about school stuff, while a coach knows about sports. Asking both gives you more facts to work with.

However, you still have to make the final choice yourself. Other people can only give advice, and they are not the ones who will live with the result. In the end I took band, but I also signed up for an after school art club, so I got a little of both. Asking for many opinions helped me find that answer, and I would not have thought of it otherwise.
