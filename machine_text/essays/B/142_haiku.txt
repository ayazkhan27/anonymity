I believe summer projects should be student-designed, because students learn more when they choose what they study. When a teacher assigns a project, many students just complete the steps without really thinking about the topic. But when students pick something they care about, they put in more effort and remember more.

For example, my friend Marcus wanted to learn about how cars work because he likes fixing his dad's old truck. Instead of writing a boring essay about a famous scientist, he designed a project where he rebuilt a small engine and kept a journal of what he learned. He had to research, make mistakes, and figure out solutions on his own. A teacher might not have thought of a project that fit him so well.

Some people argue that teachers know what skills students need, so teachers should design the projects. That is a fair point. However, students can still work with teachers to make sure their projects meet learning goals. The teacher can approve the plan and check the progress.

Overall, student-designed summer projects help students stay interested and build independence. Learning should not stop when school ends, and students are more likely to keep learning when the project belongs to them.
