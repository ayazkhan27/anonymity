Last winter I had the flu for almost two weeks, and when I came back to school I was so far behind in chemistry that I never really caught up that quarter. My teacher gave me a stack of worksheets and told me to ask a friend for notes. If my school had offered a way to watch class from home, I think that whole quarter would have gone differently. For this reason and a few others, I believe students would benefit from being able to attend classes from home, as long as it is an option and not the only way to go to school.

The first reason is that students miss school for things they can't control. Being sick is the obvious one, but there are others. A girl in my grade broke her leg in soccer and was out for a month after surgery. Some kids have to stay home to watch a younger brother or sister when a parent has to work. Right now, when that happens, the student just loses the day. With distance learning they could log on, hear the lesson, and even ask a question. Getting notes from a friend is not the same as hearing the teacher explain it, because half the time your friend's notes don't make sense to anybody but them.

Another reason is that not everybody learns well in a loud classroom. I know people who are smart but get really anxious at school, or who get distracted by every little thing going on around them. At home they could focus better. Also, a lot of online classes get recorded, so if you didn't understand how to do a math problem the first time, you can go back and watch that part again. In a normal class, if you miss what the teacher said, it's gone, and most people are too embarrassed to ask them to repeat it for the third time.

Distance learning can also help students who want classes their school doesn't have. My school is pretty small and we only have two languages and not many AP classes. My cousin goes to a school that lets students take a class online through another school, and she is taking Japanese that way. She would never have gotten that chance otherwise. It also saves time. Some students at my school ride the bus for over an hour each way, which is two hours a day they could use for homework or sleep.

Of course, some people say that students at home will just lay in bed with the camera off and play on their phones. Honestly, some will. I'm not going to pretend every teenager is responsible. Students also need to see their friends and learn how to work with other people, and you can't really do a lab or play in the band through a screen. But these are reasons not to make everybody learn from home. They are not reasons to take the choice away from the students who need it. A school could also set rules, like you have to keep your grades up to stay in the online option.

In conclusion, I think letting students attend class from home would help a lot of them, especially the ones who are sick, have family responsibilities, or want a class their school can't offer. It won't be right for every student, and it shouldn't replace regular school. But having the option is better than falling behind over something that wasn't even your fault.
