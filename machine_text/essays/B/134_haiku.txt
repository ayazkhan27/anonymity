Why Talking to More Than One Person Helps

When I have a big decision to make, I like to ask more than one person for advice. Sometimes people think that asking lots of people is confusing, but I think it helps me make a better choice because I get to hear different ideas and think about things I might have missed.

Last year, I had to decide whether to join the school band or the soccer team in the spring. I already knew I liked both. First, I asked my mom. She said I should pick the activity that I would be happiest doing every day, and she reminded me that I always come home smiling after practice. That was helpful, but she did not know much about how the band rehearsals worked. Next, I asked my older cousin, who played trumpet in high school. He told me that band takes a lot of time after school on Tuesdays and Thursdays, and that the concerts are in the evening. He also said that the friends you make in band stick with you for a long time. Last, I asked my best friend, Marcus, who was on the soccer team. He told me that the coach was really fun and that we would be playing in a tournament in May. He also said I could still practice piano at home, which was something I had not thought about.

After I talked to all three of them, I had a clearer picture. My mom helped me think about what made me happy. My cousin showed me the real schedule and what band would be like. Marcus told me about the parts of soccer I would enjoy. If I had only asked my mom, I would have guessed I should pick soccer, because I always like being outside. But my cousin's advice showed me that band could fit into my week better than I expected.

Getting different opinions also protects you from one person's bias. My mom wants me to be healthy, so she might focus on sports. My cousin played trumpet, so he might think band is always better. Marcus is my friend, so he might want me on the team with him. When I hear all of these views, I can tell which parts are true for me and which parts are just one person's favorite thing.

Finally, talking to several people shows me that I do not have to decide alone. I still make the final choice, but I feel more confident because I have thought about it from many sides. In the end, I picked band, and I am glad I asked for help from more than one person.

Asking more than one person for advice is like looking at a map from several directions. Each person can point out a different road, and together they help you find the best way to go.
