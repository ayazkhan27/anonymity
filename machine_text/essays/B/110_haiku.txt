Today most people carry a cell phone everywhere they go. We use them to text friends, check the weather, look at maps, and listen to music. Because phones are so useful and so common, many people think it should be fine to use them while driving. However, drivers should not be able to use cell phones in any way while operating a vehicle, because phones take attention away from the road and make crashes more likely.

The most important reason is that driving requires full attention. When a person drives, they need to watch the road, check mirrors, keep an eye on other cars, and react quickly to surprises like a child running into the street or a car stopping suddenly. Using a phone takes the driver's eyes, hands, and mind away from these jobs. Even a short glance down at a screen can mean a car travels the length of a football field without anyone watching the road. At highway speeds, that distance is more than enough for a serious accident to happen.

Some people argue that hands-free phones are safe because the driver never has to touch the device. Talking on a hands-free phone, however, still distracts the brain. Researchers have found that when people are having a conversation, their attention shifts away from their surroundings. They may fail to notice a red light or a pedestrian at the crosswalk. Hands-free laws do not solve this problem, because the danger comes from the mind being somewhere else, not only from the hands being busy. A driver who is deep in a conversation may not realize how little they remember about the last few miles they drove.

Texting is even more dangerous than talking. When someone types a message, they must look at the screen, move their eyes back and forth between the phone and the road, and think about what they want to write. This means their eyes are off the road for several seconds at a time. Many teenagers have told reporters that they think they can text safely, but studies show that new drivers are especially likely to crash when they text. Young drivers already have less experience reacting to danger, so adding a phone makes the risk even larger.

Another reason to ban phone use is that laws are easier to enforce when the rule is simple. If the law says that drivers may use phones only in certain ways, police officers have to decide whether each driver is following the rules, and this is hard to do from a moving police car. A complete ban is clear. Everyone knows what is allowed and what is not. Clear rules also help drivers remember to keep their phones away while they drive, since they do not have to decide each time which kind of phone use is legal.

Some people say that phones are needed in emergencies, and that a driver might need to call for help. This is a fair point, but there is an easy answer. A driver can pull over to a safe place and make the call. Stopping for a few minutes is a small price to pay for protecting everyone on the road. Passengers can also make calls for the driver, and many cars now have emergency systems that contact help automatically after a crash. These options show that the emergency argument does not justify using a phone while the car is moving.

Finally, people who drive are responsible for more than themselves. A car is a heavy machine that can seriously injure or kill other people, including passengers, pedestrians, cyclists, and other drivers. When someone chooses to look at a phone instead of the road, they put all of these people at risk. Most people would never agree to drive while drunk, and the same logic applies to phones, because both make a driver's reactions slower and less careful. Society has already accepted that some personal freedoms must be limited to keep everyone safe on the road.

In conclusion, cell phones are important tools in modern life, but they do not belong in a driver's hands or in a driver's attention while a car is moving. Drivers should not be able to use phones in any capacity while operating a vehicle. Focusing completely on the road is the only way to make sure that everyone gets home safely. A few minutes without a phone is a small sacrifice compared to the lives that could be saved.
