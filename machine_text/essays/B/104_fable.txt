Almost everybody I know has a phone in their pocket, and most of them check it without even thinking about it. That habit does not turn off when someone gets behind the wheel. I believe drivers should not be allowed to hold or look at their phones while the car is moving, but I do not think phones should be banned from cars completely. Hands free uses like GPS directions and emergency calls should still be allowed, because those can actually make driving safer.

The biggest reason to keep phones out of drivers' hands is that texting takes your eyes off the road. In drivers ed we were told that reading a text takes around five seconds, and at highway speed that is like driving the length of a football field with your eyes closed. A lot can happen in a football field. A kid can run out after a ball, the car in front can slam on its brakes, or a light can turn red. A driver who is looking down at a screen will not see any of it until it is too late. People like to think they are good at multitasking, but nobody is good at looking at two things at once.

It is also not only the driver who is in danger. If I decide to eat junk food every day, that mostly hurts me. If I decide to text while driving, I am risking the people in my car, the people in other cars, and anyone walking or riding a bike nearby. They did not agree to that risk. My cousin was rear ended at a stoplight two years ago by a woman who admitted she was looking at her phone. Nobody was badly hurt, but his car was totaled and he had neck pain for months. He was sitting completely still at a red light and doing everything right. That is what makes it unfair. The law is supposed to protect people from other people's bad choices, and this is a clear example of where it should.

Some people argue that adults should be able to decide for themselves, and that a quick glance at a phone is no worse than changing the radio or eating a burger. I understand that point, and it is true that there are other distractions. But a phone is different because it keeps asking for your attention. A burger does not buzz and light up with a message from your friend. Also, the fact that other distractions exist is not a good reason to allow one more, especially one that is this common.

On the other hand, I do not agree with people who say phones should not be used in any capacity. Most people my age do not own a paper map and would not know how to read one while driving anyway. A phone mounted on the dashboard that reads directions out loud lets the driver keep their eyes up instead of searching for street signs. Being able to call 911 after a crash or when you see a drunk driver is also important. Even hands free calls can be distracting, so I think they should be kept short, but they are much less dangerous than typing. The rule should be simple: set it up before you start driving, and after that do not touch it.

In conclusion, drivers should not be able to text, scroll, or hold their phones while driving, because it puts innocent people in danger for something that can almost always wait. However, hands free tools like navigation and emergency calls should stay legal since they help more than they hurt. No message is so important that it is worth somebody's life, and if it really is that important, the driver can pull over.
