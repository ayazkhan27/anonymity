Cell Phones and Driving

Almost everyone I know has a cell phone, and most people check it way more than they should. My mom, my friends, even my grandpa can't go an hour without looking at a screen. That is fine when you are sitting on the couch, but it becomes a real problem when you are behind the wheel of a car. In my opinion, drivers should not be able to use cell phones while operating a vehicle, except in a few limited ways like hands-free GPS or an emergency call.

The biggest reason is safety. When you look down at a text, your eyes leave the road for a few seconds. That might not sound like a lot, but if you are going fifty miles per hour, you can travel the length of a football field without seeing anything. A kid could run into the street or the car in front of you could slam on its brakes, and you would never know until it was too late. Texting takes your eyes, your hands, and your mind off of driving all at the same time.

Some people say they are good at multitasking and that they can text and drive just fine. I don't think that is true. Last year a senior at our school got in a crash because she was answering a Snapchat at a red light and pulled forward without looking. Nobody was seriously hurt, but her car was totaled and she was really shaken up. She thought she was being careful too. Everyone thinks they are the exception until something happens.

On the other hand, I understand that phones can be helpful in the car. A lot of people use maps to get places, and calling 911 after seeing an accident could save a life. That is why I think hands-free use should be allowed. If your phone is mounted on the dashboard and you are using voice directions, you are not really distracted. The problem is when people pick up the phone and start scrolling or typing.

In conclusion, cell phones are a huge part of our lives, but they do not belong in a driver's hands. Laws should ban texting, scrolling, and holding the phone while driving, while still allowing GPS and emergency calls. No message is so important that it is worth risking your life or someone else's. The text can wait until you park.
