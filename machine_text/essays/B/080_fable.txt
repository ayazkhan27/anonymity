Imagine getting home from school at 3:30, already tired, and then finding out you have to go back for yearbook club because the principal said so. Our principal has decided that every student must do at least one extracurricular activity. I disagree with this decision because not everyone has the time, it could cost families money, and activities are only fun when you choose them.

First, a lot of students are busy after school already. My friend Marcus has to pick up his little sister from the elementary school every day and watch her until his mom gets home at six. He can't just stay for basketball practice. Other kids have a ton of homework or they go to church groups or take music lessons outside of school. If they are forced to add one more thing, their grades might drop, and school work is supposed to come first.

Second, activities are not always free. Sports need cleats, uniforms, and sometimes a fee to play. Even clubs sometimes ask for money for T-shirts or trips. Also if you stay after school you miss the bus, so somebody has to drive you home. Some parents work late and can't do that. It isn't fair to make a rule that is easy for some families and really hard for others.

Lastly, when people are forced to do something they usually don't try very hard. Think about a kid who hates running being put on the track team. He is going to be miserable and the team will be worse too. The students who actually want to be on student council would have to work with people who are just there because they have to be. That ruins it for everybody.

Some people say that activities help you make friends and learn new skills, and that is true. But the school could encourage students to join by having a club fair or letting us try things out, instead of making it a rule.

In conclusion, the principal should let students decide for themselves. Extracurricular activities should be a choice, not a requirement.
