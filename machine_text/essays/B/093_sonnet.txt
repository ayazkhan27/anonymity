I agree with the principal's decision that every student should have to join at least one extracurricular activity. Some kids might not like being told what to do, but I think it will be good for the whole school.

First, activities help students make friends. When I started eighth grade, I only talked to the few kids in my classes. My cousin joined the yearbook club last year, and she said she met a lot of new people she never would have talked to. If everyone has to join something, nobody gets left out and more students will feel like they belong at school.

Second, activities teach skills that you can't learn from a textbook. Playing on a sports team teaches teamwork and how to handle losing. Being on the student council teaches you how to speak in front of people and how to solve problems with a group. Yearbook teaches you about deadlines and taking photos. These skills will help us later when we get jobs.

Third, it keeps students busy in a good way. A lot of kids go home and just play video games or scroll on their phones for hours. If they have practice or a meeting after school, they are doing something that actually matters. Some studies even say students who do activities get better grades because they learn to manage their time.

Some people say that students who have jobs or take care of siblings won't have time. That is a fair point, but the principal could pick activities that are short, like a club that meets once a week. Then everyone could find one that fits.

In conclusion, I think the principal made the right choice. Extracurricular activities help students make friends, learn new skills, and stay out of trouble. Even if some students complain at first, they will probably end up thankful they joined.
