Why Asking More Than One Person Helps

Everybody has to make hard choices sometimes. Maybe it is what sport to try out for, which friend to sit with at lunch, or even what high school classes to sign up for. When I have a big choice to make, I usually don't just ask one person. I ask a few people. I think seeking multiple opinions can help someone make a better choice because different people have different experiences, they can catch mistakes you didn't notice, and hearing more than one side helps you figure out what you really want.

The first reason asking more than one person helps is that everyone has different experiences. One person only knows what happened to them. For example, last year I was trying to decide if I should join band or take art as my elective. My cousin was in band and she said it was the best thing ever because she made a ton of friends and got to go on trips. If I only listened to her, I would have joined band right away. But then I asked my neighbor Marcus, who also did band, and he said it took up a lot of time because you have to practice every night and go to concerts on weekends. Then I asked my art teacher, and she told me art class lets you work at your own pace and you get to keep your projects. All three of them were right, but they each only saw one piece of the picture. By putting their answers together I got a way better idea of what each choice would really be like.

The second reason is that other people can see problems that you don't see. When you really want something, you kind of ignore the bad parts. My older brother is a good example of this. He wanted to buy a used car that he found online and he was super excited about it because it looked cool and it was cheap. He asked my dad and my dad said it seemed okay. But then he also asked our uncle, who works at a car repair shop. My uncle looked at the pictures and asked a bunch of questions and found out the car had been in a flood. That means it could have a lot of problems later on that would cost way more money than he saved. If my brother had only asked my dad, he might have bought a car that broke down after a month. Asking one more person saved him a lot of money and stress. Sometimes the second or third person knows something the first person doesn't.

The third reason is that hearing a lot of different opinions actually helps you figure out what you think. This sounds weird, but it's true. When people give you advice, you start to notice which advice makes you feel happy and which advice makes you feel disappointed. That tells you something about what you really want. When I was deciding whether to go to a summer camp that my friends were going to or stay home and do a coding class, I asked my mom, my best friend, and my coach. My best friend obviously said come to camp. My mom said the coding class would be good for my future. My coach said either one was fine. When my mom talked about the coding class, I realized I felt kind of excited, more than when my friend talked about camp. So I picked the coding class, and I'm really glad I did, because now I want to make video games when I grow up. I don't think I would have known how I felt if I had only talked to one person.

Some people might say that asking too many people just makes you more confused. And that can happen if you ask like twenty people and they all say something different. But I think the answer is to ask a few people you trust who know about the topic. You don't have to do what everybody says. You just listen, think about it, and then make the choice yourself. In the end it's still your decision, but you are making it with more information.

In conclusion, asking more than one person for advice can really help you make a better choice. Different people have different experiences, they can point out problems you didn't think about, and listening to all of them helps you understand what you actually want. Next time you have a hard choice to make, don't just go with the first answer you get. Ask around a little bit. It might change your mind, or it might make you even more sure, but either way you'll feel better about your choice.
