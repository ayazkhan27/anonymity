Have you ever asked somebody for advice and then found out later that it was totally wrong? I have. That is why I think it is smart to ask more than one person when you have a big choice to make. Seeking multiple opinions can help someone make a better choice because different people know different things, one person can be biased, and hearing a lot of ideas helps you figure out what you really want.

First of all, different people know different things. Nobody knows everything, even adults. For example, last year I wanted to buy a new bike with the money I saved up from mowing lawns. I asked my dad and he told me to get the cheapest one because I would just outgrow it. Then I asked my cousin Marcus, who rides trails all the time, and he said the cheap ones have bad brakes and the chain falls off. After that I asked the guy at the bike shop and he showed me a used bike that was good quality but cost less than a new one. If I only listened to my dad, I would have got a bike that broke in a few months. Because I asked three people, I found out about an option I didn't even know about. Each person had a piece of the answer.

Another reason is that one person might be biased. Biased means they lean one way because of their own feelings. Like if you ask your best friend if you should try out for the basketball team, she might say no just because she wants you to be in drama club with her. She is not trying to be mean, she just wants to hang out with you. But if you also ask your coach and your older brother and your mom, you get opinions from people who want different things. Then you can kind of see which advice is about you and which advice is really about them. If four people say you should try out and one says don't, that tells you something.

Lastly, hearing a lot of opinions helps you figure out what you think yourself. This sounds weird but it's true. When my friend Dani was picking her classes for high school, she didn't know if she should take Spanish or French. She asked her sister, two teachers, and me. Her sister said French, and Dani told me she felt disappointed when she heard that. That is how she knew she actually wanted Spanish the whole time. Sometimes you don't know how you feel until someone tells you to do the opposite. So even the advice you don't take can still help you.

Some people might say that asking too many people just makes you confused. I get that, because sometimes everybody says something different and you feel stuck. But I think being a little confused is better than being sure about a bad choice. Also, you don't have to do what everyone says. You are still the one who decides. The opinions are just like tools, and you pick which ones to use.

In conclusion, asking more than one person for advice is a good idea. People know different things, some people are biased without meaning to be, and listening to other people helps you understand your own mind. So next time you have a hard decision, don't just ask one person and call it done. Ask a few, think about it, and then choose. You will probably be happier with what you pick.
