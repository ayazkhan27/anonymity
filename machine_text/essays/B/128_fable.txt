Have you ever asked just one person for advice and then found out later it was the wrong thing to do? I have, and it was not fun. When people have a hard choice to make, they usually talk to more then one person about it. I think this is a smart thing to do. Asking multiple people for their opinions can help someone make a better choice because different people know different things, one person can be biased, and it helps you figure out what you really want.

First of all, different people know different things. Nobody knows everything, even adults. For example, last year I had to pick between joining band or taking art as my elective. I asked my mom and she said band because she played the flute when she was in school. But then I asked my cousin who is in high school, and he told me that band has practices after school and you have to go to football games on Fridays. My mom didn't know that because it was different when she was a kid. Then I asked my friend Dana who was already in art, and she said the teacher lets you pick your own projects. If I only asked my mom I would of never known any of that stuff. Each person gave me a piece of the puzzle, and when I put them together I could see the whole picture.

Another reason is that one person might be biased. That means they have their own reasons for wanting you to pick something. Like if you ask your best friend if you should try out for the soccer team, and your friend is on the soccer team, of course they are going to say yes. They want you there with them. That doesn't mean they are lying, it just means they are only thinking about it from one side. But if you also ask your coach, your parents, and maybe somebody who quit the team, you get a more fair answer. If three out of four people tell you the same thing, it is probably true. If everybody says something different, then at least you know the choice is not as simple as you thought.

Finally, talking to more people helps you figure out what you actually want. This one sounds weird but it is true. Sometimes when a person gives me advice, I notice that I feel dissapointed by what they said. That tells me that deep down I already wanted the other choice. When I was deciding about band and art, my uncle told me to do band, and I felt kind of bummed out. That is when I knew I really wanted art. The more people you talk to, the more chances you get to hear your own thoughts out loud and see how you react to things.

Some people might say that asking too many people just makes you confused. I get that, because sometimes it does feel like everybody is telling you something different. But I think being a little confused is better than being wrong. And in the end you are still the one who makes the choice. Other people's opinions are just tools to help you, they are not the boss of you.

In conclusion, seeking multiple opinions helps people make better choices. Different people know different facts, it protects you from one person's bias, and it helps you understand yourself better. I ended up picking art and I am really glad I did. So next time you have a big decision, don't just ask one person. Ask a few, and then decide for yourself.
