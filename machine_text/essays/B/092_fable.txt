Imagine it is 3:15 on a Tuesday and the last bell rings. Some kids run to the gym for basketball practice, some go to the art room to work on the yearbook, and some just want to go home because they have a little brother to watch or a ton of homework. Now imagine the principal says every single one of those kids has to stay for an activity, no matter what. Our principal has decided that all students must participate in at least one extracurricular activity. I disagree with this decision. I think activities are great, but forcing them on everybody is not fair, it will not work the way the principal wants, and it takes away a choice that should belong to students and their families.

My first reason is that not every student has the time or the way to do it. A lot of people think that after school kids just sit around and play video games, but that is not true for everyone. My friend Marcus has to walk his two little sisters home from the elementary school every day because his mom works until six. If he had to stay for soccer or student council, who would pick them up? Other kids ride the bus, and the bus leaves at 3:25. If you miss it, you need a ride, and some parents do not have a car or they are at work. There are also costs. Sports need cleats and shin guards and sometimes a fee. Even band needs an instrument. The principal might say the school will help, but I have not seen the school pay for everybody's cleats before. A rule that is easy for some families and really hard for other families is not a fair rule.

My second reason is that making people do something does not make them care about it. Think about what happens in gym class when we play a sport that half the class does not like. Those kids stand in the back and wait for it to be over. The same thing would happen in clubs. Right now, the yearbook club is full of people who actually want to take pictures and design pages. If you add fifteen kids who are only there because they have to be, they are going to sit on their phones and distract everyone else. The teacher who runs the club will have to spend her time telling people to pay attention instead of helping the kids who want to learn. It would be worse for teams. Nobody wants a teammate who does not show up to practice or does not try in the game. So the rule would actually hurt the activities for the students who love them.

My third reason is that students already have a lot going on, and some of us have activities that are not at school. I know people who take karate, who go to church youth group, who help at their family's restaurant, or who take care of their grandparents. Those things teach responsibility and teamwork too, but they would not count under this rule because they are not school activities. Also, eighth grade has a lot of homework. Last week I had a science project, a math test, and an essay all due in the same three days. I stayed up until eleven one night. If I had to add practice four days a week on top of that, something would have to give, and it would probably be my grades or my sleep. Teachers always tell us that school work comes first, so it does not make sense to make a rule that takes time away from it.

Some people will say that extracurriculars help students make friends and find out what they are good at, and that shy kids will never join unless somebody makes them. I understand that. I joined the chess club in sixth grade because my friend dragged me there, and I ended up liking it. But there is a big difference between encouraging and forcing. The school could have an activity fair in the gym where every club has a table. They could let students try a club for a week with no commitment. They could have some clubs meet during lunch so that kids who have to ride the bus can still be in one. Teachers could personally invite quiet students to join something. All of those ideas would get more students involved without punishing the ones who cannot stay.

In conclusion, I disagree with the principal's decision to require every student to join an extracurricular activity. It is unfair to students who have family responsibilities or no ride home, it would fill clubs and teams with people who do not want to be there, and it adds more stress to kids who are already busy. Extracurriculars are supposed to be the fun part of school, the part you pick for yourself. If the principal makes them mandatory, they just turn into one more class. I hope the principal will change the rule and find ways to invite us instead of forcing us.
