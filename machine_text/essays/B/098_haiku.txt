Should Every Student Be Required to Join an Activity?

My principal has decided that every student must take part in at least one extracurricular activity. I agree with this decision. I think school should be more than just classes and tests, and everyone should have a place where they belong and can try something new. Some students might not want to join anything, but I believe the benefits are worth it even for them.

The first reason I agree is that activities help students make friends. In my grade there are kids who eat lunch alone every day. I know one boy named Marcus who was very quiet in the hallway and never talked to anyone. Then he joined the chess club during the second semester. Now he sits with the same group of people every Tuesday and Thursday, and he laughs a lot more than he used to. If he had never been required to join something, he might have kept eating alone all year. Activities give students a reason to talk to people they would not normally meet, especially people from other grades and other friend groups.

The second reason is that activities teach skills that you cannot learn from a textbook. When you are on a sports team, you have to show up on time, listen to the coach, and work with people even when you do not like them. When you work on the yearbook, you have to meet deadlines and take responsibility for your pages. My older sister was on the student council last year, and she told me it taught her how to run a meeting and how to speak in front of a crowd without shaking. Those skills will help her in high school and later in her job. A class can tell you how to do something, but an activity makes you actually do it.

Some people argue that forcing students to join something is unfair, because they might already have jobs or family responsibilities or hobbies at home. That is a fair point, and I think the rule should be flexible. The students do not have to choose something huge like the varsity basketball team. They can pick something small, like the garden club that meets for thirty minutes, or a one-day volunteer event. The goal is just to get everyone involved in something outside the classroom. Also, a student who has a job after school might still find that the robotics club or the drama club is a good break from work and a good place to meet new people.

Another reason I support this decision is that activities help students discover interests they did not know they had. Many people think they will hate something until they try it. My friend Priya said she would never join anything because she was shy and did not think she was good at anything. The principal made her pick one activity, and she chose the school newspaper mostly because it was the only one with an open spot. Now she writes a column every month about animals in our town, and she says it is her favorite part of school. If she had never been pushed to try, she would have never known she could write well or that she cared so much about animals.

Finally, activities keep students connected to school. Students who feel like they belong at a school are less likely to skip class or drop out. When students have an activity they care about, they have a reason to come to school on time and a group of people who will notice if they are missing. I think this is especially important for middle school students, who can feel lost during the transition from elementary school. An activity can be an anchor that keeps them from drifting away.

I understand why some students feel that the requirement is annoying. Nobody likes being told what to do, and some students may feel like they are wasting their time at first. However, I believe that a requirement can start something good. Many people end up loving an activity they only joined because they had to. The principal is not trying to make students miserable. The principal wants every student to have a chance to grow, make friends, and find something they enjoy.

For these reasons, I agree that all students should participate in at least one extracurricular activity. Activities help students make friends, learn real-world skills, discover new interests, and feel connected to their school. The requirement might feel strict at first, but it gives every student the same opportunity to experience something beyond the classroom. I think that is a decision worth supporting.
