Phones and Driving Don't Mix

Almost everyone I know has a cell phone, and most of us check it way more than we should. My mom, my friends, even my grandpa can't go an hour without looking at a screen. But there is one place where phones just don't belong, and that is in the hands of someone driving a car. In my opinion, drivers should not be able to use cell phones in any capacity while operating a vehicle.

The first reason is that phones take your attention off the road. Driving is a serious job that needs your eyes, your hands, and your brain all at once. When you look down to read a text, even for a few seconds, your car keeps moving. At highway speed, a car can travel the length of a football field in the time it takes to read a short message. Anything could happen in that time, like a kid running into the street or the car in front of you stopping suddenly.

Some people say that hands-free calling is safe because you can keep both hands on the wheel. I don't agree with this. Even if your hands are free, your mind is still somewhere else. I have been in the car when my dad was on a speaker call and he missed our exit because he was so focused on the conversation. That was not dangerous, but it shows that talking on the phone splits your attention, and in a different situation it could cause a crash.

Another reason is that it is not worth the risk. Nothing on a phone is so important that it can't wait until you park. A text from a friend or a new video is not worth someone's life. If there really is an emergency, a driver can pull over safely and then make the call.

In conclusion, cell phones and driving should be kept completely separate. Phones distract drivers, even when they are hands-free, and no message is worth risking a life. If everyone put their phone away while driving, the roads would be a lot safer for all of us.
