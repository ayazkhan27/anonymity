I agree with my principal's decision that all students must participate in at least one extracurricular activity. Some people might think it is unfair to make kids do something after school, but I think it would help students in a lot of ways. Extracurricular activities help students make friends, learn new skills, and stay out of trouble.

First of all, extracurricular activities help students make friends. When you are in class, you mostly sit at your desk and listen to the teacher, so you don't really get to talk to people. But when you join a club or a team, you spend time with people who like the same things as you. For example, last year I joined the soccer team even though I didn't know anybody on it. By the end of the season, some of my teammates became my best friends. We still hang out on the weekends. If I never joined, I would probably not know them at all. Some students are shy and would never join anything on their own, so making it a rule would give them a push they need.

Second, extracurricular activities teach students skills they can't always learn in class. Working on the yearbook teaches you how to take good pictures, write captions, and meet deadlines. Being on the student council teaches you how to be a leader and speak in front of people. Sports teach you teamwork and how to keep going even when you are losing. My cousin was in the robotics club in middle school and now she wants to be an engineer. She said she never would have found out she liked building things if she didn't join that club. These skills will help students when they get to high school and even when they get jobs someday.

Third, extracurricular activities keep students busy and out of trouble. A lot of kids go home after school and just play video games or scroll on their phones for hours. Some kids might even get into bad situations because they are bored and have nothing to do. If they are at practice or a club meeting, they are doing something positive instead. Also, being in an activity can make students care more about school. On my soccer team, you had to keep your grades up to play, so a lot of players worked harder in class.

Some people might say that students are already too busy with homework or have jobs or have to babysit their siblings. That is a fair point. But the principal said at least one activity, not five. There are lots of choices, and some clubs only meet once a week or even during lunch. Almost everyone can find something that fits their schedule.

In conclusion, I think the principal made a good decision. Extracurricular activities help students make friends, learn important skills, and stay out of trouble. Even if some students don't want to at first, I think most of them will end up being glad they joined something.
