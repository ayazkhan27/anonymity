When people have a big decision to make, they often ask for advice. Sometimes they ask just one person, but I think it is usually smarter to talk to more than one. Asking several people gives you more information, helps you catch mistakes, and shows you what you really want. Getting multiple opinions can help someone make a better choice.

First, different people know different things. Last year I had to choose between joining the school band and trying out for the soccer team. Both met after school on the same days, so I could not do both. I asked my mom first. She said soccer would be good because I would get exercise. That was a fine point, but she did not know much about band. Then I asked my friend Marcus, who plays trumpet. He told me that band goes on a trip to a festival every spring and that you get to pick your own instrument. After that, I asked my gym teacher, who said that soccer tryouts are only held once a year, but band lets you join whenever. Each person gave me something the others left out. If I had only asked my mom, I would have missed a lot of important facts.

Second, asking more than one person helps you notice when someone's advice is biased. Everybody has their own interests and feelings, even when they are trying to be helpful. For example, if you ask a salesperson at a store whether you should buy the most expensive phone, they will probably say yes, because they want to make money. My cousin wanted a new phone and only asked the guy at the store. He bought the expensive one and then found out his friends had the same features on cheaper phones. If he had asked a friend or his dad too, he would have heard a different side. When several people give you advice and some of it matches, you can feel more sure that it is honest. When one person says something totally different from everyone else, you can think harder about why.

Third, hearing different opinions can help you think of choices you did not even know about. Sometimes a person is stuck because they only see two options. A new person may suggest a third one. When I could not decide between band and soccer, my older sister said, "Why not do band this year and try out for soccer next year?" I had not thought about that at all. I had just assumed that I had to pick one forever. Her idea made the whole problem feel smaller. That is something I never would have figured out by talking to only one person, or by thinking about it alone.

Fourth, talking to many people can help you learn what you really want. When you listen to everyone's advice, you often notice how you feel about each answer. If someone tells you to do something and you feel disappointed, that may mean you wanted the other choice. When my mom said I should do soccer, I felt kind of let down, and that was when I realized I was more excited about band. Without hearing her opinion, I might not have understood my own feelings. So even advice you do not take can still be helpful.

Some people might say that asking too many people is a bad idea because it can get confusing. If five people each tell you something different, you might feel even more stuck than before. That is a fair point. I think the trick is to ask a few people you trust, and people who know different things about the problem, instead of asking everybody you meet. Also, in the end, the choice belongs to you. Advice is only there to help you think, not to make the decision for you. You should listen to everyone, but you are the one who has to live with the result.

In conclusion, seeking more than one opinion helps because people know different things, some advice can be biased, new ideas can come up, and you can learn about your own feelings. My experience choosing between band and soccer showed me this. I ended up joining band, and I am really glad I did, partly because I took the time to ask different people. The next time I have a hard choice, I will definitely ask more than one person before I decide.
