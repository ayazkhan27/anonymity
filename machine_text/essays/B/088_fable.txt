Our principal has decided that every student has to be in at least one extracurricular activity. When I first heard this I was kind of annoyed, because nobody likes being told what to do with there free time. But after thinking about it more, I agree with the decision. I think it will help students make friends, learn things they can't learn in a classroom, and maybe even do better in school.

The first reason I agree is that activities help you meet people. In sixth grade I didn't really know anybody because I came from a different elementary school than most of the kids. I ate lunch with the same two people every day and I barely talked in class. Then my mom made me sign up for cross country even though I am not a fast runner at all. I finished almost last in every race. But I ended up with like ten new friends, and some of them were seventh and eighth graders who I never would of talked to otherwise. If my mom hadn't pushed me I would have stayed home and played video games. A lot of kids are like that. They want to join something but they are too nervous to do it on their own, so a rule gives them the push they need.

Another reason is that you learn different skills in activities than you do in class. In math you learn how to solve equations, but you don't learn how to work with a group of people who all want different things. My cousin is on the yearbook staff at her school and she told me they had to meet deadlines, take pictures at games, and argue about which photos go on which page. She said it was stressful but she learned how to plan ahead. Student council is the same way. You have to speak in front of people and listen to ideas you don't agree with. Those are things you will need for a job someday, and a worksheet can't teach them.

Some people say this rule is unfair because students are already busy. They have homework, chores, and some kids have to babysit their little brothers and sisters after school. I understand that, and I think it is the best argument against the rule. But the principal only said one activity, not five. There are clubs that meet once a week during lunch, like chess club or the art club, so you wouldn't even have to stay after school. The school should make sure there are enough choices like that for kids who can't get a ride home late. If they do that, I don't think being busy is a good enough excuse.

Other people say that if you force someone to join, they won't try and they will ruin it for everyone else. That might happen sometimes. But I think most kids will find at least one thing they like if they have to look. Our school has sports, band, drama, robotics, yearbook, and a bunch of other stuff. Nobody hates every single one of those. And even if a kid starts out not caring, they might change their mind, just like I did with running.

Lastly, I think activities can actually help your grades. On most teams you have to keep your grades up or you can't play, so it gives you a reason to turn in your homework. Also when you have practice you learn to manage your time better, because you can't just wait until ten at night to start everything.

In conclusion, I agree with the principal's decision. It might seem annoying at first, but one activity is not a lot to ask. Students will make friends, learn real life skills, and feel more like they belong at school. I think in a year most kids will be glad the rule was made.
