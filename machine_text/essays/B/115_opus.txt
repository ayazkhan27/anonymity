Phones and Driving Don't Mix

Almost everyone I know has a cell phone, and most of them can't go ten minutes without checking it. My friends text during lunch, during class when the teacher isn't looking, and even while walking down the hallway. That habit is mostly harmless at school, but it becomes dangerous when people bring it into the driver's seat. I believe drivers should not be allowed to use cell phones in any way while operating a vehicle, because it causes accidents, it is impossible to do safely, and the few reasons people give for needing their phones can be solved in other ways.

The biggest reason is that using a phone while driving causes crashes. When you look down at a text, your eyes leave the road for a few seconds. That doesn't sound like a lot, but at fifty miles per hour a car can travel the length of a football field in that time. Anything could happen in that distance. A kid could run into the street, or the car in front could slam on its brakes. I've heard about people in my own town who got into accidents because they were reading a message, and one of them was a senior at my school. Stories like that show this isn't just something adults warn us about to scare us.

Some people argue that hands-free calling is safe, since you don't have to hold the phone or look at it. However, even talking on a phone takes your attention away from driving. Your mind is focused on the conversation instead of the road. I've noticed this when my mom talks on speaker in the car. She'll miss a turn or forget to use her blinker because she is distracted. If an experienced driver like her has trouble, then a new driver like a teenager will have even more trouble. Your brain can only focus on so many things at once, and driving needs all of it.

Another argument is that people need their phones for GPS or for emergencies. This is a fair point, but there are easy solutions. You can set up your directions before you start driving and let the voice tell you where to go without touching anything. If there is an emergency, you can pull over to the side of the road and make the call safely. Nothing is so important that it is worth risking your life or someone else's life to answer it at a red light.

In conclusion, drivers should not be able to use their cell phones in any capacity while they drive. Phones take your eyes, your hands, and your mind off of the road, and that leads to accidents that could have been prevented. Texts and calls can wait a few minutes. A person's life can't be replaced. The next time you get in the car, put your phone in the glove box and just drive.
