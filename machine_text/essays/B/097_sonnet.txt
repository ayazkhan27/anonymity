I agree with the principal's decision that every student should join at least one extracurricular activity. I think it would make our school a better place for a lot of reasons.

First, activities help students meet new people. At the start of the year I only knew the kids from my elementary school. Then I joined the yearbook club and met students from other grades who I never would have talked to. Some of them are now my best friends. If students are forced to join something, they will have a chance to make friends too, especially the shy ones who might never go out of their way to do it.

Second, activities teach skills that you can't learn in a regular class. On the student council, you have to plan events, give speeches, and work with a budget. In sports, you learn about teamwork and not giving up when you lose. These are things that will help us when we are adults and have jobs. Nobody learns how to be a leader just by sitting at a desk.

Some people might say that this is unfair because many students have chores or have to take care of their little brothers and sisters after school. That is a good point. However, the principal could pick activities that happen during lunch or have clubs that only meet once a week. That way everybody can find something that fits their schedule.

Also, students who are busy with activities usually get better grades. When you have less free time, you learn to manage it better. My cousin was on the soccer team and he said he did his homework faster so he wouldn't miss practice.

In conclusion, I agree with the principal. Extracurricular activities help us make friends, learn important skills, and stay organized. Every student deserves the chance to find something they love, so this rule is a good idea.
