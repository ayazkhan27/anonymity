Almost everybody I know has a phone in their pocket right now, and most of us check it without even thinking about it. That is fine when you are sitting on the couch, but it becomes a serious problem when you are behind the wheel of a car that weighs two tons. I believe drivers should not be allowed to use their cell phones while operating a vehicle, with a small exception for emergencies and hands-free directions. Driving takes all of your attention, and a phone is designed to steal it.

The first reason is the most obvious one, which is that phones cause accidents. When you look down to read a text, your eyes are off the road for about five seconds. That might not sound like much, but if you are going 55 miles an hour, you travel about the length of a football field in that time without seeing anything in front of you. A kid could run into the street, the car ahead could slam on its brakes, or the light could turn red. Thousands of people die every year in crashes that involve a distracted driver, and a lot of those drivers were on their phones. These are not accidents that had to happen. Somebody decided a message was more important than paying attention.

Some people say they are good at multitasking, so the rule should not apply to them. I used to think that too, until I started learning to drive last year. My dad took me to an empty parking lot and I could barely handle the mirrors, the pedals, and the steering all at once. Even now that I am better at it, I notice that when my friend in the passenger seat shows me something funny on her phone, I drift a little in my lane. The truth is that the brain can not really do two things at once. It just switches back and forth quickly, and each time it switches, it misses something. Nobody is the exception, even if they think they are.

Another reason is that driving on your phone does not only put you in danger. If I choose to eat junk food every day, I am mostly hurting myself. But if I choose to text while driving, I am risking the lives of my passengers, the people in other cars, and anybody walking or riding a bike nearby. Those people never agreed to that risk. A few years ago, a girl from a school near mine was hit by a driver who was looking at his phone, and she was in the hospital for months. She did nothing wrong. She was just crossing the street. That is why I think this should be a law and not just a personal choice.

People who disagree with me usually bring up the fact that phones are useful in the car. They are right about that. Most people use their phone for GPS now, and it would be silly to make everyone go back to paper maps. Also, if there is an accident or your car breaks down, you need to be able to call for help. I think both of these can be allowed without changing my main point. Directions can be set up before you start driving and then spoken out loud, so you never have to touch the screen. Emergency calls can be made after you pull over. What should not be allowed is holding the phone, texting, scrolling through social media, or picking a new song while the car is moving.

Lastly, a strict rule is easier to follow than a loose one. If the law says you can use your phone "a little bit," everybody will decide for themselves what a little bit means. If the law says the phone stays down, there is no confusion.

In conclusion, drivers should not be using cell phones while they are driving, except for hands-free directions and real emergencies. No text, call, or notification is worth somebody's life. The message will still be there when you park, and so will you.
