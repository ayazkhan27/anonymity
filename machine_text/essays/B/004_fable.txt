Dear Principal,

I heard that you are thinking about changing the cell phone policy at our school. I think you should pick Policy 1, which lets students bring phones and use them at lunch and free times as long as they are off during class. I believe this is the better choice for a few reasons.

First of all, phones are important for safety. A lot of kids at this school walk home or ride the bus, and some of us have practice after school that ends at different times. Last month my basketball practice got canceled and I had to call my mom to come get me early. If I didnt have my phone I would of been sitting outside the gym for an hour and a half. Parents want to be able to reach their kids, and if phones are banned completly then they can't. The office phone is always busy and they dont really like when students ask to use it.

Second, Policy 1 teaches us responsibility. In high school and at jobs people have phones and they have to know when it is ok to use them and when it isn't. If you just take phones away, we never learn that. But if we are allowed to have them and have to keep them off in class, we get practice at controlling ourselfs. Students who break the rule can get there phone taken away for the day, which is fair. That way only the people who mess up get punished and not everybody.

Another reason is that lunch is our free time. We work hard all morning, and for some students texting a friend or listening to music is how they relax. It doesn't hurt anybody's learning because nobody is learning at lunch anyway. Some people say kids will just stare at there screens and not talk to each other, but honestly most of us show each other stuff on our phones and laugh about it, so it is still being social.

Finally, I think Policy 2 would not even work. Kids would sneak phones in their backpacks and teachers would have to spend alot of time searching for them instead of teaching. That would cause more problems then it solves.

In conclusion, Policy 1 is safer, it teaches responsibility, and it is more fair to students. I hope you will think about my reasons when you make your decision. Thank you for reading my letter.

Sincerely,
A concerned 8th grader
