I agree with the principal's decision that all students must participate in at least one extracurricular activity. School is about more than classes and tests. It is also about finding out who you are, making friends, and learning how to work with other people. An extracurricular activity gives students a place to do all of those things, and I think every student deserves that chance.

First, extracurricular activities help students make friends. At my school, there are a lot of kids who sit alone at lunch. Some of them are shy, and some just have not found the people they click with. When you are on a team or in a club, you see the same people every week, and you have something to talk about besides homework. My friend Marcus was really quiet when he started sixth grade. He joined the chess club because his cousin was in it. Now he is the one who organizes the Friday games, and he has friends in every grade. Without the club, I do not think he would have met most of them.

Second, activities teach skills that are hard to learn in a regular classroom. Sports teach teamwork, because a basketball team does not win if everyone plays alone. The yearbook teaches deadlines and responsibility, because if you do not finish your page, the whole book is missing something. Student council teaches people how to listen to others and speak up in front of a crowd. These are real skills that adults use at their jobs. A student who learns them early will be better prepared for high school and for life after school.

Third, activities help students discover their interests. Many students do not know what they are good at until they try something new. A kid who thinks he hates reading might find out he loves writing for the school newspaper. A girl who has never held a paintbrush might discover she loves designing posters for the drama club. If students are not required to try something, they might never find out. Requiring one activity gives every student a chance to explore, and some of them will find a passion that lasts for years.

Some people argue that students who are busy with other things will not have time for their studies, or that forcing students to join something takes away their freedom. I understand these concerns. However, the principal does not say students must do a huge amount of work. They only need to participate in at least one activity, and many clubs meet only once a week or even less. Most activities also end early enough that students still have time for homework. Also, students can choose which activity they want, so the choice still belongs to them. The requirement just makes sure they make the choice.

Another argument is that some students cannot afford certain activities, like travel sports or expensive lessons. This is a fair point, and the school should make sure there are free options too. Many schools already have clubs that cost nothing, such as the library club, the science club, or the gardening club. If the school offers a wide range of choices, including low-cost ones, then every student can find something that fits their budget and their schedule.

In conclusion, I agree that all students should participate in at least one extracurricular activity. Activities help students make friends, learn important skills, and find out what they enjoy. Marcus's story shows how one club can change a student's whole year. If the school makes sure every student has access to a fun and affordable option, then the requirement will help everyone grow. I believe the principal made a good decision, and I hope every student will take the chance to try something new.
