Should Students Be Able to Learn From Home?

When schools shut down during the pandemic, almost every student in America got a taste of distance learning, whether they wanted it or not. Some people loved it and some people hated it. Now that things are back to normal, some schools still offer online classes as an option. I think students would benefit from being able to attend classes from home, as long as it stays a choice and not a requirement.

The first reason is that not every student learns the same way. In a regular classroom, the teacher has to move at one speed for thirty kids. If you are behind, you get lost, and if you are ahead, you get bored. With online classes, a lot of lessons are recorded, so a student can pause, rewind, and watch a hard part again. My friend struggled in chemistry freshman year, and when we went online she said she finally understood stoichiometry because she could replay the teacher's explanation three times without feeling embarrassed to ask.

Another reason is that some students have situations at home or with their health that make going to school every day really hard. A student with a chronic illness might miss weeks of school and fall way behind. A student who has to babysit younger siblings or work to help their family might not be able to make it to first period. Distance learning lets these students keep up instead of failing or dropping out. It gives them a fair chance that they would not have otherwise.

Distance learning can also help students who deal with anxiety or bullying. For some kids, the hallway is the worst part of the day. Learning from home can let them focus on school work instead of worrying about who is going to say something to them at lunch.

Some people argue that students at home will just sleep in, play video games, or not pay attention. That is true for some students, and I admit I have turned my camera off and scrolled on my phone before. But that is exactly why it should be an option and not forced on everyone. Students who know they need structure can still go to the building. Schools can also require cameras on and check attendance to keep people accountable.

In conclusion, letting students attend class from home would help a lot of different kinds of learners. It is not perfect for everyone, but giving students the choice means more of them can succeed in a way that works for their lives.
