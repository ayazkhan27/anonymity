Dear Principal,

I am writing to share my view on the possible policy that would stop students from playing sports or joining other activities unless they have at least a B average. I think this policy would be a mistake, and I hope you will think about my reasons before you decide.

First, sports and activities help students learn things that are not always shown on a report card. On the soccer team, I have learned how to work with people I do not always agree with, how to keep trying after I lose a game, and how to manage my time between practice and homework. These skills matter in class too. If students with a C average were not allowed to join, they would lose the chance to learn these lessons at the time they need them most.

Second, many students with a C average are working hard. A C does not always mean a student is lazy or does not care. Some students are still learning English, some have trouble at home, and some just need a different way to learn. For many of these students, the team or the club is the place where they feel like they belong. If we take that away, their grades might actually get worse, because they would have less reason to come to school and feel happy about being there.

Third, the policy might not solve the problem it is meant to solve. If the goal is to help students do better in class, then we should give them extra help, like tutoring or study halls, instead of punishing them by taking away the things they enjoy. Students who are struggling in math or science need support, not a rule that tells them they cannot do the activities they love until their grades go up.

Some people might say that sports are a privilege, and students should earn that privilege with good grades. I understand that point of view. School comes first, and I agree that students should not ignore their classes to play games. However, a student with a C average can still be responsible. Maybe the school could set up a plan where students who are below a B get support, check-ins with teachers, and a chance to improve, while still being allowed to play. This would keep students accountable without shutting them out.

Finally, I think the school should care about all of its students, not just the ones with the best grades. Sports and clubs bring people together and make school feel like a community. If we make the rule too strict, we could lose a lot of students who would have grown and learned from being part of something bigger than their classes.

Thank you for listening to the students' views. I hope you will consider keeping sports and activities open to everyone, and using extra help to support students who need it most.

Sincerely,

A Student
