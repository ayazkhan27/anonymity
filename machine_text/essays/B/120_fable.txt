Have you ever asked just one person for advice and then regretted what you did? I have. I think asking more than one person for advice helps you make a better choice because you get different points of view, you can catch mistakes, and you feel more sure about your decision.

First, different people know different things. Last year I had to pick between joining band or taking art as my elective. My mom told me to do band because she played the flute in high school and loved it. But my cousin, who is in band now, told me that you have to practice every night and go to a lot of weekend events. My friend Marcus said art class was more relaxed. If I only listened to my mom, I wouldn't have known about all the practice time. Each person gave me a piece of the picture.

Second, one person can be wrong. Nobody knows everything, even adults. One time my uncle told me the best way to fix my bike chain, and it actually made it worse. When I asked my neighbor, who works on bikes all the time, he showed me the right way. If two or three people tell you the same thing, it is probably good advice. If they all say something different, then you know you need to think harder about it.

Lastly, getting more opinions makes you more confident. When you only ask one person, you might keep wondering if you made the right call. But when you have heard from a few people, you can compare what they said and decide what makes sense for you. In the end I picked band, and I didn't feel nervous about it because I already knew what I was getting into.

Some people say asking too many people is confusing. That can be true, but you don't have to do what everyone says. You just listen and then choose for yourself.

In conclusion, asking multiple people for advice gives you more information, protects you from bad advice, and helps you feel good about your choice. So next time you have a hard decision, don't stop at one person.
