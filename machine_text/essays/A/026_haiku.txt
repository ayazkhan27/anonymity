Dear Principal,

I believe students should be required to complete community service. Helping others is one of the most valuable lessons a school can teach, and it should not depend on whether a student happens to feel like volunteering.

Consider the students who tutor elementary school children. A ten-year-old who struggles with reading often gains more confidence from one patient older student than from another hour of worksheets. The tutor, in turn, learns patience, responsibility, and the satisfaction of seeing someone improve because of their effort. Students who clean up litter in a local park learn something just as important: their town belongs to them, and they are responsible for keeping it livable.

I understand that some students worry about lost time. Homework, sports, jobs, and family obligations are real pressures. However, a requirement of perhaps twenty hours over a school year is modest. Most students spend far more time on video games or social media without a second thought. Service asks them to spend a small part of that time on something that matters to someone else.

Requiring service also levels the playing field. Students who already have time and resources would otherwise be the only ones who volunteer, while others never get the chance. A requirement ensures every student experiences what it feels like to contribute.

For these reasons, I urge you to make community service a graduation requirement. It will make our students more capable, more caring, and more connected to the community they will soon lead.

Sincerely,
A Concerned Student
