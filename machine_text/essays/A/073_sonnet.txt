Dear Principal,

I am writing to share my view on the proposal to require a B average for students who want to play sports or join other activities. I understand the goal of encouraging good grades, but I believe this policy would hurt many students and would not accomplish what you hope. I urge you to reject it.

First, many students who now have a C average are the ones who benefit most from activities. A student who struggles in math may still find confidence on the soccer field or in the drama club. Those activities give students a reason to come to school every day. If we take them away, we may remove the very thing that keeps some students motivated. A student who feels like a failure in every part of school is more likely to give up than to study harder.

Second, the policy ignores how many students would be affected. You mentioned that many students have a C average. That means a large share of our school would be shut out of teams, clubs, and music programs. Teams would struggle to find enough players, and clubs could shrink until they disappear. The school would lose spirit, and students would lose friendships and experiences they will remember long after they forget a chemistry quiz.

Third, activities teach skills that grades do not measure. Playing on a team teaches cooperation and discipline. Practices and games force students to manage their time carefully. Many of us do our homework right after practice because we know we have limited hours. Students who have nothing to do after school often fall behind anyway, or they spend the time on screens or getting into trouble. Activities keep students busy in a positive way and under adult supervision.

Fourth, a B average is a high bar for some students for reasons beyond their control. Some have jobs, care for younger siblings, or have learning differences that make school harder. Punishing them with exclusion seems unfair. Their grades may reflect their circumstances more than their effort.

I do agree that grades matter, and there are better ways to protect them. The school could keep a minimum standard, such as a C average, instead of a B. Students who fall below it could be placed on a probation period and offered free tutoring or a supervised study hall. This approach supports students instead of shutting them out. Coaches and advisors could also check in on grades regularly, so problems are caught early.

In conclusion, requiring a B average would take away motivation, shrink our teams and clubs, and treat struggling students unfairly. A policy of support and a reasonable minimum would do more to raise grades. Thank you for taking the time to consider the students' opinions.

Sincerely,
A Concerned Student
