Dear Principal,

I am writing about the cell phone policy you are reconsidering. I believe Policy 1 is the better choice: students should be allowed to bring phones and use them at lunch and during free time, as long as they stay off in class.

My first reason is safety. Many of us walk home, ride the city bus, or stay late for practice. When a game runs long or a ride falls through, a phone is how we tell our parents. Last winter my bus broke down two miles from school, and the only reason my mother knew where I was is that I could call her. Under Policy 2, I would have been standing on the roadside with no way to reach anyone.

Second, a total ban is hard to enforce. Students will still bring phones, only now they will hide them in pockets and backpacks. Teachers would spend their time searching for phones instead of teaching, and the office would fill up with confiscated ones. A rule that most students quietly break teaches us that rules do not matter. Policy 1 is a rule we can actually follow, because it asks for something fair.

Third, Policy 1 protects what matters most, which is class time. Phones would be off during lessons, so teachers would not be competing with screens. If a student breaks that rule, the phone can be taken until the end of the day. That consequence is clear, and most of us would rather keep our phones than lose them.

Finally, learning to put a phone away at the right time is a skill we will need at work and in college. Banning phones does not teach that. Trusting us with limits does.

Please choose Policy 1. It keeps classrooms focused and students safe.

Sincerely,
A concerned student
