Dear Principal,

I am writing about the cell phone policy you are reconsidering. I believe Policy 1 is the better choice. Students should be allowed to bring phones to school and use them during lunch and free periods, as long as the phones stay off during class.

The first reason is safety. Many parents want to reach their children during the day, and students need a way to contact home when plans change. If practice gets cancelled or a student feels sick, a quick text solves the problem. In a real emergency, such as a lockdown or a bus accident, a phone could help a family know their child is safe. Banning phones completely would take that peace of mind away.

Second, Policy 1 teaches responsibility. In a few years we will be in college or at jobs where no one takes our phones away. We will have to decide for ourselves when to put them down. Letting students use phones at lunch but requiring them to be off in class gives us practice with that kind of self-control. A total ban only teaches us to sneak.

Third, a complete ban would be hard to enforce. Students will still bring phones in their pockets and backpacks, and teachers will spend time searching and confiscating instead of teaching. With Policy 1, the rule is clear and fair, so most students will follow it. Teachers only need to step in when a phone comes out during class.

Finally, phones can be useful during free time. Students can check assignments online, look up information for a project, or coordinate with group partners. Lunch is also a time to relax, and a few minutes with music or messages from family is harmless.

Some people worry that phones will distract students. That concern is fair, but Policy 1 already deals with it by keeping phones off when learning happens. Students who break the rule can face consequences, while everyone else keeps the benefits.

For these reasons, I hope you will choose Policy 1. It keeps students safe, builds responsibility, and is realistic to enforce.

Sincerely,
A Concerned Student
