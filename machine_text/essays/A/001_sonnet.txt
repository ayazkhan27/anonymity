Dear Principal,

I am writing to urge you to adopt Policy 1, which lets students carry phones and use them at lunch and during free time, as long as they are turned off in class. I believe this approach is fairer and more realistic than banning phones outright.

First, a total ban would be hard to enforce. Almost every student already owns a phone, and many would simply hide them in backpacks or pockets. Teachers would then spend valuable class time hunting for hidden devices instead of teaching. Policy 1 avoids this problem by giving students a clear, reasonable rule: phones off during class, allowed during breaks. Students are far more likely to follow a rule that feels fair.

Second, phones matter for safety and family life. Many of us have parents who work late, siblings to pick up, or after-school activities that change without warning. If a bus is delayed or a practice is canceled, a quick text home solves the problem in seconds. In a true emergency, students should never be cut off from their families. Policy 2 would take that connection away for the entire day.

Third, free time is meant for relaxing. After hours of lessons and tests, a student who can listen to music, message a friend, or check a schedule during lunch often returns to class calmer and more focused. Allowing phones during breaks also teaches responsibility. Learning when to use technology and when to put it away is a skill we will need in college and at work, and a ban would give us no chance to practice it.

Policy 1 protects learning while respecting students and their families. I hope you will choose it.

Sincerely,
A Concerned Student
