Dear Principal Morales,

I am writing about the proposal to require community service from every student. I think you should adopt it, with a modest number of hours and a lot of freedom in how we complete them.

My main reason is that most students will never try service unless something pushes them. Last spring my friend Dana signed up to tutor second graders at Lincoln Elementary only because her older sister dragged her along. She expected to be bored. Instead she spent ten weeks helping a boy named Marcus sound out words, and by May he was reading chapter books to her. Dana now talks about becoming a teacher. She would not have discovered that on her own, and I suspect many of us are in the same position.

The community benefits too, and in ways we can see. A group from my neighborhood picks up litter along Creek Road one Saturday a month. Before they started, the ditch was full of bottles and fast food bags. Now families walk there. Our school has about nine hundred students. If each gave only twenty hours a year, that would be eighteen thousand hours of tutoring, cleaning, and visiting nursing homes in a town that cannot afford to pay for those things.

I know the objection, because I hear it from my own friends. They have jobs, sports, younger siblings to watch, and homework that already keeps them up late. That concern is fair, and it is why the details of the rule matter. Twenty hours across a school year comes to about half an hour a week. Students should be allowed to finish the hours over the summer, and the school should count a wide range of activities, including helping at a church food pantry or coaching a youth soccer team. A student who babysits siblings every afternoon could do one weekend project and be finished.

Some people say that forced service is not real service. I disagree. We are required to take math, and nobody claims that what we learn in it is fake. A requirement only gets us through the door. What we find inside is up to us.

I hope you will approve the requirement and keep it flexible.

Sincerely,
A ninth grade student
