Dear Principal,

I am writing to urge you to adopt Policy 1, which lets students bring phones to school and use them during lunch and other free times, provided the phones stay off during class. A complete ban may seem like the simpler choice, but it solves the wrong problem and creates new ones.

First, students today rely on phones for things that matter beyond entertainment. Many carry a phone so they can reach a parent after a bus is cancelled, a practice or activity ends late, or a family emergency arises. Under Policy 2, those students would have no reliable way to make contact during the school day, and the office would be flooded with requests to pass messages along. Policy 1 keeps that line of communication open without interfering with learning.

Second, the phone-free rule in class already addresses the main concern. Requiring phones to be off during instruction removes the distraction teachers worry about most, while lunch and passing periods remain a time for students to rest and socialize. Free time is valuable, and a student who spends lunch on a phone is not necessarily doing worse than one who spends it on a chess board or a game of cards.

Third, a total ban is hard to enforce. Students who are used to carrying phones will keep them in backpacks, hide them in jackets, or use them in bathrooms, where supervision is weakest. Staff time would go toward confiscating devices and resolving disputes rather than supporting students. A clear, limited rule that students can actually follow is easier to enforce consistently than a rule that most of them will break.

Finally, a policy that allows phones within limits teaches responsibility. Students learn to manage their own devices and to switch them off when it is time to focus, a skill they will need in college and at work. A ban removes that opportunity and leaves students unprepared for a world where phones are always present.

For these reasons, I believe Policy 1 is the better choice. It keeps students connected, protects instructional time, is practical to enforce, and helps students build self-control. I would be glad to help draft guidelines or talk with teachers about how the policy could work in practice.

Respectfully,

A Concerned Student
