Dear Principal,

I believe students should be required to perform community service, and I hope you will consider making it a graduation requirement. Helping others teaches lessons that no classroom can fully deliver.

Consider the friends who tutor elementary school children. They learn patience, explain ideas they once found difficult, and discover that they are capable of teaching. Students who clean up litter along a city park learn that their actions affect the places where others live and play. These experiences build responsibility and empathy, qualities that employers and colleges value.

I understand the concern that service takes time away from homework, sports, jobs, and rest. However, a requirement of perhaps twenty hours spread across four years is modest. Students already spend hours on activities that serve only themselves, and a small commitment to others can broaden their perspective without overwhelming their schedules. Many students also find that the work they expected to dislike becomes the most meaningful part of their year.

To make the requirement fair, the school could offer a wide range of options, from tutoring and food drives to environmental cleanups and hospital volunteering, so that every student can find a placement that fits their interests and abilities.

Thank you for considering this proposal. I believe that a community is strengthened when its young people learn to give back, and our school is the right place to begin that lesson.

Sincerely,
A Concerned Student
