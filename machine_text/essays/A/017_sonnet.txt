Dear Principal,

I am writing to urge you to adopt Policy 1, which lets students bring phones to school and use them at lunch and during other free times, as long as they are turned off during class. I believe this approach protects learning while treating students fairly.

First, a total ban would be very hard to enforce. Almost every student owns a phone, and many would hide them in backpacks or pockets anyway. Teachers and staff would then spend their time searching for phones and handing out punishments instead of teaching. Under Policy 1, the rule is simple and clear: phones off in class, free to use outside of it. Rules that students see as reasonable are the ones they actually follow.

Second, phones are a real safety tool. Many of us rely on them to reach parents after practice, to arrange rides, or to report an emergency. If a student feels sick or a bus is late, a quick call can solve the problem right away. A complete ban would cut off that connection for the entire school day, and parents who worry about their children might object strongly.

Third, free time is when students need to relax. Lunch is a break from hard work, and being allowed to text a friend, listen to music, or check messages helps us return to class calm and ready to focus. Students who are given some trust tend to show more responsibility in return. Policy 1 teaches us how to manage technology, which is a skill we will need long after we graduate.

Some people argue that phones distract students. That is a fair concern, and it is exactly why Policy 1 requires them to be off during class. This keeps lessons protected while still allowing sensible use during breaks. If a student breaks the rule, the school can take the phone for the day, which gives the policy real consequences.

For these reasons, I believe Policy 1 is the better choice. It keeps classrooms focused, keeps students safe, and shows that our school trusts us to act maturely. Thank you for considering my views.

Sincerely,
A Concerned Student
