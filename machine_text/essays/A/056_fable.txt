When my cousin broke her leg in tenth grade, she missed almost five weeks of school. Her teachers sent home packets of worksheets, but nobody explained the chemistry she was missing, and by the time she came back she was so far behind that she ended up retaking the class over the summer. If her school had let her join classes by video, she would have lost a few days instead of a whole semester. Stories like hers are the main reason I believe students would benefit from having the option to attend classes from home.

The key word is option. I am not arguing that every student should sit in front of a laptop all year. I am arguing that the choice should exist for the students who need it. Some kids deal with long illnesses or injuries. Others live an hour's bus ride from the nearest high school, which means two hours a day spent staring out a window. Some have anxiety so severe that walking into a crowded cafeteria makes it hard to think about anything else. For these students, the alternative to learning at home is often not learning at school. It is not learning at all.

Distance learning also lets students work in a way that fits them. In a regular classroom, the teacher explains something once and moves on. Online lessons are frequently recorded, so a student who did not understand a math problem can watch the explanation again instead of pretending to get it. A student who works a part-time job to help the family can finish assignments in the evening. Small schools could even offer classes they cannot staff themselves, like a third year of a foreign language or an advanced physics course, by connecting students to a teacher somewhere else.

People who oppose this idea usually say that students at home will get distracted or lonely, and they have a point. It takes discipline to pay attention when your bed and your phone are three feet away, and you cannot really make friends through a screen. But that argument only shows that online classes are wrong for some students, not that they should be unavailable to everyone. Schools can require attendance checks, cameras on during discussions, and a minimum grade to stay in the program. A student who starts failing can be brought back into the building.

School is supposed to be about learning, not about which chair you sit in. Giving students a way to attend from home would keep more of them learning when life gets in the way.
