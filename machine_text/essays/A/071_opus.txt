Dear Principal,

I am writing about the proposal that students would need at least a B average to play sports or join other activities. I understand why you want it. You want students to take their classes seriously, and so do I. Still, I believe this policy would hurt more students than it helps, and I ask you not to adopt it.

First, activities are often the reason a struggling student keeps coming to school. My friend on the soccer team has a C average. Some weeks practice is the only part of the day he looks forward to, and his coach checks on his grades more often than anyone else does. If you take the team away from him, you take away the adult who pushes him hardest. He would not suddenly have more time for homework. He would have more time to give up.

Second, a C is a passing grade. Many students work very hard just to earn it, especially in subjects that are difficult for them or while they hold jobs or look after younger siblings. Telling these students they are not good enough to join the band or the robotics club punishes effort along with results. Since many students here have a C average, the policy would empty out teams and clubs that whole groups of students depend on.

Third, activities teach things report cards do not measure. In the drama club I learned to show up on time, speak in front of people, and work with people I would not have picked. Employers and colleges care about those skills. Students with lower grades may need that practice most of all.

I do think the school should support academics, and there are better ways to do it. Students below a C could be required to attend a study hall before practice. Coaches and club advisers could receive grade reports and meet with students who are slipping. Older team members could tutor younger ones. These ideas keep students connected while still asking them to improve.

Please keep the current policy and look for ways to help students raise their grades without shutting them out. Thank you for asking for our opinions.

Sincerely,
A concerned student
