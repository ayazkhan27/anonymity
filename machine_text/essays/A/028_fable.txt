Dear Principal Morales,

I am writing about the proposal to require community service from every student. I think you should not require it, even though I do community service myself and would keep doing it either way.

On Tuesdays I tutor two third graders in reading at the elementary school down the road. I started because my neighbor asked me to, and I stayed because one of the boys finally read a whole chapter book out loud in March and looked up at me like he had won something. Nobody assigned me those hours. That is the reason they work. The kids can tell I want to be there, and I can tell when another tutor is only waiting for the clock.

If service becomes a requirement, a lot of students will treat it the way they treat any other requirement. They will find the easiest way to get the signature. I have already seen this with the hours some clubs ask for. People sign in at a park cleanup, pick up a few bottles, and leave after the photo. The park is not much cleaner, and the students learn that helping is paperwork.

There is also the problem of time. My friend Dara works at her uncle's restaurant four nights a week because her family needs the money. Another friend watches his little sisters until his mother gets home at seven. They are already helping people, just not in a way that would count on a form. Adding twenty or thirty required hours would come out of their sleep or their homework, and they are the students who can least afford that.

I do think the school should make service easier to start. You could give us a list of places that need help, let groups present at an assembly, and offer a ride to the elementary school after the last bell. You could put volunteer hours on our transcripts so colleges see them. I think more students would sign up than you expect, and the ones who did would mean it.

Please keep community service a choice.

Sincerely,
A tenth grade student
