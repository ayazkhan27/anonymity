Dear Principal,

I am writing to urge you not to require all students to perform community service, although I believe service is valuable and should be strongly encouraged.

My friends who tutor elementary school children get a lot out of it. One of them says that watching a second grader finally sound out a hard word is the best part of her week. Another friend who cleans up litter along the creek behind our school feels proud when he sees the difference. These experiences work because the students chose them. They care about the kids and the creek, so they show up on time and put in real effort.

A requirement would change that. Some of my other friends work after school to help pay family bills, and others care for younger siblings or spend hours on sports and homework. For them, a mandatory service quota would be one more obligation squeezed into an already full day. Students who resent an assignment tend to do the minimum, such as signing a sheet and leaving early. That helps no one and could turn them against volunteering for good.

A better approach is to make service easy and appealing. The school could host sign-up fairs with local organizations, offer flexible weekend and lunchtime projects, and recognize volunteers at assemblies. Many more students would join if they felt invited rather than forced.

Thank you for considering my view.

Sincerely,
A concerned student
