I agree with my principal's decision that every student should take part in at least one extracurricular activity. School teaches us facts, but activities outside the classroom teach us how to work with people, manage time, and discover what we care about.

First, extracurricular activities build teamwork. On a soccer team, a player learns that winning depends on passing the ball and trusting others. On the yearbook staff, writers, photographers, and editors must meet deadlines together or the book will not be finished. These are the same skills adults need at work.

Second, activities help students manage their time. When I joined the debate club, I had practice three afternoons a week. At first I worried my grades would drop, but instead I learned to plan my homework and stop wasting evenings. Many students find that a busy schedule actually makes them more focused.

Finally, activities help students find their interests. A shy student might join the drama club and discover a talent for acting, or serve on the student council and realize she wants to study law.

Some people say students are already too busy, but one activity is a small requirement with large rewards. For these reasons, I support the principal's decision.
