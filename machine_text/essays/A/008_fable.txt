Dear Principal,

I am writing about the cell phone policy you are reconsidering. I believe Policy 1 is the better choice. Students should be allowed to bring phones to school and use them at lunch and during free time, as long as the phones stay off in class.

My first reason is safety. A lot of students walk home, ride the city bus, or stay late for practice. Last winter my basketball practice ended forty minutes early because the gym heater broke, and I had to call my mom to pick me up. Without a phone I would have stood outside in the cold until five thirty. The office phone is not a real solution, because the office closes before most practices let out.

Second, banning phones completely would be very hard to enforce. Students will bring them anyway and hide them in backpacks, lockers, and pockets. Teachers would have to spend their time searching for phones and writing referrals instead of teaching. A rule that most students break every day teaches us that rules do not matter. Policy 1 is a rule we can actually follow, since it asks for something reasonable.

Third, Policy 1 still protects class time, which is the main thing a phone policy needs to do. Phones would be off during lessons, so nobody is texting under a desk or distracting the person next to them. If a student breaks that rule, the teacher can take the phone until the end of the day. That consequence is fair, and students would understand it.

Finally, lunch and free periods are our time to relax. Checking a message or listening to music for twenty minutes does not hurt anyone's grades. It also gives us practice at deciding when using a phone is appropriate, which is a skill we will need in high school and at work.

I hope you will choose Policy 1. It keeps students safe, keeps classrooms focused, and treats us as people who can be responsible.

Sincerely,
A student
