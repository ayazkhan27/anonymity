Dear Principal,

I am writing to urge you to adopt Policy 1, which would let students bring phones to school and use them during lunch and other free periods, as long as the phones stay off during class. A complete ban may look like the simpler choice, but it asks students to leave a tool they rely on at the door and gives them no practice using it well.

First, phones are part of how students stay in touch with families. Many students have parents who work late, siblings at other schools, or a ride home that changes at the last minute. A phone lets a student confirm a pickup, reach a parent after an emergency, or let family know they are safe. Under Policy 2, those students would have no reliable way to reach home during the school day.

Second, a ban does not remove phones from campus. It only pushes them out of sight. Students would hide devices in bags, bathrooms, and lockers, and the time and energy spent on that concealment would be far worse than a few minutes of scrolling at lunch. Policy 1 turns the issue into a clear rule that is easy to enforce: phones away during class, free use outside it.

Third, free time is when students are most likely to socialize, read the news, or check a schedule. Those are ordinary, healthy habits. Keeping phones in pockets during lunch lets students decide how to spend a break, and it treats them as responsible young people rather than as suspects.

Finally, schools should teach students to manage their devices, not simply hide the problem. Policy 1 gives teachers a chance to set phone expectations in class, to talk with students about screen habits, and to step in when a phone becomes a distraction. A total ban ends that conversation before it starts.

For these reasons, I believe Policy 1 is the better choice. It keeps students connected to their families, it is easier to enforce than a ban that students will work around, and it prepares students to use technology with judgment. I hope you will consider it.

Respectfully,

A Student
