Dear Principal,

I am writing to share my view on the proposed policy that would bar students from sports and other activities unless they maintain at least a grade B average. I understand the concern behind this idea. We all want students to succeed in the classroom, and I know that grades matter. However, I believe this policy would hurt the very students it is meant to help, and I urge you not to adopt it.

My first reason is that activities often keep students engaged with school. Many students who struggle academically are the same students who feel least connected to the building. For them, a team practice or a club meeting may be the one part of the day they look forward to. When we take that away, we do not give them more time to study. We give them more reasons to stay home, skip class, and feel that school has no place for them. A policy meant to raise grades could end up lowering attendance and effort across the board.

My second reason concerns who would be affected. Right now a large number of students have a grade C average. Under this rule, most of them would be removed from teams, bands, and clubs. That is not a small group. It would mean a substantial share of the student body losing access to something that builds confidence, friendship, and responsibility. These students are not failures. Many of them work part-time jobs, care for younger siblings, or simply learn more slowly in some subjects. Shutting them out of activities does not address those problems. It just makes their school days harder.

Third, activities teach lessons that a report card does not measure. A student on the volleyball team learns to show up on time, to support teammates, and to recover from a bad game. A student in the debate club learns to prepare and to speak under pressure. These habits carry into the classroom. In many cases, the discipline a student gains from practice helps raise grades rather than hurting them. Removing the activity removes that discipline at the moment a student may need it most.

Some will argue that a B average requirement sends a clear message that academics come first. I agree that academics should come first. But a policy does not have to exclude students in order to make that point. The school could instead offer tutoring or study hall to any student whose grades drop, and require those students to attend while keeping them on the team or in the club. This approach keeps the standard high while giving struggling students a path to improve rather than a door closing behind them. It asks the school to invest in support, which is more fair than simply punishing those who are behind.

Finally, I would ask that the principal consider how this policy would look to families. Parents who already feel that their children are judged harshly may see this rule as one more way the school has given up on students who are not at the top of the class. Trust between the school and its community is hard to build and easy to lose. A policy that removes students from activities based on a single number may damage that trust in ways that are difficult to repair.

For these reasons, I believe the school should not require a grade B average to participate in sports or other activities. Instead, I hope the school will focus on helping students who are struggling, keep them connected to the activities that motivate them, and measure success by how much each student grows. Thank you for asking for student input on this important question. I would be glad to discuss these ideas further or to help organize a meeting where other students can share their views.

Respectfully,

A Student
