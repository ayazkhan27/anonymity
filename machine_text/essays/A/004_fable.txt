Dear Principal,

I heard that you are thinking about changing the cell phone policy, and I would like to ask you to choose Policy 1. Letting students bring phones and use them at lunch and during free time, while keeping them off in class, is the fairer and more practical choice.

My first reason is safety and family communication. Many of us do not go straight home after school. I have practice until five on some days, and my mother works a shift that changes every week. If practice is cancelled or my ride falls through, I need a way to tell her. The office phone works, but there is one line and a few hundred students. Parents also want to reach us directly. A total ban would not remove that need. It would only make every changed plan harder to handle.

My second reason is that Policy 2 would be very hard to enforce. Phones are small, and students who want them will keep them in a pocket or a locker. Teachers would have to spend time searching, confiscating, and arguing instead of teaching. A rule that most people quietly break teaches students that rules do not matter. Policy 1 asks for something reasonable, so most students will follow it, and teachers can save their energy for the few who do not.

Third, Policy 1 protects class time just as well. The real problem with phones is distraction during lessons, and both policies deal with that. Under Policy 1 a phone that is seen or heard in class can still be taken away until the end of the day. Students would have a strong reason to obey, because breaking the rule would cost them a privilege they actually have.

Finally, learning when to put a phone away is a skill we will need. In a job or at college, nobody will lock our phones up for us. We will be expected to turn them off in a meeting and check them on a break. Policy 1 lets us practice that now, with adults nearby to correct us when we get it wrong.

I understand that you want students paying attention, and I want that too. Policy 1 gives you quiet classrooms without taking away something families rely on. I hope you will consider it.

Sincerely,
A student
