Dear Principal,

I am writing to argue against the proposed policy that would require at least a grade B average for students to take part in sports and other activities. While I understand the goal of encouraging academic effort, this policy would hurt the students it is meant to help.

First, many students who struggle in the classroom find a sense of belonging on a team or in a club. Those activities give them a reason to come to school, to stay engaged, and to build friendships with adults and peers who support them. If we take that away, students with C averages may drop out emotionally, and their grades could fall even further.

Second, sports and activities teach skills that grades do not measure. Teamwork, discipline, leadership, and time management are learned through practice and competition. Students who are barred from these experiences lose the chance to grow in ways that will matter after graduation.

Finally, the policy would punish students for struggling rather than helping them improve. A better approach would be tutoring, study hall, or mentoring for students who are falling behind, so they can raise their grades while keeping the activities that motivate them.

For these reasons, I respectfully ask that the school keep sports and activities open to all students.

Sincerely,
A Concerned Student
