Dear Principal,

I am writing about your decision on whether to require every student to perform community service. I believe our school should make it a requirement, but a flexible one that respects how busy students already are.

Some of my friends already volunteer, and I have watched what it does for them. My friend Maya tutors second graders in reading every Tuesday after school. At first she said she only signed up because her mother made her. Now she talks about her students constantly, and she brags when one of them finishes a chapter book. She has become more patient and more confident speaking in front of people. Another friend, Daniel, joined a weekend group that cleans litter out of the creek behind the shopping center. He told me he never noticed how much trash was in our town until he spent a morning pulling bottles and plastic bags out of the water. Now he picks up garbage even when nobody is watching. Experiences like these teach responsibility in a way a classroom lecture cannot.

I also understand why other students are against the idea. Many of us have jobs, sports practice, music lessons, or younger siblings to watch at home. Homework already takes hours every night. If the school demanded fifty hours a semester, some students would fall behind or lose sleep, and the service would feel like a punishment instead of a chance to help. That would defeat the whole purpose.

That is why I think the requirement should be reasonable. Perhaps twenty hours a year would be enough, and students could choose when and how to complete them. Someone who works on weekdays could help at a food bank on Saturday. An athlete could coach a youth team during the off-season. The school could even count activities students already do, like helping at a church event or reading to patients at the hospital. Giving students choices would make the hours feel meaningful rather than forced.

Requiring service would also help students who would never volunteer on their own simply because they do not know where to start. Many people discover they enjoy helping only after they try it.

Our community gives a lot to us through parks, libraries, and teachers. A small, flexible requirement would teach students to give something back. I hope you will consider it.

Sincerely,
A concerned student
