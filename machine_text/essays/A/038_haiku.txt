Dear Principal,

I believe that all students should be required to perform community service. Helping others is one of the most valuable lessons a young person can learn, and school is the right place to teach it.

Some students argue that service takes time away from homework, sports, or part-time jobs. I understand that concern, and I know many students already have full schedules. However, a few hours a month is a small investment compared with the benefits it returns to both the students and the community around them.

Consider the students who tutor elementary school children. A high school junior who spends an hour each week helping a first grader sound out words is doing far more than volunteering. She is building patience, learning how to explain ideas clearly, and discovering that she can make a real difference in someone's life. Many of these students would never have chosen to do this on their own, but once they begin, they often find it rewarding.

Litter cleanup offers a different kind of lesson. When a group of students spends a Saturday morning picking up trash in a neighborhood park, they see the direct results of their effort. They also learn to work as a team and to take pride in the spaces they share. Neighbors notice, and the community becomes a place where young people are seen as contributors rather than problems.

A required program also makes service fair. When only motivated students volunteer, the same few people carry the load while others never participate. Requiring service ensures that every student gains experience with the needs of people outside their own circle, regardless of background or interest.

I recommend that the school require 20 hours of community service per year, with students free to choose from a list of approved local organizations. The school can also help students who struggle with transportation or scheduling find options that fit their lives. This approach respects students' time while still giving them a meaningful experience.

Helping the community is not just a favor to others. It shapes who we become. I urge you to make community service a required part of our school's education.

Respectfully,

A Concerned Student
