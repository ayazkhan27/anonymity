I agree with the principal's decision to require every student to take part in at least one extracurricular activity. Some students may complain at first, but the requirement would help them build skills, make friends, and feel more connected to their school.

First, extracurricular activities teach lessons that a classroom cannot. A student on the soccer team learns to show up to practice on time, push through when tired, and accept a loss without giving up. A student who works on the yearbook learns to meet deadlines, take photographs, interview people, and edit other people's writing. These skills matter in real jobs later. Employers and colleges look for people who can work on a team and finish what they start, and activities give students a chance to practice exactly that.

Second, activities help students find friends they might never have met. In a large school, it is easy to sit with the same small group every day. When a shy student joins the drama club or the robotics team, she is suddenly working beside people from different grades and backgrounds who share an interest with her. My cousin barely spoke to anyone during his first year of high school until he joined the chess club. Within a month he had a group of friends, and his grades even improved because he was happier coming to school.

Third, a requirement gives students a push to try something new. Many students would enjoy an activity but never sign up because they are nervous or assume they will not be good at it. If everyone must choose something, that fear matters less, since all their classmates are trying too. A student who reluctantly joins student council might discover a talent for leadership and public speaking that shapes the rest of his life.

Some people argue that students are already busy with homework, jobs, or family duties. That is a fair concern, but the principal's rule only asks for one activity, and schools offer options that meet once a week or during lunch. The school could also count responsibilities such as volunteering or helping at home in special cases.

Overall, requiring one extracurricular activity is a small demand with large rewards. It builds character, creates friendships, and opens doors students did not know existed. I hope others will support the principal's decision.
