Drivers should not be allowed to use cell phones while operating a vehicle, with one narrow exception for hands-free navigation and emergency calls.

Driving demands full attention. A car moving at sixty miles per hour covers the length of a football field in about four seconds, which is roughly how long it takes to glance down and read a text. In that time a child can step into the street or traffic can stop suddenly. Studies of crash data consistently show that distracted driving kills thousands of people every year, and phones are one of the leading causes.

Some argue that hands-free calling solves the problem. It helps, but it does not remove the mental distraction. A driver deep in a tense conversation is still thinking about the call, not the road. Texting, scrolling, and watching videos are even worse, because they take the eyes, hands, and mind away from driving all at once.

Phones do have real value in a car. GPS directions keep drivers from getting lost, and calling 911 after an accident can save lives. These uses should stay legal if the phone is mounted and operated by voice.

Everything else can wait. No message is worth a life.
