Dear Principal,

I am writing to share my thoughts on whether students should be required to perform community service. I believe they should, as long as the requirement is reasonable and gives students real choices.

Some of my friends already volunteer, and I have seen what it does for them. One friend tutors second graders in reading every Thursday afternoon. At first she did it only because her club needed hours, but now she talks about her students constantly and has started planning lessons on her own. Another friend joined a weekend group that picks up litter along the creek behind our school. He says he never noticed how much trash was there until he started cleaning it, and now he refuses to leave a wrapper on the ground. Neither of them would have discovered these interests if nobody had pushed them to try.

I understand why other friends object. Many of us have sports practice, part-time jobs, or family duties, and homework already fills our evenings. A requirement could feel like one more burden. But this concern can be solved with good planning rather than by dropping the idea. The school could ask for a modest number of hours spread across the year, perhaps ten hours each semester, and let students count work they already do, such as helping at a church food drive or caring for a younger sibling's classmates. Flexibility would keep the rule fair for busy students.

A requirement also teaches lessons that textbooks cannot. Students learn to show up on time, work with people unlike themselves, and see that their effort matters to someone else. Those habits will help us in college and in any job. Meanwhile, the community gains energy and help it would not otherwise have, from clean parks to children who read better.

For these reasons, I urge you to require community service with flexible hours and many options. Students may grumble at first, but I believe most will come away proud of what they have done.

Sincerely,
A Concerned Student
