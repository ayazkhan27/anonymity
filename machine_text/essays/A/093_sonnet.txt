I agree with the principal's decision that every student should take part in at least one extracurricular activity. School is about more than passing tests, and activities teach lessons that classrooms often cannot.

First, activities help students build friendships. Many teenagers spend the day moving between classes, sitting beside the same few people. When I joined the yearbook staff last year, I started working with juniors and seniors I would never have met otherwise. We spent weeks arguing over page layouts and laughing over bad photos, and by spring those people felt like family. A shy student who is required to join a club has a real chance to find a group where he belongs.

Second, activities teach practical skills. Serving on the student council means running meetings, planning a budget, and persuading classmates to support an idea. Playing a sport teaches discipline, because you must show up to practice even when you are tired. Skills like time management, teamwork, and leadership look good on college applications, but more importantly they matter in every job a student will ever hold.

Some people argue that a requirement is unfair to students who already have heavy homework or who must work after school. That concern is fair, but the rule only asks for one activity, and the choices are wide. A student with little time could join a club that meets once a week, such as chess or recycling. Schools can also help by offering activities during lunch or right after the final bell.

Finally, students who are involved tend to care more about school. They have a reason to come every day, and they feel connected to something larger than themselves. For these reasons, I believe the requirement will make our school stronger and our students more confident, and I hope everyone will support it.
