Cell phones have become so woven into daily life that many people reach for them without thinking, including behind the wheel. Yet drivers should not be permitted to use cell phones in any capacity while operating a vehicle. Driving demands full attention, and a phone competes for that attention in ways that are easy to underestimate.

Even hands-free calls require the driver to listen, speak, and mentally process a conversation. Research on distracted driving has repeatedly shown that this cognitive load slows reaction times and reduces awareness of traffic, pedestrians, and changing road conditions. Texting, scrolling, and checking notifications are more dangerous still, since they take the driver's eyes off the road and a hand off the wheel at the same time.

Some argue that a complete ban is unrealistic and that responsible drivers can manage a quick glance at a map or a message. But the problem is that distraction is unpredictable. A single second of inattention at highway speed can be the difference between a close call and a fatal collision. Laws that allow limited use also become difficult to enforce, because officers cannot easily tell whether a driver is dialing, listening, or reading.

A clear and comprehensive prohibition gives drivers a simple rule and protects everyone else on the road. Phones can wait until the vehicle is parked. The small inconvenience of waiting is far outweighed by the lives that such a rule would save.
