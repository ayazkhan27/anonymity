Dear Principal,

I am writing about whether our school should require every student to perform community service. I believe it should not be a requirement, but the school should strongly encourage it and make it easy to do.

My friends who volunteer are proud of it. One tutors third graders in reading after school, and she says the kids light up when a hard word finally clicks. Another joins a weekend litter cleanup at the creek near our neighborhood. They both feel that helping others matters, and I agree. Service teaches responsibility, patience, and a sense that we belong to something larger than ourselves.

However, a requirement would create problems. Many students already have very full schedules. Some work part-time jobs to help their families pay bills. Others care for younger siblings or play on teams that practice every day. For these students, a mandatory service quota would be one more demand on time they barely have. A friend of mine works at a grocery store four evenings a week. Telling him to find extra hours for service would only add stress and might hurt his grades.

There is also the question of motivation. When something is forced, many people do the bare minimum and watch the clock. A student who is made to sign a log sheet may learn to resent volunteering instead of valuing it. Service that comes from a person's own choice usually means more, both to the student and to the people being helped.

Instead of a requirement, I suggest the school create better opportunities. We could hold a volunteer fair where local groups, such as food banks, libraries, and animal shelters, explain what they need. We could offer service during lunch or on school grounds so busy students can take part without extra travel. We could also recognize volunteers with certificates or a mention at assemblies. When students see their classmates enjoying service and being honored for it, more will want to join.

I believe this approach will bring more students into service willingly, and with better attitudes, than a rule ever could. Thank you for considering my opinion.

Sincerely,
A Concerned Student
