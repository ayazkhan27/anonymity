Dear Principal,

I believe students should be required to perform community service. Service to others teaches lessons that no classroom can fully deliver, and a school has a responsibility to prepare young people for life beyond its walls. Requiring service makes that preparation available to every student, not only to the few who already volunteer.

Consider the example of students who tutor elementary school children. A high school student who spends an hour each week helping a first grader sound out words learns patience, communication, and responsibility. The younger child gains a mentor who shows up, and the tutor learns what it means to be depended upon. Both gain something that a worksheet or a lecture could never provide.

Consider also the students who clean up litter in a neighborhood park or along a city street. Such work may seem simple, but it teaches students that the spaces they share are their responsibility too. Residents notice a cleaner park, and students begin to see themselves as people who can improve their surroundings. Many of these students will never again look at a piece of trash on the sidewalk in quite the same way.

I understand the concern of students who feel that service takes time away from homework, sports, jobs, or family obligations. These concerns are real, and the requirement should be designed with them in mind. A modest commitment, such as ten or fifteen hours over a school year, is enough to build habits without overwhelming a busy schedule. The school can also offer flexible options, including weekend projects, activities during school hours, and service connected to classes the students already take.

Some will argue that service should be a choice, since forced volunteering may not produce genuine commitment. Yet many students who begin service out of obligation discover that they enjoy it, and some continue long after graduation. A requirement can open a door that students would otherwise never have walked through. Every student deserves the chance to find out what they are capable of giving to others.

For these reasons, I urge you to require community service for all students. The benefits to individual students and to the wider community are too important to leave to chance. With thoughtful design and reasonable time commitments, this requirement can help our school produce graduates who are capable, compassionate, and ready to contribute.

Respectfully,

A Concerned Student
