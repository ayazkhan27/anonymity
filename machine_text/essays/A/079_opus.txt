Dear Principal,

I am writing to ask you not to adopt the policy that would require a B average to take part in sports and other activities. I understand the goal of pushing students to work harder in class, but I believe this rule would hurt the very students it is meant to help.

First, many students at our school have a C average, and a C is still a passing grade. These students are doing the work and meeting the standard. Taking away the one thing that keeps some of them excited about school could make them care less about their classes, not more. For a lot of kids, practice after school is the reason they show up in the morning.

Second, sports and clubs teach lessons that do not show up on a report card. On the soccer team or in the drama club, students learn teamwork, responsibility, and how to handle losing. They learn to manage their time because they have to balance homework with practice. These skills matter for jobs and for life, and a student with a C needs them just as much as a student with an A.

Third, activities give students adults who care about them. Coaches and club advisors often notice when a student is struggling and push them to get help. If we remove those students from teams, we also remove that support.

Instead of a B requirement, I suggest keeping the C average rule and adding a tutoring program for players whose grades slip. That way, students stay involved while getting the help they need to improve.

Thank you for asking for our views. I hope you will keep activities open to every student who is passing.

Sincerely,
A Concerned Student
