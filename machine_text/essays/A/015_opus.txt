Dear Principal,

I know you are weighing two cell phone policies, and I want to make the case for Policy 1: let students bring phones and use them at lunch and during free times, as long as the phones stay off during class.

The first reason is safety. Many parents want to reach their children during the day, and students need a way to call home if practice is canceled, a ride falls through, or something goes wrong. A complete ban would take away that lifeline. In a real emergency, being able to contact family quickly matters a great deal.

Second, Policy 1 already protects the thing a ban is supposed to protect, which is learning time. If phones must be off during class, teachers keep students' attention where it belongs. A rule against using them at lunch adds nothing to what happens in the classroom. It only punishes students during time that belongs to them.

Third, a total ban would be hard to enforce. Students will still bring phones in their pockets and backpacks, and teachers would spend their energy searching for devices instead of teaching. A clear, reasonable rule is easier to follow, and students are more likely to respect it.

Finally, Policy 1 teaches responsibility. After graduation, nobody will take our phones away at work or college. We have to learn when to put them down. Giving students a chance to manage that at school prepares us for the real world.

For these reasons, I urge you to choose Policy 1. It keeps classrooms focused while respecting students' safety and independence.

Sincerely,
A Concerned Student
