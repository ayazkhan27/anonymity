Last winter my cousin missed three weeks of school with a broken leg, and by the time she came back she was lost in every class. If her school had offered lessons online, she could have kept up from the couch. I think students would benefit from being able to attend classes from home, as long as it stays an option and not a replacement.

The biggest reason is that life gets in the way of school. Students get sick, families move, buses break down, and snow closes roads. Right now each of those means a lost day. With a video class, a student with a mild fever can still hear the lesson without spreading germs to thirty other people.

Distance learning also helps students who learn better alone. Some of my classmates are too shy to ask questions in a loud room, but they will type one in a chat box. Recorded lessons can be paused and replayed, which a live teacher can't do when twenty other kids are waiting.

Of course there are problems. It is easy to get distracted at home, and not every family has fast internet or a quiet room. Students also need friends, and you can't really make those through a screen. That is why I would not want online classes every day.

Still, having the choice hurts no one. A school that offers both gives students a way to keep learning when showing up in person isn't possible.
