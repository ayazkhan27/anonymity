I agree with the principal's decision that every student should take part in at least one extracurricular activity. Some people worry it will take time away from homework or force students into things they do not enjoy. I think it will help students more than it hurts them, because activities teach skills that classes cannot, connect students to their school, and help them figure out what they care about.

First, extracurricular activities teach lessons that are hard to learn from a textbook. On a soccer team, a player learns that showing up to practice matters even when it is cold and nobody feels like running. A student working on the yearbook learns to meet deadlines, because the pages have to go to the printer on a certain date no matter what. Student council members learn how to speak in front of a group, listen to complaints, and compromise when not everyone agrees. These are the same skills employers and colleges look for, and many students would never practice them if they only went to class and went home.

Second, activities make students feel like they belong. A big school can feel lonely, especially for new students or shy ones. When you join the chess club or the drama crew, you suddenly have a group of people who expect to see you and notice when you are gone. My cousin moved to a new town in ninth grade and did not know anyone. She joined the cross country team only because a teacher pushed her to, and by October she had a whole group of friends to sit with at lunch. Students who feel connected to school are also more likely to keep their grades up and less likely to skip.

Third, a requirement helps students discover interests they did not know they had. Many teenagers never try something new because they are nervous or assume they will be bad at it. If everyone has to pick something, a student who thought he hated writing might find out he loves taking photos for the newspaper. That discovery could shape what he studies later.

Of course, the rule should be flexible. Students with jobs or family responsibilities should have options that fit their schedules, and there should be enough choices that everyone can find something. With that in mind, I believe the principal's decision is a good one, and I hope other students will support it.
