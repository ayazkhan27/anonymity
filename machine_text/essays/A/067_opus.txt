Dear Principal,

I am writing about the proposed policy that would require students to keep at least a B average before they can play sports or join other activities. I understand that you want students to take their classes seriously, and I agree that grades matter. Still, I believe this policy would hurt more students than it helps, and I urge you not to adopt it.

First, many students at our school have a C average, and a C is a passing grade. These students are doing the work required to move forward. Telling them they cannot join the soccer team, the band, or the debate club because they are not above average sends the message that passing is not good enough to belong. For a lot of students, these activities are the main reason they feel connected to school at all. If we take that connection away, some of them may stop trying in class rather than try harder.

Second, activities teach skills that grades do not always measure. On a team, students learn to show up on time, work with people they did not choose, and keep going after a loss. In the drama club or the school newspaper, students learn to meet deadlines and speak in front of others. These are the habits that help people succeed in jobs and in life. A student who struggles in algebra might be learning responsibility and leadership as captain of the track team. It would be a mistake to shut that door.

Third, sports and clubs often motivate students to improve their grades. Coaches and advisors check on their students, remind them to finish assignments, and sometimes arrange study time before practice. My friend on the basketball team raised his science grade from a D to a C last year because his coach would not let him slack off. Under the new rule, he would have been removed from the team before that improvement ever happened.

I do think there is a fair middle ground. The school could keep the current rule that students must be passing all their classes, and add support for students who are close to failing. For example, athletes and club members with low grades could be required to attend tutoring twice a week. This would push students to improve without taking away something that matters to them.

In conclusion, a B average requirement would exclude many hardworking students, remove a source of motivation, and take away valuable lessons that happen outside the classroom. Please keep activities open to every student who is passing, and help those who are struggling instead of benching them.

Sincerely,
A Concerned Student
