Dear Principal,

I am writing to share my view on the proposed policy that would require a B average for students to take part in sports and other activities. I understand the goal of encouraging good grades, but I believe this policy would hurt more students than it would help.

First, many students have a C average, and a C is a passing grade. These students are doing acceptable work, yet they would be shut out of the teams and clubs they love. Sports and activities are often the reason a student wants to come to school at all. If we take those away, some students may lose their motivation and their grades could fall even further.

Second, activities teach skills that classrooms cannot. Playing on a team teaches cooperation and discipline. Working on the yearbook or in the drama club teaches responsibility and time management. A student who struggles in math may still be a gifted musician or a strong leader on the field. Cutting these students off from activities ignores their talents.

Third, activities can actually help students improve. Many athletes keep their schedules organized because they know practice follows school. Coaches and advisors also check on their students and encourage them to study. Without that support, struggling students may have no one pushing them.

Instead of a B average, I suggest a more reasonable standard, such as a C average, along with free tutoring for students who fall behind. This would still send the message that grades matter, but it would give students help rather than punishment.

I hope you will consider this view as you make your decision. Thank you for listening to the students.

Sincerely,
A concerned student
