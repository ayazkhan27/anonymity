Learning From Home Should Be a Choice

Most people think of school as desks in rows, a whiteboard, and a packed hallway between classes. For a lot of students that works fine. For some students though, getting to that classroom every day is a real problem. I think students should be allowed to take classes from home over video or online, as long as it is an option and not something everyone is forced to do.

The biggest reason is that not every student's life fits the normal school schedule. Some teenagers have part-time jobs because their family needs the money for rent. Some have to watch their little brothers and sisters or take care of a sick grandparent. Athletes who travel for tournaments can miss a few days in a row. For students like this, online classes aren't just a nice extra, they could decide whether they keep up or fall behind. If they can watch a recorded lesson or join a live class, they still learn something on a day they would have just been marked absent.

It would also help students who are sick or have disabilities. If someone is recovering from surgery, or has a weak immune system, they might not be able to come to school for weeks. Instead of losing the whole semester they could log on and still be part of the class. Students with bad anxiety might also do better at home where it is calmer, because they could pay attention to the lesson instead of everything stressing them out around them.

Another reason is that students could take classes their school doesn't have. A small school out in the country might not have enough kids to hire a teacher for advanced physics or Japanese. With video classes, a student there could join a class at a different school or even a college. That way where you live doesn't limit what you can learn as much.

Some people say students at home will get distracted, and that they will miss out on the social part of school like friends, clubs, and learning how to work with people in person. I agree these are real problems. Not everyone has the self-discipline to learn by themselves, and not every house has a quiet room or good wifi. But that just means schools need to set up online learning carefully, it doesn't mean they should get rid of it. Teachers can check in with students at home a lot, schools can lend out laptops and hotspots, and remote students can still be encouraged to come to clubs and sports in person when they can.

Students are all different, and one way of doing school is never going to work for all of them. Letting students choose to learn from home respects that and keeps more of them learning. Having both options doesn't make the classroom worse. It just makes sure nobody gets left out of it.
