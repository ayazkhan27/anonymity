Summer Projects Should Belong to Students

A lot of schools give students a summer project in June so they don't forget everything over break. I get why. If you go ten weeks without reading or writing or doing any math, you come back in the fall and it feels like half of last year is gone. So I'm not against summer projects. My question is who should get to design them. I think students should design their own projects, and teachers should just set the basic expectations and help out when it's needed. When students pick the project they actually put effort into it, they learn skills school doesn't really teach, and the learning feels like it belongs to them.

The biggest reason is motivation. Think about the usual summer assignment, like a report on a book the teacher picked. Most people I know do it the night before school starts and hate every minute of it. Summer is the only time all year when we get to control our own time, so anything that feels like extra homework loses to pretty much everything else. A project you picked yourself is different. Say a girl loves baking. She could look into the chemistry of why bread rises, try different amounts of yeast, and write down what happens. Or a guy who is obsessed with basketball could track his free throw percentage all summer and learn how to graph and analyze it. These come from things people already like, so they keep coming back to them without being forced, and that is the whole point of a summer project.

Second, planning a project is a lesson by itself. During the school year teachers decide almost everything for us, what we read, what questions we answer, when stuff is due. We almost never have to plan something long, set our own goals, or figure out what to do when something goes wrong. If a student has to come up with a question, figure out how to answer it, and keep the work going for weeks, that's the kind of independence college and jobs expect. If your garden experiment dies in July, you have to figure out why and try again. A worksheet can't teach you that.

Third, students aren't all the same. One assignment for the whole class assumes everybody needs the same practice and likes the same things, and that's just not true. One kid might really need to work on writing while another kid is ready for an advanced coding project. If students choose, each of them can work at their own level instead of the middle of the class.

Some people will say that if you let students choose, they'll pick something way too easy or just not do it. That's a fair point, but it means teachers should guide us, not that they should control everything. Teachers could make us turn in a short proposal before summer, set a standard for how much work counts, and have us present in the fall. That way we get freedom but we're still held accountable.

The whole reason summer projects exist is to keep us curious, and the best way to do that is to let students work on what they already care about.
