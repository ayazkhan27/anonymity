Cell phones are everywhere now, and a lot of people grab them without even thinking, even while they're driving. But drivers shouldn't be allowed to use cell phones at all while they're behind the wheel. Driving takes your full attention, and a phone pulls that attention away in ways a lot of people don't really notice.

Even hands-free calls are a problem. You still have to listen, talk, and keep up with the conversation in your head. Research on distracted driving has shown over and over that this kind of mental load slows down reaction time and makes drivers less aware of traffic, pedestrians, and changing road conditions. Texting, scrolling, and checking notifications are worse, because your eyes leave the road and one hand leaves the wheel.

Some people say a full ban isn't realistic and that responsible drivers can glance at a map or a text. The thing is, distraction doesn't come with a warning. One second of not paying attention at highway speed can turn a close call into a deadly crash. Partial laws are also hard to enforce, since cops can't easily tell if someone is dialing, listening, or reading.

A clear, total ban gives drivers one simple rule and protects everyone else on the road too. Phones can wait until the car is parked. Waiting is a small hassle compared to what a crash can cost, and it's worth it if the rule saves lives.
