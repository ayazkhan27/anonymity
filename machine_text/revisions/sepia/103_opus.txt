Phones and the Road

Pretty much everybody has a cell phone now, and a lot of us feel lost without one. But the worst place to use a phone is behind the wheel. I think drivers should not be allowed to hold or touch their phone at all while the car is moving. The only exception should be hands-free stuff like voice navigation, as long as you set it up before you start driving.

The biggest reason is attention. When you drive you need your eyes, your hands, and your brain all at once, and a text takes all three away. If you look down for five seconds on the highway, you go about the length of a football field without watching the road. In that time a little kid could step off the curb or the car in front of you could slam on its brakes. It doesn't matter how good of a driver you are, you can't react to something you never saw.

A lot of people say they can handle it because they only check messages at red lights or when the road is empty. The problem is habits don't stay where you put them. If you read texts at a stoplight, pretty soon you're reading them while you roll forward, and then while you merge. A simple rule like "the phone stays down" is easier to follow than a rule with a bunch of exceptions, and it's easier for police to enforce too.

Some people argue phones actually make driving safer. Maps keep you from getting lost and making sudden turns, and if you crash you can call for help. Those are fair points, so the rule should still allow a mounted phone with voice navigation and emergency calls. What it should ban is texting, scrolling, watching videos, and holding the phone up to your ear to talk.

Almost any call or text can wait ten minutes, but you can't get a life back. If a message is really that important, you can pull over, park, and answer it. Keeping phones out of drivers' hands is a small sacrifice, and it protects the driver, the passengers, and everyone else on the road.
