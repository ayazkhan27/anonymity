I agree with the principal that every student should have to do at least one extracurricular activity. I know a lot of kids are going to complain about it at first. But clubs and teams teach you stuff you don't really learn sitting in class, like how to make friends, how to be responsible, and what you're actually good at.

The first reason is that activities get you to meet people you normally wouldn't. Our school is big, and most people just hang out with the same few friends all day. If a quiet kid joins robotics or tries out for the school play, he ends up working with kids from other grades that he never would have talked to. My cousin was really shy in ninth grade until she joined cross country. She said running all those miles with her team gave them something to talk about, and by the end of the season she had a bunch of new friends. If everyone has to join something, then everyone gets that chance and not only the outgoing kids.

Another reason is responsibility. Homework teaches some of that, but not the same way. If you're on yearbook and you miss a deadline, the whole yearbook comes out late. If you skip soccer practice you aren't just hurting yourself, you're letting your team down. So you learn to manage your time and show up when people are counting on you. Colleges and jobs want people who can do that, and it's better to practice now when it isn't a big deal if you mess up.

Also, you might find out you like something you never tried before. Lots of people don't know they're into debate or painting or coding until they give it a shot. Say a girl is bad at math but then finds out on student council that she's a good leader. That could make her more confident in her classes too. Without the rule, a lot of kids would never try anything.

Some people will say students are already too busy with jobs or helping their families. That's true for some kids. But the rule is only one activity, and the school could have clubs that meet once a week or at lunch. That's not much to ask.

So I think the principal made the right call. One activity helps kids make friends, grow up a little, and figure out who they want to be.
