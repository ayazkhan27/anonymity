Phones Belong Out of the Driver's Hands

Almost everybody has a cell phone with them all the time now, and that includes when they are in the car. People use their phones for directions, for music, and to text their family or their boss. Since phones are so useful, a lot of drivers act like the phone is just another part of the car. I still think drivers should not hold or touch their phone at all while the car is moving. The only exception should be hands-free stuff that you set up before you leave, like a GPS route that talks to you.

The biggest reason is attention. Driving seems easy because people do it every day, but your brain is actually doing a lot. You have to watch the road, check your mirrors, figure out how fast the other cars are going, and react to things you don't expect, like a kid running into the street after a ball. Reading a text takes about five seconds. If you are going fifty-five miles per hour, the car goes about the length of a football field in that time. During those five seconds you are basically driving blind, and no text is worth that.

Some people say talking on the phone is the same as talking to someone sitting next to you. But research doesn't support that. A passenger can see the road too, so when traffic gets bad they usually stop talking or even tell you to watch out. The person on the phone has no idea what's going on, so they just keep talking, and the call keeps pulling your attention away at the exact moment you need it. Studies have even found that hands-free calls slow down your reaction time, because the distraction is in your head and not just in your hands.

Other people say phones are necessary now, especially for directions. I agree with that, and that is why I think there should be one exception. You can type the address in before you pull out of the driveway and let the phone tell you where to go from a mount on the dashboard. What you shouldn't do is type, scroll, or pick up the phone while the car is in drive. If you need to change the route, you can pull over. Spending thirty seconds in a parking lot doesn't really cost you anything.

It's also not fair to everyone else on the road. When someone decides to text and drive, they are not only risking their own life. People walking, people on bikes, and other drivers never agreed to that risk. Drunk driving is already against the law because drunk drivers put other people in danger, and distracted driving is dangerous in a similar way. Clear laws against holding your phone, with real punishments, would show people that the road belongs to everybody.

Phones aren't going away, and they do make life easier. But when you are behind the wheel, your job is to drive. Putting the phone down or turning on do-not-disturb until you get where you're going is a small sacrifice. Compared to the lives it could save, it barely counts as a sacrifice at all.
