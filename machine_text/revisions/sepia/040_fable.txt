My cousin broke her leg in tenth grade and missed almost six weeks of school. Her teachers sent packets home, but nobody explained the chemistry to her, and she spent the rest of that year trying to catch up. If her school had let her join class by video she would have lost very little. That is why I think students should be able to attend classes from home. I mean as a choice, though. It shouldn't be forced on everybody.

The students who would benefit most are the ones who can't get to school in the first place. Kids miss school because they are sick or hurt, because of a family emergency, or because of bad weather, and every day they miss makes the next one harder. Think about a student with the flu. By the third day he might feel well enough to listen to a lesson, but he shouldn't be sitting in a crowded room spreading it around. If he could watch from home he would keep up and everyone else would stay healthy. Rural students would gain even more. Some of them ride a bus for over an hour each way, which is two hours a day they could spend on homework or sleep or a job.

It would also help students who don't do well in a regular classroom. For some students with anxiety, a noisy building is exhausting. Others get distracted by classmates, and some get bullied in the hallways. At home they can just focus on the material. Recorded lessons make it even better, because if you don't understand a math problem the first time you can replay the explanation instead of pretending to follow along.

I know there are problems with this. A student at home can drift off or open another tab, and some would stop showing up at all. They would also miss the social side of school, like talking at lunch, group projects and sports. And not every family has fast internet or a quiet room. These are serious, but to me they are reasons to set the option up carefully. They are not reasons to refuse it. A school could require cameras during discussions and check in with its online students every week. It could lend out laptops and hotspots. If a student's grades fall, he could be moved back into the building.

Learning online is also a skill we are going to need later anyway. Many colleges run courses online, and many jobs involve video meetings and managing your own time. It is safer to practice that in high school, while teachers are still watching closely, than to learn it alone at eighteen.

Being in the building will still be the best fit for most students. But students who are sick, or live far away, or struggle in a crowd should be able to attend from home.
