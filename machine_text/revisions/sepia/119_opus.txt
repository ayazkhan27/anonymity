Keep Phones Out of Drivers' Hands

Pretty much everybody has a phone on them all the time now, and a lot of people keep checking it even when they're driving. So there's a big debate about whether drivers should be allowed to use phones at all while the car is moving. My opinion is that drivers shouldn't be holding or messing with their phones while they drive. The only exceptions should be calling 911 in an emergency and using hands-free GPS that you set up before you leave.

My first reason is that driving takes your full attention. It seems easy because people do it every day, but you are always paying attention to something. You have to figure out how fast the other cars are coming, watch the guy on the bike next to the road, and look out for a little kid who could run into the street chasing a ball. When you read a text, your eyes are off the road for a few seconds. On the highway your car goes about the length of a football field in that time. No one would drive that far with a blindfold on, but looking at your phone is basically the same thing.

Some people say hands-free calls fix the problem. They help some, but they don't fix it. Research on distracted driving shows that talking on the phone still takes your mind off the road even if your hands are on the wheel. It isn't the same as talking to a passenger, because the passenger can see the road too and usually stops talking when traffic gets bad. The person on the phone can't see anything, so they just keep talking while the driver stops paying attention. That's why I think even hands-free calls should only be short ones that you really need to make.

To be fair, the other side is right about GPS and emergencies. Most people use their phones instead of paper maps now, and following spoken directions is safer than squinting at street signs or trying to open a map on the steering wheel. Also, if there's a crash up ahead or someone is having a medical problem, a phone call could save a life. So a good rule would allow these, as long as you put in the address before you start driving and the phone is in a holder, not in your hand.

Everything else can wait. Texts, Instagram, picking songs, and email don't need an answer in the next five minutes, and no message is worth getting in a crash over. Tough laws, police actually enforcing them, and phone settings that block notifications while you drive would all help. But really it comes down to every driver deciding the road is more important than their phone.
