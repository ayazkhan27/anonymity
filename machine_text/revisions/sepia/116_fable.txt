Pretty much everyone I know has a phone, and pretty much everyone I know has looked at it while driving. I've done it too. It was at a red light and I told myself it didn't count because the car wasn't moving. That excuse is actually why I think the rule should be simple. Drivers should not be allowed to hold or handle a phone at all while they are operating a vehicle. The only uses that should be allowed are ones where your hands stay on the wheel and your eyes stay on the road.

My first reason is attention. Driving feels easy because most of the time nothing happens. Somebody can drive the same road to work for years and never get surprised once. But the real skill in driving is being ready for the one second when a little kid steps out between two parked cars, or the truck in front of you slams on its brakes. Reading a text takes your eyes off the road for several seconds, and on the highway a car goes about the length of a football field in that time. For those seconds the driver isn't really driving. The car is just moving, and everybody around it is trusting that nothing changes.

A lot of people say they are good at multitasking. I used to think that about myself. The problem is that when you are distracted you can't notice the things you miss. If I look down at my phone and nothing goes wrong, I decide I handled it fine. I never see the cyclist I would have hit on some other day. So every safe trip makes the habit stronger, and I get more confident even though there isn't any real evidence that I should be. A law against handheld phones takes that decision away from the person who is the worst at making it, which is the driver.

Another reason is who gets hurt. If texting and driving only put the person texting in danger, I could understand leaving it up to personal choice. But that isn't how it works. The pedestrian in the crosswalk never agreed to the risk and neither did the family in the next lane. We already think this way about drunk driving. Nobody says a driver has the right to decide for himself whether four beers affect him or not. A phone in your hand is a similar kind of impairment, and people choose it over and over again in one trip.

I don't think every single function should be banned, though. Phones have replaced paper maps, and a navigation app that says the directions out loud is probably safer than a lost driver squinting at street signs. Calling for help after a crash is a good use, and so is reporting a dangerous driver. If the phone is mounted on the dashboard, set up before the car leaves the driveway, and controlled by voice, that seems reasonable to me. Even hands-free calls take some of your attention, so I wouldn't say they are harmless. They are just a lot easier to make a rule about. A police officer can see a phone in somebody's hand. He can't see that somebody's mind is wandering.

So my position is that the phone gets set up before the trip starts and then it gets left alone until the trip is over. A message can wait twenty minutes. If something really can't wait, the driver can pull over, and that costs maybe two minutes. That is a small price compared to what can happen in the few seconds you spend looking down, and I think the law should make people pay it.
