Dear Principal,

I think the school should pick Policy 1. That's the one where students can have their phones at school and use them at lunch and in free time, but they have to be turned off in class. I think it's the best choice because it keeps classes focused and it's fair to students.

One reason is that a full ban would be really hard to enforce. Pretty much everyone has a phone, and lots of kids would just keep them hidden in their backpack or pocket. Then teachers would be spending class time hunting for phones and giving out punishments instead of teaching. With Policy 1 the rule is easy to understand. Phones off in class, and ok to use outside of class. When kids think a rule is reasonable they are way more likely to follow it.

Another reason is safety. A lot of us use our phones to tell our parents when practice ends, to get a ride, or to say something is wrong. If someone feels sick or the bus is late, one call can fix it fast. If phones are banned all day, we can't do that, and I bet some parents would be upset about it too because they worry about their kids.

Also, students need a break sometimes. Lunch is supposed to be a time to relax after working hard all morning. If we can text a friend or listen to music, we come back to class calmer and it's easier to pay attention. When students get some trust, they usually act more responsible. Policy 1 would help us learn how to handle technology, and we will need that later in life, even after we graduate.

I know some people say phones are a distraction. That's true, and that is exactly why Policy 1 says phones must be off during class. Lessons stay protected, but we still get to use them at break time. And if somebody breaks the rule, the school can take their phone for the rest of the day, so there are real consequences.

So that is why I think Policy 1 is better than the other choice. It keeps classrooms focused, it keeps students safe, and it shows that the school trusts us to be mature. Thank you for reading my letter and thinking about what I said.

Sincerely,
A Concerned Student
