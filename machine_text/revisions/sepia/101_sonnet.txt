I think drivers should not be allowed to use their cell phones at all while the car is moving. The only exception should be a phone that is mounted and already set up for directions before the trip starts. Driving takes your full attention, and a phone is made to grab it.

A lot of people think a quick look at the screen is no big deal. But if you look away for five seconds on the highway, you go about the length of a football field without seeing the road. Reading one text or changing one song can take that long. Distracted driving hurts and kills thousands of people every year, and plenty of them are passengers, people walking, or other drivers who never chose to take that risk.

Some people say hands-free calling fixes this, but I don't agree. The problem is not just your hands, it is also your brain. If you are in a serious conversation, you can look right at a red light and not really notice it. The person on the phone can't see the traffic, so your mind goes to them instead of the road. Talking out loud to send a text or making a hands-free call feels safer, yet your reaction time gets slower in almost the same way as holding the phone.

Other people might say a full ban is too strict because phones have maps, music, and ways to call for help. That is a fair point, and it is why I would allow some use. You can type in where you are going before you leave, put the phone in a mount, and listen to the directions. If there is a real emergency, you can pull over somewhere safe and call. None of that makes you take your eyes or mind off the road while you are moving.

We also need laws because just telling people to be careful does not work. Lots of drivers say that texting and driving is dangerous and then do it anyway. When there are clear rules and real fines, people change what they do. That is what happened with seat belts. Today hardly anybody thinks wearing a seat belt is too much to ask.

In the end, a call or a text can wait ten minutes. A person's life can't be given back once it is gone. So drivers should keep the phone out of their hands and out of their heads until the car is parked.
