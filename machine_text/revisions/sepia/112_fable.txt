Pretty much everyone I know has a phone, and pretty much everyone I know has looked at it while driving. I've done it too. It was at a red light and I told myself it didn't count because the car wasn't moving. When people argue about whether drivers should be allowed to use cell phones in any capacity, they usually act like the answer has to be all yes or all no. I don't think it is either one. I think drivers should not be allowed to hold a phone, read it, or type on it while the car is moving or stopped in traffic. But a phone that is in a mount giving directions, or a phone used to call for help in an emergency, is different and should stay legal.

The reason to ban handheld use is pretty simple. To drive safely you need your eyes on the road, your hands on the wheel, and your mind on what is in front of you. Texting takes all of those away at the same time. The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration has said that reading a text takes a driver's eyes off the road for about five seconds. At highway speed that is about the length of a football field, and the driver didn't see any of it. Nobody would agree to drive a football field with their eyes closed, but that is basically what looking at one message is. The same agency says distracted driving causes thousands of deaths in the United States every year. The real number is probably higher, because if you caused a crash you aren't going to want to admit you were on your phone.

Phones are also worse than other distractions because a lot of the time it is someone else who gets hurt. A driver who is eating a sandwich or messing with the radio is distracted too. But a phone pulls on your attention more, because a text or a conversation wants you to answer back. The person walking in the crosswalk never agreed to that risk. Neither did the cyclist on the shoulder or the family in the next lane. This is what laws are for, when one person's convenience costs another person their safety. We already have speed limits and drunk driving laws for the same reason, and hardly anybody says those take away our freedom anymore.

Some people say hands-free calling fixes the problem. It fixes part of it. If you are on speaker or a headset, you have both hands on the wheel and you are looking forward, so that is obviously better than holding the phone. But studies on driver attention have found that part of your mind stays in the conversation and your reaction time gets slower even when your hands are free. A passenger is different. A passenger can see the road, so they stop talking when traffic gets bad. The person on the other end of a call can't see the road. I wouldn't ban hands-free calls completely, because a law like that would be almost impossible to enforce, but I'm not going to say they are harmless either. Drivers should keep those calls short. New drivers shouldn't be allowed to make them at all, since they are still learning how to handle the car.

There are also ways a phone makes driving safer, and a total ban would get rid of those. Navigation is the obvious one. A phone in a dashboard mount that says the directions out loud is a lot less dangerous than a lost driver slowing down to read street signs or trying to unfold a paper map. Another one is being able to call for help after a crash, or to report a drunk driver or something dangerous in the road. If the law said no phones "in any capacity," people doing those things would be criminals. And when a law is one that sensible people ignore, they respect the sensible parts of it less too.

So the rule I would argue for is easy to remember. Set your route before you pull out, put the phone in the mount, and don't touch it again until you are parked. If something really can't wait, pull over. The message will still be there in ten minutes, and so will everybody else on the road.
