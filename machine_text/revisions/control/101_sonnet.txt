I think drivers should not be allowed to use cell phones at all while driving. The only exception should be a phone that is mounted and used for directions that were set up before the trip started. Driving takes your whole attention, and a phone is made to steal it.

A lot of people think they can handle a quick look at their screen. But if you look away from the road for five seconds while going highway speed, you travel the length of a football field without seeing anything in front of you. Reading a text or changing a song can easily take that long. Distracted driving crashes hurt and kill thousands of people every year, and a lot of them are passengers, pedestrians, and other drivers who never chose to take that risk.

Some people say hands-free calling fixes this problem, but it doesn't. The danger is mostly in your head and not in your hands. Someone in the middle of a serious conversation can stare right at a stoplight and not even notice it turned red. The person on the phone can't see the traffic either. Voice texting and hands-free calls feel safer, but studies show reaction time is slowed about the same as with a handheld phone.

Other people argue that a full ban is too strict because phones have maps, music, and emergency tools now. That is a fair point, and it is why I think a small exception makes sense. A driver can put in the address before leaving, mount the phone, and listen to the directions. If there is a real emergency, the driver can pull over safely and call for help. None of that needs you to take your eyes off the road while the car is moving.

Laws matter too because people don't always have self control. Plenty of drivers will say texting while driving is dangerous and then do it anyway. Clear rules with real fines can change habits, like seat belt laws did a long time ago. Nobody today really thinks wearing a seat belt is too much to ask.

A call or a text can almost always wait ten minutes. A life can't be given back. So drivers should keep their phones out of their hands and out of their minds until the car is parked.
