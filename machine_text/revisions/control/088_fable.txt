One Activity Is Not Too Much

When our principal said that every student has to join at least one extracurricular activity, people started complaining before he even finished talking. They said they don't have time, that school should be over when the last bell rings, and that you can't force somebody to have fun. I get it, because I said some of that stuff too. But now that I've thought about it more, I agree with the principal. One activity is not a lot to ask, and I think students get way more out of it than the hour or two a week it takes.

My biggest reason is that a lot of kids won't try anything unless somebody makes them. I know this because I was one of them. In sixth grade I didn't sign up for anything. I went home, did my homework, and watched videos until dinner. Then in seventh grade my mom made me join the school newspaper because she was sick of seeing me on the couch. I was mad about it for like two weeks. But then I wrote a story about why the cafeteria always runs out of pizza on Fridays, and kids I never talked to before were stopping me in the hall to talk about it. I found out I really like interviewing people. I never would have known that if it was up to me. This rule would basically do for everybody what my mom did for me.

Another reason is that activities teach you things you don't learn in class. In math, if I don't do my work, it only hurts my own grade. But on yearbook, if one person misses a deadline, then forty pages get to the printer late and everybody has to deal with it. The treasurer on student council has to tell actual classmates why there isn't enough money for a dance and a field day both. A goalie who lets in three goals still has to show up to practice the next day. All of these are about other people counting on you and you counting on them. You can't learn that from a worksheet.

Activities also help you feel like you belong. Our school has hundreds of kids, and it can get pretty lonely if the only people you know are the ones who sit next to you. Clubs mix up kids who wouldn't meet any other way. In chess club at our school the eighth graders teach the sixth graders how to play, and stage crew has kids from every lunch table. If you have even one group you're part of, then you have a reason to come to school on the days when your classes aren't enough of a reason.

I know some people have good reasons to be against this. Some students really are busy. They have to watch their little brothers and sisters, or they have a job, or their bus leaves at 3:10. The principal should take that seriously and the rule should be flexible. Some clubs could meet during lunch or advisory. And if a kid already plays in a town soccer league or volunteers at their church, that should count. If the school does that, then the rule is really not asking much. It's one activity, and you get to pick it out of dozens.

Other people say that if you force kids to do it, it's not fun anymore. I think that's backwards. Nobody is getting put on the debate team if they don't want to be. You still choose, and there's a lot to choose from, like robotics, art club, track, the literary magazine, and the garden committee. And if you don't like anything on the list you can start something new. That's how our school got a cooking club.

School is supposed to get us ready for more than just tests. It's supposed to help us figure out what we're good at and how to work with other people. Making everyone do one activity means every student gets that chance, not just the confident ones. At the start of seventh grade I was mad that I got pushed into something. Now I'm glad somebody pushed me.
