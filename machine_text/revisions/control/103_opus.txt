Phones and Driving

Pretty much everyone has a cell phone these days, and a lot of us feel lost without one. But the worst place to use a phone is when you are driving. I think drivers should not be allowed to hold or use their phone at all while the car is moving. The only exception should be hands-free stuff like voice GPS, as long as you set it up before you start driving.

The biggest reason is attention. When you drive you need your eyes, your hands, and your brain all at once. Texting takes away all three. If you look down for five seconds on the highway, you basically drive the length of a football field without looking at the road. In that time a little kid could walk out into the street or the car in front of you could slam on its brakes. It doesn't matter how good of a driver you are, you can't react to something you didn't even see.

A lot of people say they can handle it because they only check their phone at red lights or when the road is empty. The problem is that habits don't stay where you want them to. If you read texts at a stoplight, pretty soon you're reading them while the car is rolling, and then while you're merging. A simple rule like "the phone stays down" is easier to follow than a rule with a bunch of exceptions, and it's easier for cops to enforce too.

Some people argue that phones actually make driving safer. GPS keeps you from getting lost and making sudden turns, and if you crash you can call for help. Those are good points, and that's why the rule should still allow mounted GPS that you control by voice, and emergency calls. What it should ban is texting, scrolling, watching videos, and holding the phone up to your ear.

Almost any call or text can wait ten minutes, but you can't bring someone back to life. If a message is really that important, you can pull over and park and answer it safely. Keeping phones out of drivers' hands is a small sacrifice that protects the driver, the passengers, and everyone else on the road.
