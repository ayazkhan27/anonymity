Why Drivers Should Not Use Cell Phones While Driving

Almost everybody has a phone these days, and we check them all the time. Because of that, a lot of people think its fine to use them in the car too. I disagree. A car weighs about two tons and goes really fast, so the driver needs to be paying attention. I believe drivers should not use cell phones at all while they are driving.

The main reason is that people can't really multitask. We like to think we can do two things at once, but our brain actually just switches back and forth, and every time it switches we lose a little bit of focus. If a driver looks at a text for only five seconds while going on the highway, they travel about the length of a football field without looking at the road. That is not safe at all, and it doesn't matter how good of a driver you are or how many years you have been driving.

Some people say hands-free calling is a good middle ground because both hands are still on the wheel. But the problem isn't just your hands, its your mind. When you are in a conversation your brain is busy with that. Studies show that drivers on hands-free calls react slower to things on the road and sometimes miss stuff right in front of them. They might be looking at the road but not really seeing it. Also, a rule that bans all phone use is easier to enforce than one that only bans some of it. A police officer would not have to guess which kind of use is allowed.

Other people say you need your phone for directions or for emergencies. I understand that, but there are easy fixes. You can put the address in before you start driving, or put the phone in a holder and use voice directions so you never have to touch it. If there is a real emergency, you can pull over somewhere safe and then call. Waiting a few minutes to stop is nothing compared to getting in a crash.

Another reason is that driving affects other people, not just you. A distracted driver puts their passengers, people walking, bike riders, and other drivers in danger, and those people never agreed to take that risk. We already have laws against things like drunk driving and running red lights because they put others in danger, and texting while driving should be treated the same way.

I know it is hard to break a habit, but it can be done. Drivers can turn off notifications, use a do not disturb mode, or just put the phone in the back seat where they cant reach it. Any message can wait until you park. Someones life is worth way more than a text back.
