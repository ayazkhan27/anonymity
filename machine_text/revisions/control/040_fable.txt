Learning From Home Should Be an Option

In tenth grade my cousin broke her leg and missed almost six weeks of school. Her teachers sent packets home, but a packet can't explain chemistry to you, and she was behind for the rest of the year. If she could have just watched her classes on video she would have been fine. That is why I think students should be able to attend class from home. It should be a choice though, not something everybody gets forced into.

The biggest reason is that some kids really can't get to school. People get sick, get hurt, have family emergencies, or get snowed in, and every day you miss makes the next day harder. If you have the flu, by the third day you can probably listen to a lesson, but you shouldn't be sitting in a room with thirty people coughing on them. If you could log in from home you would keep up and nobody else would catch it. It would help kids who live far out in the country too. Some of them are on the bus more than an hour each way. That is two hours every day they could be sleeping, doing homework, or working a job.

It would also help students who don't do well in a normal classroom. Some people have anxiety, and being in a loud building all day wears them out. Other kids get distracted by whoever sits next to them, or they get bullied in the hallway. At home they can actually pay attention to the work. Also, if the lessons are recorded, you can go back and watch the part you didn't get. In a real class most people just nod and pretend they understand the math problem.

I know there are problems with this. A kid at home can open another tab, zone out, or just quit showing up. They miss out on lunch with friends, group projects, and sports, which is a big part of school. And not every family has good internet or a quiet place to work. I think these are real problems, but they mean schools have to set it up carefully. They don't mean schools should say no. Schools could make students keep cameras on during discussions, check in with online students every week, and lend out laptops and hotspots. If somebody's grades drop, they come back to the building.

The last reason is that we are going to need to know how to do this anyway. A lot of college classes are online now, and a lot of jobs have video meetings where nobody is standing over you making you work. It is better to practice that in high school while teachers are still watching than to figure it out by yourself at eighteen.

Most students will still do best going to school in person. But for kids who are sick, or live far away, or can't handle the crowd, a seat at home beats an empty desk.
