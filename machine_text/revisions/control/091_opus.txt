I agree with my principal that every student should do at least one extracurricular activity. School teaches us a lot of facts, but stuff outside of class teaches you how to work with people, manage your time, and figure out what you actually like.

First, activities teach teamwork. When you play soccer, you learn pretty fast that you can't win by yourself. You have to pass the ball and trust your teammates. It's the same with yearbook, where the writers and photographers all have to get their parts done on time or the book doesn't get finished. Grown ups need these skills at their jobs too.

Second, activities help you manage your time. When I joined debate club I had practice three days a week after school. At first I was scared my grades would go down, but they didn't. I actually started planning my homework better and stopped wasting my nights on my phone. Being busy can make you more focused.

Last, activities help kids find out what they're into. A shy kid could try drama club and find out they're good at acting. Someone on student council might decide they want to be a lawyer.

Some people say students are already too busy. But one activity isn't that much to ask, and it's worth it. That's why I agree with my principal.
