Phones and the Driver's Seat

Almost everybody I know has a phone, and almost everybody I know has looked at it while driving. I have seen my own cousin do it with me in the passenger seat. It happens so much that it doesn't even feel dangerous anymore, and I think that is the real problem. In my opinion drivers should not be allowed to hold or touch a phone while the car is moving. But I don't think the law should ban every single use of a phone. A phone on the dashboard giving directions is not the same as a phone in your hand, so the rules shouldn't be the same either.

The reason to ban holding a phone is pretty simple. To drive you need your eyes, your hands, and your attention, and a phone takes all three of them. Reading one text only takes a few seconds, but on the highway a car goes about the length of a football field in that time. So for that whole distance the driver is basically blind. Nothing even has to go wrong with the car. A little kid could step off the curb or a truck could hit its brakes. Thousands of people die every year because of distracted driving, and a lot of them were not the ones on the phone. That is the part that bothers me the most. If somebody doesn't wear a seat belt they are mostly risking their own life. If somebody texts going forty miles an hour they are risking the lives of people who never had a choice.

Some people say they are good enough drivers to do both. I don't believe that. People are bad at knowing how much they are really paying attention, and the ones who feel the most sure are usually the ones swerving into the other lane. The only way you find out you were wrong is by crashing.

However, banning phones completely would be too much. Nobody uses paper maps anymore, and a voice telling me to turn left in half a mile is way safer than trying to read a map on the steering wheel. A phone is also how you call for help if your car breaks down or you see an accident. Hands free calls are not perfect because talking still distracts you, but at least your eyes are up and your hands are on the wheel. It is not much different from talking to a passenger. Also, if a law banned all of this, most people would just ignore it, and then they would start ignoring other laws too.

So the rule should be about touching. Put in your directions before you leave the driveway, let the phone talk, and don't pick it up until you are parked. A text can wait ten minutes. The person in the crosswalk can't.
