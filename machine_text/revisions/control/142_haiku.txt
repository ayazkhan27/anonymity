Summer projects should be designed by students. Teachers already control most of the school year, and a summer assignment handed down from above often turns into a chore to finish instead of something to learn from. When students pick their own projects, they actually care about them.

Think about a student who loves baseball but hates her required reading list. If she designs a project comparing batting statistics across decades, she has to learn some statistics, look up history, and write clearly to explain what she found. That same student, given a generic book report, might skim the book and forget it by September.

Student design does not mean no guidance. Teachers can set rules, like a minimum amount of time, a final product, and a short proposal that has to be approved. This keeps projects serious while still letting students own their learning.

Ownership also builds the skill that matters most after high school, which is deciding what to learn and how to go after it. A project a teacher designs teaches students to follow directions. A project a student designs teaches them to think for themselves.
