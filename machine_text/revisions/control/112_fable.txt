Phones and the Driver's Seat

Pretty much everyone I know has a phone, and pretty much everyone I know who drives has looked at it while driving. I've done it too. It was at a red light and I told myself it didn't count because the car wasn't moving. When people ask if drivers should be allowed to use cell phones in any capacity, they usually want a yes or a no. I don't think either one is right. Drivers should not be allowed to hold a phone, read it, or type on it while the car is moving or stopped in traffic. But a phone that is in a mount giving directions, or a phone being used for an emergency call, is different and should stay legal.

The reason to ban holding a phone is pretty simple. To drive safely you need your eyes on the road, your hands on the wheel, and your mind on what's in front of you. Texting takes away all three at the same time. In driver's ed they told us that the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration says reading a text takes your eyes off the road for about five seconds. At highway speed that is about the length of a football field. Nobody would drive a whole football field with their eyes closed, but that is basically what you are doing when you check a message. The same agency says thousands of people die every year in the United States because of distracted driving. I think the real number is probably higher, because if you caused a crash you are not going to want to admit you were on your phone.

Phones are also worse than other distractions because a lot of the time it is somebody else who gets hurt. A driver eating a sandwich or messing with the radio is distracted too, but a phone is different because a text or a call wants you to answer it. It keeps pulling at you. The person walking in the crosswalk, the guy on the bike, and the family in the next lane never agreed to take that risk. That is what laws are for, when one person's convenience costs another person their safety. We already have speed limits and drunk driving laws for the same reason, and hardly anybody says those are taking away our freedom.

Some people say hands-free calling fixes the problem. It fixes part of it. If you are on speaker or using a headset, you have both hands on the wheel and you are looking forward, which is obviously better than holding the phone. But I have read that studies show your mind is still partly in the conversation and you react slower even if your hands are free. Also, when my mom is driving and traffic gets bad, I stop talking because I can see what is going on. Someone on the other end of a call can't see the road, so they just keep talking. I would not ban hands-free calls completely, because I don't see how the police could even enforce that. But I would not act like they are totally safe either. Drivers should keep those calls short. New drivers, who are still learning how to handle the car, should not be allowed to make them at all.

There are also ways a phone makes driving safer, and a total ban would get rid of those. The biggest one is directions. A phone in a mount on the dashboard that tells you where to turn is a lot less dangerous than a lost driver slowing down to read every street sign. Another one is calling for help after a crash, or reporting a drunk driver or something in the road. A law that says no phones "in any capacity" would make people criminals for doing these things. And when a law is so strict that normal people ignore it, they start ignoring the parts that make sense too.

So the rule I would want is easy to remember. Set up your directions before you leave, put the phone in the mount, and don't touch it again until you are parked. If something really can't wait, pull over. The text will still be there in ten minutes, and so will everybody else on the road.
