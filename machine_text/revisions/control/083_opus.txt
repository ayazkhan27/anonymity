I agree with the principal that every student should have to do at least one extracurricular activity. I know some kids are going to complain about it at first, but clubs and teams teach you stuff you can't really learn in a classroom. They help you make friends, they teach you to be responsible, and they help you figure out what you are good at.

First, activities get kids together who would probably never talk otherwise. Our school is pretty big, so it's easy to just hang out with the same few friends every day. But if a quiet kid joins robotics or the school play, they have to work with people from other grades that they don't know. My cousin was really shy in ninth grade until she joined cross country. Running all those miles with her team gave her stuff to talk about, and by the end of the season she had a bunch of new friends. If everyone has to join something, then everyone gets that chance, not just the kids who are already outgoing.

Second, activities teach responsibility in a way homework doesn't. If you are on yearbook and you miss a deadline, the whole yearbook is late. If you skip soccer practice, you aren't just hurting yourself, you are letting your whole team down. So you learn how to manage your time and show up when people are counting on you. Colleges and jobs want people who can do that, and it's better to practice now when it's not a huge deal if you mess up.

Third, activities help you find out what you like. A lot of people don't know they like debate or art or coding until they actually try it. Like, a kid who is bad at math might find out they are a really good leader in student council, and then they feel more confident in class too. If it wasn't required, a lot of kids would never even try.

Some people might say students are already too busy with jobs or helping their family. That's true for some kids, but the rule is only one activity, and the school could have clubs that meet once a week or at lunch. That's not that much to ask.

That's why I agree with the principal. Making everyone do one activity helps kids make friends, grow up, and figure out who they want to be.
