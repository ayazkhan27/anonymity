Drivers Should Not Use Cell Phones While Driving

Almost everybody has a cell phone these days, and most people check theirs like a hundred times a day without even thinking about it. On a couch that is no big deal. In a car it can get somebody killed. I believe drivers should not be allowed to use handheld cell phones at all while they are driving, and the law should treat phones as the danger they really are.

The main problem is attention. Driving seems easy once you've done it a while, but you are actually making decisions the whole time. You watch the car in front of you, check your mirrors, look out for people on the sidewalk, and try to guess what the other drivers are going to do. A phone uses the same part of your brain. There are three kinds of distraction, which are visual (your eyes leave the road), manual (your hands leave the wheel), and cognitive (your mind leaves the road). Texting does all three at once. If a driver looks at a text for just five seconds while going highway speed, they travel about the length of a football field without looking at the road. No car feature can make up for that.

The statistics show how serious this is. Thousands of people die every year in the United States from distracted driving, and the real number is probably higher because people almost never admit they were on their phone after a crash. A lot of the people who die are not even the distracted driver. They are passengers, bike riders, kids walking to school, and other drivers who were following every rule. That is why this is not just about personal freedom. If someone wants to risk their own life, that is one thing, but risking other people's lives is something else.

Some people say hands-free calling is safe enough and that the law should only ban texting. That sounds fair, but studies do not back it up. Research with driving simulators and real traffic has found that the conversation is itself the distraction, not only holding the phone. Drivers who are deep in a phone call miss more red lights and react slower when the car in front of them brakes, even when both of their hands are on the wheel and their eyes are straight ahead. Your brain can't really focus on a conversation and on the road at the exact same time. It goes back and forth, and every switch takes a little time that a driver might not have. Hands-free fixes the hands part but it does not fix the thinking part.

Other people argue that drivers already talk to passengers or change the radio, so phones are not any different. I disagree. A passenger is in the car and can see the road too, so they usually stop talking when traffic gets scary. Someone on the phone can't see the intersection and just keeps talking. Also, changing the radio is quick and simple, but a phone has endless messages, notifications, and apps that are made to keep you looking at them. A phone is designed to be hard to ignore, which is the worst thing you could want inside a moving car.

Enforcement is a fair concern because a law does not work if nobody follows it. But look at seat belt laws and drunk driving laws. When the rules were strict and people understood why they existed, behavior slowly changed, and things that used to seem normal started to feel irresponsible. A simple rule is also easier to enforce than a bunch of exceptions. If a police officer sees a phone in a driver's hand, they do not have to guess whether the person was dialing, reading, or doing something that was allowed.

There should be a few reasonable exceptions. If a driver has a real emergency, like reporting a wreck or calling for help, they should be able to use the phone. Setting up a GPS before the trip starts is also fine. And anyone can always pull over and park before using their phone. These exceptions are small and they do not change the main point, which is that driving is not the time for calls, texts, or scrolling.

Nobody has to be available every single minute of the day. Getting somewhere a little later or letting a message wait costs almost nothing. Not waiting could cost someone their life. Drivers should put the phone away until the car is parked, and the law should make them do it.
