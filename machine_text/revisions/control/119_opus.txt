Phones and Driving Don't Mix

These days almost everyone has a cell phone with them all the time, and a lot of people can't stop checking it, even when they are driving. Because of this, people argue about whether drivers should be allowed to use their phones while the car is moving. In my opinion, drivers should not be holding or messing with their phones at all while they drive. The only exceptions should be calling 911 in an emergency and using GPS directions that you set up before you start driving.

My first reason is that driving takes all of your attention. It might seem like something you do on autopilot, but it really isn't. You have to watch how fast other cars are coming, keep an eye on a bike rider on the side of the road, and be ready in case a little kid runs into the street after a ball. When you read a text, your eyes are off the road for a few seconds. On the highway, your car can go about the length of a football field in that time. No one would drive that far with their eyes closed, but looking at your phone is basically the same thing.

Some people think hands-free calling fixes the problem. It does help some, but it doesn't solve it. Research on distracted driving shows that talking on the phone still takes your mind off the road, even if both your hands are on the wheel. Talking to a passenger is not the same, because the passenger can see what's happening and will usually stop talking if traffic gets bad. The person on the phone can't see anything, so they just keep talking while the driver gets more distracted. That is why I think even hands-free calls should only be short ones that you actually need to make.

To be fair, the other side does have a good point about GPS and emergencies. Most people use their phones for directions now instead of paper maps, and listening to directions is safer than trying to read street signs or looking at a map on the steering wheel. Also, if there is a crash up ahead or someone is having a medical problem, a phone call could save a life. A good rule would allow these things, as long as you put in the address before you drive and keep the phone in a holder, not in your hand.

Everything else can wait. Texts, social media, picking songs, and emails don't need an answer in the next five minutes, and no message is worth getting in a crash. Tougher laws, more police enforcement, and phone settings that block notifications while driving would all help. But in the end, every driver has to decide for themselves that the road deserves their full attention.
