Drivers should not be allowed to use cell phones at all while they are driving. Phones are convenient, but that does not matter more than a crash that could kill somebody.

Driving takes your full attention. A lot of people think hands-free calling is a safe middle ground, but the research says it is not. There are three kinds of distraction, which are visual, manual, and cognitive. A hands-free call gets rid of the manual part, but the driver's mind is still stuck on the conversation. Drivers on the phone miss their exits, don't notice people crossing the street, and are slower to hit the brakes when the car in front of them stops. Looking at the road is not the same thing as actually seeing it. Since the mental distraction is still there, a law that allows hands-free calls just gives drivers a false sense of safety.

Texting is even worse. If you read one message at highway speed, you travel the length of a football field without looking at the road. Nothing on a screen is important enough for that. Most texts can wait ten minutes, and if it really is an emergency, the driver can pull over first.

Some people say a total ban is too strict because phones also give you directions and music. But there is an easy answer to this. A driver can pick a route and a playlist before leaving the driveway, and a lot of newer cars have built-in systems made to be used while driving. A phone that is mounted for directions and left alone is not the same as a phone someone is using. A simple rule that bans holding the phone and using it for calls or texts is also easier to enforce than a bunch of exceptions, since a police officer can tell when a phone is being used without having to guess what the driver is doing on it.

We already accept limits on how people drive, like speed limits and drunk driving laws, because everyone shares the road and one person's choices can put lots of other people in danger. Using a phone belongs in that same group. A call or a text is never worth someone's life, so drivers should wait until the car is parked.
