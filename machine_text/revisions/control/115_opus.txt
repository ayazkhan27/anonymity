Keep the Phone Out of Your Hands When You Drive

Pretty much everybody has a cell phone on them all the time now, and that includes when they are in the car. Phones give directions, play music, and let us text our parents or our boss. Since they are so useful, a lot of drivers act like the phone is just part of the car. But I think drivers should not hold or touch their phone at all while the car is moving. The only exception should be hands-free stuff that is set up before you leave, like a GPS route that talks to you.

The biggest reason is paying attention. Driving seems easy once you get used to it, but your brain is actually doing a lot. You have to watch the road, check your mirrors, figure out how fast other cars are going, and react to stuff you don't see coming, like a little kid running into the street after a ball. Reading a text takes around five seconds. If you are going fifty-five miles per hour, you go about the length of a football field in that time. So for five seconds you are basically driving blind. No text is worth that.

Some people say talking on the phone is the same as talking to someone in the passenger seat. But research says that isn't true. A passenger can see the road too. If traffic gets bad, they usually stop talking or even warn you. The person on the phone has no clue what is going on, so they just keep talking right when you need to focus the most. Studies have even shown that hands-free calls make your reaction time slower, because the problem is in your head and not just your hands.

Other people say phones are necessary now, especially for directions. That's true, and that is why I think there should be one exception. You can type in the address before you pull out of the driveway and let the phone tell you where to go from a mount on the dashboard. What you shouldn't do is type, scroll, or pick up the phone while the car is in drive. If you need to change the route, just pull over. Spending thirty seconds in a parking lot is not a big deal.

It's also about being fair to everyone else on the road. If someone decides to text and drive, they are not only risking their own life. People walking, people on bikes, and other drivers never agreed to that risk. We already have laws against drunk driving because drunk drivers put other people in danger, and distracted driving is dangerous in a similar way. Strict laws against using a phone in your hand, with real punishments, would show people that the road belongs to everybody.

Cell phones aren't going anywhere, and they do make life easier. But when you are behind the wheel, your job is to drive. Putting the phone down or turning on do not disturb until you get where you're going is a small sacrifice. When you think about the lives it could save, it's barely a sacrifice at all.
