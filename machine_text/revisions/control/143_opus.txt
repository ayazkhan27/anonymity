Students Should Get to Pick Their Own Summer Projects

A lot of schools give out summer projects every June so students don't forget everything over break. I get why they do it. If you go ten weeks without reading or writing or doing any math, you lose a lot of what you learned that year. But the real question is who should come up with the projects. I think students should design their own summer projects, and teachers should just set some basic rules and help out when needed. When students pick their own projects they actually try harder, they learn skills school doesn't really teach, and it feels like their own learning instead of something being forced on them.

The first reason is motivation. When a teacher assigns a project, like a report on a book everyone has to read, most kids end up doing it the night before school starts and they hate every minute of it. Summer is the only time of year we actually control our own time, so an assignment that feels like more homework just loses to everything else. A project you picked yourself is different. Say a girl loves baking. She could study the chemistry of why bread rises, try different amounts of yeast, and write down what happens. Or a guy who is obsessed with basketball could track his free throw percentage all summer and learn how to graph it and figure out what the numbers mean. Since these projects come from stuff people already like, they will actually want to keep working on them, and that is the whole point of a summer project.

Second, planning a project is a lesson by itself. During the school year teachers decide pretty much everything, like what we read, what questions we answer, and when stuff is due. We almost never get to plan out a long project, set our own goals, or change plans when something doesn't work. If a student has to come up with a question, figure out how to answer it, and keep working on it for weeks, they are practicing the kind of independence you need in college and at a job. For example, if someone's garden experiment dies in July, they have to figure out what went wrong and start over. A worksheet is never going to teach you that.

Third, every student is different. One assignment for the whole class assumes everybody needs the same practice and likes the same things, which isn't true. One kid might need to work on writing while another kid is ready for a hard coding project. If students can choose, everyone gets to work at their own level instead of the class average.

Some people say that if students get to choose, they will pick something way too easy or just not do it at all. That is a fair point, but it means teachers should guide students, not control everything. Teachers could have students turn in a short proposal before summer, set rules for how much work is expected, and have them present it in the fall. That way students get freedom but still have to be responsible.

The whole point of summer projects is to keep kids curious, and nothing does that better than letting them work on what they already care about.
