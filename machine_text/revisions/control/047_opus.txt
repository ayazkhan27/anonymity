Learning From Home Should Be an Option

When most people think of school they think of desks in rows, a whiteboard, and hallways packed with kids between classes. For a lot of students that works fine. But for some students, getting to school every day is a real problem. I think students would benefit from being able to take classes from home through online or video classes, as long as its a choice and not something everyone is forced to do.

The biggest reason is that some students lives just dont fit a normal school schedule. Some teenagers have part-time jobs so they can help their family pay rent. Some have to babysit younger siblings or take care of a sick grandparent. Athletes who travel for games or tournaments can miss a bunch of days in a row. For these students, online learning isnt just a nice extra, its what decides if they keep up or fall behind. If they can watch a recorded lesson or join a live class on video, they still learn something on a day they would of just been absent.

Online learning also helps students who are sick or have disabilities. If someone is recovering from surgery, or has a weak immune system, they might not be able to come to class for weeks. Instead of losing a whole semester they could just log in and still be part of the class. Also students with bad anxiety might focus better at home where it's calmer, instead of stressing about everything going on around them.

Another good thing is that students could take classes their school doesn't offer. A small school out in the country might not have enough kids to hire a teacher for something like AP Physics or Japanese. With video classes, a student there could join a class at a different school or even a college. That way where you live doesn't limit what you get to learn.

Some people say that students at home will get distracted, and that they will miss out on the social part of school like friends, clubs, and learning how to work with people in person. I agree these are real problems. Not everyone has the self-control to learn by themselves, and not every house has a quiet place to work or good wifi. But these are reasons to plan online learning carefully, not reasons to get rid of it. Schools could check in with online students a lot, lend out laptops and hotspots, and get students to come in for clubs and sports when they can.

In the end, every student is different, and one type of school is never going to work for everybody. Letting students choose to learn from home respects their situations and keeps more of them learning. Offering both options doesn't make regular classes worse. It just makes sure no student gets left out.
