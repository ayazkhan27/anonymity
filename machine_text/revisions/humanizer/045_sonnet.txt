I think students should be able to attend classes from home, as long as distance learning is an option and not a requirement. School works fine for most kids, but some students get left behind, and having a choice helps them.

The biggest reason is access. Some students live really far from school, and a long bus ride can take two or three hours out of the day. Other students are sick for a long time, recovering from surgery, or living with a chronic illness. If there is no distance learning, they fall behind or drop out. If there is, they can keep up with the same lessons as their classmates and come back to school without losing a year.

Distance learning also helps people who don't learn well in a crowded room. A shy student might be scared to ask a question in front of thirty other kids, but in a video class they can just type it in the chat. A student who gets bullied might finally be able to focus on the lesson instead of worrying about the hallway. If lessons are recorded, they can replay a hard explanation as many times as they need.

Families need flexibility too. A teenager who helps take care of a little brother or sister, or who has a job to help pay for the house, might find it almost impossible to keep a strict schedule. Online classes let that student attend at times that fit their real life.

Some people say students lose social contact and that a lot of them won't stay disciplined at home. I think those are fair concerns. School is where you make friends, and some students would be tempted to play games instead of listening. But there are ways to cut down on these problems. Schools could require regular check-ins and set clear attendance rules, and they could invite distance students to come in for clubs, labs, and special events. If a student shows they can't handle learning at home, they can be moved back to the classroom.

In the end, a school should serve the student, and the student shouldn't have to bend to fit the school. Students have different needs, so one setting is never going to be best for everybody. Offering distance learning as an option gives students who are struggling, far away, sick, or overloaded a real chance to succeed, and the ones who do well in a classroom can stay right where they are. That makes education fairer, so I think schools should offer it.
