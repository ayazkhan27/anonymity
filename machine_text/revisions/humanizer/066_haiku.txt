Dear Principal,

I am writing about the proposed policy that would require students to have at least a B average before they can play sports or join other activities. I get why the school wants this. We all want students to do well in class. But I think this policy would cause more problems than it fixes, and I hope you will read the reasons below before you decide.

First, a lot of students have a C average right now, and this policy would take them off the teams and clubs that keep them motivated. For a lot of us, sports and clubs are the reason we come to school at all. Practice gives us a place to belong and goals that have nothing to do with tests. It also gives us adults who notice when we are doing well. If we lose those things, we are more likely to stop caring about school, not less. A kid who is struggling in math does not need to be kicked off the team that gets him out of bed in the morning.

Second, activities teach things grades do not measure. On the field, in the band room, or on the debate team, students learn how to manage their time, work with people who are different from them, and bounce back after a loss. Those skills show up in class too. Lots of athletes plan their homework around practice, and that routine often helps their grades go up over time. Taking away the activity takes away the structure that could help a struggling student the most.

Third, this policy would hit the students who need help the most. A kid with a C average is often not lazy. Some have jobs to help their families. Some have problems at home. Some just learn differently than the way they are taught. Kicking them out of activities punishes them for things they cannot control, and it does not help them learn anything. If the goal is better grades, the school should offer tutoring, study halls, or extra time with teachers. That way students can raise their averages and still stay part of the activities they like.

Finally, a grade rule could make activities less fair and less safe. Coaches and advisors would spend their time on eligibility paperwork instead of teaching and mentoring. A student who loses eligibility in the middle of a season could be pulled from a team right when the team matters most to him. A better plan would be early warnings for students who are falling behind, a plan to get back on track, and help along the way.

So I am asking you not to adopt the B average requirement. I think students should be encouraged to stay in activities, and the school should spend its money helping every student bring up their grades. I would be happy to join any meeting or student survey that could help us find a better answer.

Thank you for asking for our opinions and for thinking carefully about this.

Respectfully,

A Concerned Student
