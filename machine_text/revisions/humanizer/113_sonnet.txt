Drivers should not be allowed to use cell phones at all while driving. Phones are convenient, but that does not outweigh the risk of a crash that kills someone.

Driving takes a person's full attention. A lot of people think hands-free calling is a safe middle ground, but the research on distraction says otherwise. There are three kinds of distraction: visual, manual, and cognitive. A hands-free call gets rid of the manual part, but the driver's mind is still on the conversation. Drivers who are on a call miss their exits, don't notice pedestrians, and react slower when brake lights come on. Looking at the road is different from actually seeing it. Since the mental distraction is still there, a law that allows hands-free use just gives drivers a false sense of safety.

Texting is even worse. If you read a message at highway speed, you travel the length of a football field with your eyes off the road. Nothing that shows up on a screen is important enough for that. Most messages can wait ten minutes, and if something really can't wait, like an emergency, the driver can pull over first.

Some people say a total ban is too strict because phones also give directions and play music. I think there is an easy answer to that. A driver can pick a route and a playlist before leaving the driveway, and many cars today have built-in systems made to be used on the road. A phone that is mounted for directions and left alone is not the same as a phone someone is actively using. A clear rule that bans holding the phone and using it for calls or texts is also easier to enforce than a bunch of exceptions, because a police officer can see a phone being used without having to guess what the driver is doing with it.

We already accept limits on how people drive, like speed limits and drunk driving laws, because the road is shared and one person's choices put a lot of other people in danger. Phone use belongs in that group too. A call or a text is never worth a life, so drivers should wait until the car is parked.
