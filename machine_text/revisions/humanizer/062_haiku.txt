Dear Principal,

I am writing to argue against the new policy that would require at least a B average for students to play sports or join other activities. I understand that the school wants students to work hard in class. But this rule would hurt the students it is supposed to help.

First, a lot of students who struggle in class feel like they belong on a team or in a club. Those activities give them a reason to come to school and stay interested. They also help students make friends with other kids and with adults who support them. If we take that away, students with C averages might stop caring about school, and their grades could get even worse.

Second, sports and activities teach things that grades cannot show. Students learn to work with others, follow rules, lead a group, and manage their time. People get better at these things by practicing and competing. Students who are kept out of activities miss out on learning these skills, and they will need them after graduation.

Finally, this policy punishes students when they need help. The school could offer tutoring, study hall, or mentoring for students who are falling behind. That way, they can raise their grades and still keep the activities that motivate them.

For these reasons, I respectfully ask the school to keep sports and activities open to all students.

Sincerely,
A Concerned Student
