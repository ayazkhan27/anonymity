Phones and the Road

Pretty much everyone has a cell phone now, and most of us feel lost without one. But the worst place to be using a phone is behind the wheel. I think drivers should not be allowed to hold or touch their phone at all while the car is moving. The only exception should be hands-free stuff like voice navigation that you set up before you start driving.

The biggest reason is attention. When you drive you need your eyes, your hands, and your brain all at once, and texting takes away all of them. If you look down for five seconds on the highway, you go about the length of a football field without watching the road. A kid could step off the curb in that time, or the car in front of you could slam on its brakes. Even a really good driver can't react to something they didn't see.

A lot of people say they can handle it because they only check messages at red lights or when the road is empty. The problem is that habits don't stay where they started. If you read texts at a stoplight, pretty soon you're reading them while the car is rolling, and then while you're merging. A simple rule like "the phone stays down" is easier to follow than a rule with a bunch of exceptions, and it's easier for police to enforce too.

Some people argue that phones make driving safer. Maps keep you from getting lost and making sudden turns, and if you crash you can call for help. I agree with that, so the rule should still allow mounted navigation you control by voice, and emergency calls. What it should ban is typing, scrolling, watching videos, and holding the phone up to your ear.

Almost any call or text can wait ten minutes, but you can't bring someone back to life. If a message is really important, you can pull over, park, and answer it. Keeping phones out of drivers' hands isn't much to give up, and it protects the driver, the passengers, and everybody else on the road.
