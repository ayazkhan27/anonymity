I agree with the principal that every student should have to do at least one extracurricular activity. Some kids will probably complain at first, but clubs and teams teach you stuff you can't really learn in a classroom. You make friends, you learn to be responsible, and you figure out what you're good at.

First, activities get kids together who would never meet otherwise. In a big school it's easy to hang out with the same few friends every single day. If a quiet kid joins robotics or the school play, he ends up working with people from other grades that he never would have talked to. My cousin was really shy in ninth grade until she joined cross country. Running all those miles with the team gave her something to talk about, and by the end of the season she had a bunch of new friends. If everyone has to join something, then everybody gets that chance and not only the outgoing kids.

Second, activities teach responsibility in a way homework doesn't. If someone on yearbook misses a deadline, the whole book comes out late. If a soccer player skips practice, he lets the whole team down. Kids in activities learn how to manage their time and show up when people are counting on them. Colleges and bosses want people who can do that, and it's better to practice now when messing up isn't a big deal.

Third, activities help kids find out what they like and what they're good at. A lot of people don't know they like debate or painting or coding until they actually try it. Say a girl is having a hard time in math. She might join student council and find out she's a really good leader, and feeling confident about that could help her in class too. If it wasn't required, lots of students would never even try.

Some people say students are already too busy with jobs or helping their families. I get that, and it's a fair point. But the rule is only one activity, and the school could have clubs that meet once a week or at lunch. That's not a lot to ask when you get so much out of it.

That's why I think the principal made the right call. Making everyone do one activity helps kids make friends, grow up a little, and figure out who they want to be.
