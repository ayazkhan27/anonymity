Learning From Home Should Be an Option

My cousin broke her leg in tenth grade and missed almost six weeks of school. Her teachers sent packets home, but nobody explained the chemistry to her, and she spent the rest of the year trying to catch up. If her school had let her join class by video, I don't think she would have fallen behind much at all. That is the main reason I believe students should be able to attend classes from home. It should be a choice though. I would not want it forced on everyone.

The students who would benefit the most are the ones who can't get to school in the first place. Kids miss school for illness, injuries, family emergencies and bad weather, and every day you miss makes the next day harder. A student with the flu might feel good enough by the third day to listen to a lesson, but they shouldn't be sitting in a crowded room getting everybody else sick. If they could watch from home, they keep up and the rest of the class stays healthy. It would help rural students even more. Some of them ride the bus for over an hour each way. That is two hours every day they could have used for homework or sleep, or for a job.

Learning from home would also help students who just don't do well in a regular classroom. Some have anxiety, and a loud building wears them out. Some get distracted by classmates or get bullied in the hallways. At home they can pay attention to the actual material. Also, if lessons are recorded, a student who didn't get a math problem the first time can replay the explanation. In class most of us just pretend we followed along.

I know there are problems with this. A student at home can zone out, open another tab, or quit showing up. They miss the social part of school too, like talking at lunch, group projects and sports. And some families don't have fast internet or a quiet room. I think these are serious, but to me they mean schools have to set it up carefully. A school could require cameras on during discussions and check in with online students every week. It could lend out laptops and hotspots. If a student's grades drop, they could be moved back into the building.

One more reason is that we are going to need this skill later. A lot of colleges have online courses, and a lot of jobs have video meetings and expect you to manage your own time. I would rather practice that in high school, while teachers are still watching me closely, than have to figure it out alone at eighteen.

Most students will probably still do best in person, and that's fine. But when I think about my cousin with her chemistry packets, I think a student at home on a screen is a lot better off than an empty desk.
