Drivers should not be allowed to use cell phones while they are driving. The only exception I would make is a phone that is mounted and set up for navigation before the trip starts. Driving takes your full attention, and a phone is made to grab it.

A lot of people think they can handle a quick look at a screen. But if you look away from the road for five seconds on the highway, you travel about the length of a football field without seeing what is in front of you. Changing a song or reading a text can easily take that long. Distracted driving injures and kills thousands of people every year. Many of them are passengers, pedestrians, and other drivers who never chose to take that risk.

Some people say hands-free calling fixes the problem. I disagree. Studies show that a lot of the danger is in the mind and not the hands. Someone in a serious conversation can look right at a stoplight and still not notice it turned red. The person on the phone can't see the traffic, so the driver's thoughts get pulled away from the road. Hands-free feels safer, but reaction time is slowed about as much as with a phone held in the hand.

Other people say a full ban is too strict because phones have maps, music, and emergency tools. That is a fair point, which is why I think a small exception makes sense. A driver can type in a destination before leaving, mount the phone, and listen to the directions. If there is a real emergency, the driver can pull over and call for help. None of that means taking your attention off the road while the car is moving.

We also need laws because telling people to be careful does not work very well. Plenty of drivers admit that using a phone is dangerous and then do it anyway. Clear rules with real fines change habits, like seat belt laws did a long time ago. Hardly anyone today thinks buckling up is too much to ask.

A call or a text can almost always wait ten minutes. A life can't be given back. So drivers should keep their phones out of their hands and out of their minds until the car is parked.
