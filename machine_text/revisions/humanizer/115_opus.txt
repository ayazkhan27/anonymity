Phones Belong Out of the Driver's Hands

Pretty much everyone carries a cell phone everywhere now, and that includes the car. Your phone gives you directions, plays your music, and lets your family and your job reach you. Since it's so useful, a lot of drivers treat it like it's part of the dashboard. I still think drivers shouldn't hold or touch their phone at all while the car is moving. The only use that makes sense is hands-free and set up before you leave, like a GPS route that talks to you.

The biggest reason is attention. Driving seems easy because we do it every day, but your brain is doing a lot. You have to watch the road, check your mirrors, figure out how fast other cars are going, and react to stuff you didn't see coming, like a kid running into the street after a ball. Reading a text takes about five seconds. At fifty-five miles per hour, a car goes about the length of a football field in that time, and for those five seconds the driver might as well have their eyes closed. I can't think of any text that's worth that.

Some people say talking on the phone is the same as talking to someone in the passenger seat. Research says it isn't. The passenger can see the road too, so when traffic gets bad they usually stop talking or even warn you. The person on the phone has no clue what's going on, so they just keep talking right when you need to focus the most. Studies have even found that hands-free calls slow down reaction time, because the distraction is in your head and not just in your hands.

Other people say phones are basically necessary now, especially for directions. I agree, and that's why I made the one exception. You can type in the address before you pull out of the driveway and put the phone on a mount so it reads the directions out loud. What you shouldn't do is type, scroll, or pick the phone up while the car is in gear. If you need to change the route, pull over. Stopping in a parking lot for thirty seconds costs you almost nothing.

It's also about being fair to everyone else on the road. If someone decides to text and drive, they aren't just risking their own life. Pedestrians, people on bikes, and other drivers never agreed to that. We already have laws against drunk driving because drunk drivers put other people in danger, and distracted driving is dangerous in a similar way. Clear laws against holding your phone while driving, with real penalties, would show people that the road belongs to everybody.

Cell phones aren't going anywhere, and I'll admit they make life easier. But when you're behind the wheel, your job is to drive. Putting the phone down or turning on do-not-disturb until you get where you're going is a small sacrifice, and compared to the lives it could save, it barely counts as one.
