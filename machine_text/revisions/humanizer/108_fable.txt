Phones and Driving

Pretty much everyone I know has a phone, and pretty much everyone I know has looked at it while driving. I have too. One time I looked down at a text at a red light, and when I looked back up the light had been green long enough that the guy behind me honked. Nothing bad happened, but I couldn't believe how fast I stopped paying attention to the road. Because of that I think drivers should not be allowed to hold or use a phone with their hands while the car is moving. I wouldn't ban every single use though.

Driving takes your eyes and your hands and your attention, and a phone takes all of them at once. Reading a message takes about five seconds. At highway speed a car goes the length of a football field in that time. If you are typing you are not steering well or checking your mirrors, and you are not ready to hit the brakes. The worst part is that the person who gets hurt is a lot of the time somebody else, like a kid crossing the street or a cyclist or a family in the next lane. A text can wait ten minutes, but you can't undo an injury.

Some people say they are good enough drivers to do both. I doubt it. Nobody feels distracted while they are distracted, and that is why the habit is so dangerous. Being confident doesn't mean you are actually good at it, and the law shouldn't depend on what every driver thinks about himself.

I still don't think a total ban makes sense. Phones are maps now. A phone on a mount that says the directions out loud is safer than a paper map on the passenger seat, or a lost driver making sudden turns. A driver should also be able to call for help in an emergency. Hands-free calls are harder for me to decide on, because a conversation still takes your mind off traffic. But a short voice call is closer to talking with a passenger than it is to texting.

The rule I would support is to set your route before you leave, mount the phone, and then not touch it again until the car is parked. Navigation and emergency calls would stay legal. Texting, scrolling and holding the phone would not. That is not much to ask of drivers, and it protects everybody else on the road.
