Almost everybody I know has a phone, and almost everybody I know has looked at it while driving. I have done it too. It was at a red light and I told myself it didn't count because the car wasn't moving. That excuse is kind of why I think the rule should be simple. Drivers should not be allowed to handle a phone at all while they are driving. The only uses that should be allowed are the ones where your hands stay on the wheel and your eyes stay on the road.

My first reason is attention. Driving feels easy because most of the time nothing happens. Somebody can drive the same road to work for years and never get surprised once. But the real skill is being ready for the one second when a little kid steps out between two parked cars, or the truck in front of you slams on its brakes. Reading a text takes your eyes off the road for several seconds. On the highway a car goes about the length of a football field in that amount of time. For those seconds nobody is really driving. The car is just moving, and all the people around it are trusting that nothing is going to change.

A lot of people say they are good at multitasking. I used to think that about myself for a long time. The problem is that when you are distracted you can't notice the stuff you miss. If I look down at my phone and nothing bad happens, I figure I handled it fine. I never see the cyclist I would have hit if it was a different day. So every trip where nothing goes wrong makes the habit stronger, and I get more confident even though I have no real proof that I'm safe. A law against holding the phone takes that decision away from the driver, who is the worst person to be making it.

Another reason is who pays for the mistake. If texting and driving only put the texter in danger, then I could maybe see the argument that it should be a personal choice. But that's not how it works. The person in the crosswalk and the family in the next lane never agreed to take that risk. We already think this way about drunk driving. Nobody says a driver should get to decide for himself if four beers affected him or not. Having a phone in your hand is a similar kind of impairment, and people choose it over and over again in just one trip.

I don't think every single thing a phone does should be banned though. Phones have replaced paper maps, and a navigation app that says the directions out loud is probably safer than a lost driver squinting at street signs. Calling for help after a crash is a good use too, and so is reporting a dangerous driver. If the phone is mounted on the dashboard, set up before the car leaves the driveway, and controlled by voice, that seems reasonable to me. Hands-free calls still take away some of your attention, so I wouldn't say they are harmless. They are a lot easier to make a rule about though. A police officer can see a phone in somebody's hand. He can't see if their mind is wandering.

So my position is that the phone should get set up before the trip starts and then be left alone until it's over. Messages can wait twenty minutes. If something really can't wait, the driver can pull over, and that takes maybe two minutes. Compared to what could happen in the few seconds of looking down, that is a small price to pay. I think the law should make people pay it.
