I agree with the principal's decision. Making every student join at least one extracurricular activity might sound like one more thing for busy people to do, but I think it would help students a lot and make our school better.

The first reason is that a lot of students never try new things unless someone pushes them. When I started high school, I told myself I was not a club person. I went to class, went home, and did my homework. Then a teacher asked me to help the yearbook staff take pictures at a pep rally, just one time. I ended up staying the whole year. I learned how to crop a photo, how to meet a deadline, and how to argue nicely with an editor about which picture should get a full page. If nobody had pushed me, I never would have found out I liked it. A requirement works the same way. It gives shy or unsure students a reason to go through a door they would just walk past.

The second reason is that activities teach things you can't learn in a classroom. In math class I mostly work by myself and my grade depends on me. On a soccer team, a debate squad, or student council, other people depend on me. If the treasurer of student council forgets to count the money from a bake sale, the whole dance budget gets messed up. Students learn to show up on time, finish what they said they would do, and work with people they didn't pick as friends. Colleges and employers say they want these habits, so a requirement makes sure every student gets practice.

Some people say students are already too busy, especially the ones who have jobs or take care of younger brothers and sisters. That is a fair point, and the principal should deal with it. The rule only says at least one activity, not three, and the school can give students lots of choices. A student could join a club that meets once a week, volunteer in the school garden, or help build sets for the spring play. A student with a part-time job could count a few hours of tutoring younger kids. One activity won't take over someone's schedule, and for most students it is a break from studying instead of extra work.

A third reason is that activities make the school feel more like a community. Our school has over a thousand students, and it is easy to go four years only knowing the kids in your classes. When students join the same band, service club, or track team, they meet people from other grades and other groups. I have seen seniors teach freshmen how to do a relay handoff, and I have seen friendships start between kids who would never have talked in the hallway. This makes the building friendlier and helps students feel like they belong. Students who feel like they belong usually come to school more and care more about their grades.

In the end, school is for more than passing tests. It is also for growing up into people who can do things and who are curious. An extracurricular requirement is a small step toward that. It asks each student for a few hours a week and gives back confidence, teamwork, and friends. I hope my classmates support the principal's decision, and I think many of them will be glad they did once they find an activity they didn't know they would love.
