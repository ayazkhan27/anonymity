Phones and Driving

Almost everybody I know has a phone, and almost everybody I know has looked at it while driving. I have done it too. It is such a normal thing to do that it doesn't feel dangerous, and I think that is why people keep doing it. In my opinion drivers should not be allowed to hold or handle a phone while the car is moving. I don't think the law should ban every use of a phone, though. A phone on the dashboard that is giving directions is different from a phone in your hand, so the rules for them should be different.

The main reason is that driving takes your eyes, your hands and your attention, and a phone takes all of them away. Reading a text only takes a few seconds, but on the highway a car goes about the length of a football field in that time, and the driver is basically blindfolded for all of it. Nothing even has to go wrong with the car. A kid could step off the curb or a truck in front could brake. Safety agencies say thousands of people die every year because of distracted driving, and a lot of them were not the ones holding the phone. This is the part that bothers me the most. If someone doesn't wear a seat belt he is mostly risking his own life, but if someone texts while going forty miles an hour he is risking the lives of strangers who never agreed to that.

Some people say they are good enough drivers to do both at once. I doubt that. People are bad at judging their own attention, and the drivers who feel the most confident are often the ones drifting into the other lane. The only way they find out they were wrong is by crashing.

However, banning phones completely would go too far. Navigation apps have replaced paper maps, and a voice telling me to turn left in half a mile is safer than unfolding a map on the steering wheel. A phone is also how a driver calls for help after a breakdown or reports an accident. Hands-free calls are not totally safe, because a conversation still takes your mind off the road. They do keep your eyes up and your hands on the wheel, and it is hard to say how they are different from talking to a passenger. If a law banned all of this, most people would just ignore it, and then they learn to ignore the next law too.

For these reasons I think the line should be touching the phone. Drivers should set the route before they leave the driveway, let the phone talk, and keep it out of reach until the car is parked. A text can wait ten minutes, but somebody walking in the crosswalk can't wait for the driver to look up.
