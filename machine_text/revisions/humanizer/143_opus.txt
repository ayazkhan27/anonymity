Summer Projects Should Belong to Students

A lot of schools give students a summer project every June so we don't forget everything over break. I get why. If you go ten weeks without reading, writing, or doing any math, you lose a lot of what you learned that year. But who should design the projects? I think students should design their own, and teachers should just set some basic expectations and help out when needed. When students pick their own projects they actually try harder, they learn skills school doesn't really teach, and the learning feels like it belongs to them instead of being forced on them.

My first reason is motivation. Take a project the teacher assigns, like a report on a novel everyone has to read. Most people finish it the night before school starts, and they hate every minute of it. Summer is the only time of year we get to control our own time, so an assignment that feels like more homework loses to pretty much everything else. A project you picked yourself is different. Say a girl loves baking. She could look into the chemistry of why bread rises by trying different amounts of yeast and writing down what happens. A guy who is obsessed with basketball could keep track of his free throw percentage all summer and then graph it and figure out what the numbers mean. Since these projects come from things kids already like, they keep coming back to them without anyone making them, and that's what keeps them learning over the break.

Second, just designing a project teaches you something. During the school year teachers decide almost everything for us, like what we read, which questions we answer, and when stuff is due. We almost never get to plan a long assignment, set our own goals, or change the plan when something goes wrong. If a student has to come up with a question, figure out how to answer it, and keep the work going for weeks, that's the same kind of independence colleges and jobs expect. If your garden experiment dies in July, you have to figure out why and start over. You don't learn to keep going like that from a worksheet.

Third, students are all different, and letting them choose takes that into account. One assignment for the whole class assumes everybody needs the same practice and likes the same things. That's just not true. One kid might need to work on her writing while another one is ready for an advanced coding project. If students can choose, each of them can push as far as they're able to go instead of working at the class average.

Some people say that if you leave students alone, they'll pick something way too easy or just not do it. That's a fair point, but it means students need guidance, not that teachers need to control the whole thing. Teachers could have students turn in a short proposal before summer, set rules for how much work counts, and have everyone present their project in the fall. With that kind of setup, students get freedom and still have to be accountable.

The whole point of a summer project is to keep kids curious, and letting them work on what they already care about is the best way to do that.
