Learning From Home

When most people think of school they think of rows of desks, a whiteboard, and a packed hallway between classes. That works fine for a lot of students. For some of us it's a problem we deal with every day. I think students should be allowed to take classes from home online or over video calls, as long as it's a choice and nobody is forced into it.

The biggest reason is that some students' lives don't fit the normal school schedule. Some teenagers have part-time jobs because their family needs help with rent. Some have to watch their little brothers and sisters or take care of a sick relative. Athletes who travel for competitions can miss a few days of class in a row. For students like that, being able to learn from home could decide whether they keep up or fall behind. If there's a recorded lesson or a live video class, they can still learn on days when they would have just been absent.

Learning from home would also help students with health problems or disabilities. Someone getting over surgery, or someone with a weak immune system, might not be able to come to school for weeks. They could log in and take part instead of losing a whole semester. I also think students with bad anxiety might focus better somewhere calmer, where they can think about the work and not about everything stressful going on around them.

Online classes could also give students courses their own school can't offer. A small high school out in the country might not have enough kids to hire a teacher for advanced physics or Japanese. With video conferencing, a student there could join a class at another school or even a college. That means where you live wouldn't limit what you can learn as much.

People who disagree say students at home will get distracted and miss out on the social part of school, like friends, clubs, and learning to work with people in person. I'll admit that's true. Not everybody has the self-discipline to learn on their own, and not every house has a quiet room or good internet. But I think that means schools should plan online learning carefully, and it doesn't mean they should get rid of it. Schools could check in with students at home often, lend out laptops and hotspots, and push them to come in for clubs and sports when they're able to.

Students are all different, and one way of doing school is never going to work equally well for everyone. Letting students choose to learn from home takes their situations seriously and keeps more of them in school. A school with both options still has its classrooms, and it also has a way for students who can't be there to keep learning.
