Phones and Driving

Pretty much everyone has a cell phone now, and a lot of people check it all the time, even when they are driving. So people argue about whether drivers should be allowed to use phones at all while the car is moving. I think drivers should not be holding or messing with their phones while they drive. The only exceptions should be calling 911 in an emergency and using hands-free GPS that you set up before you leave.

My first reason is about paying attention. Driving seems easy after a while, but you are always having to watch for something. You have to figure out how fast the other cars are coming, keep an eye on a bike rider on the side of the road, and look out for a little kid who might chase a ball into the street. When you read a text your eyes are off the road for a few seconds, and on the highway your car goes about the length of a football field in that time. Nobody would drive that far with a blindfold on, but looking at your phone is basically the same thing.

Some people say hands-free calling fixes this. It helps some, but it doesn't solve it. Studies on distracted driving show that talking on the phone still takes your mind off the road even if both your hands are on the wheel. Talking to a friend in the passenger seat is different because they can see the road too, and they usually stop talking when traffic gets bad. The person on the phone can't see anything, so they just keep talking while you stop paying attention. That's why I think even hands-free calls should be short and only when you really need them.

I will admit the other side is right about GPS and emergencies. Most people use their phones instead of paper maps now, and a driver listening to directions is safer than one trying to read street signs or opening up a map on the steering wheel. If there's an emergency, like a crash up ahead or someone having a medical problem, a phone call could save someone's life. So the rule should allow those things, as long as you put the address in before you start driving and the phone is in a holder and not in your hand.

Everything else can wait. Texts, social media, picking songs, and email don't need an answer in the next five minutes, and no message is worth getting in a crash over. Tougher laws, police actually enforcing them, and phone settings that turn off notifications while you drive would all help. But really it is up to each driver to decide to keep their eyes on the road.
