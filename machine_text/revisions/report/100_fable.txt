Phones and the Driver's Seat

Pretty much everyone I know has a phone, and pretty much everyone I know has looked at it while driving. I only have my permit and I've already done it once at a red light, with my mom sitting right next to me. It feels so normal that you don't even think about it. I think drivers should not be allowed to hold a phone while the car is moving, but I don't think the law should ban phones from the car completely. A phone on the dashboard giving directions is not the same as a phone in your hand, and the law should treat them differently.

The reason to ban holding a phone is pretty easy to understand. To drive well you need your eyes, your hands, and your attention, and a phone wants all three. In driver's ed they told us that reading one text takes about five seconds, and on the highway that is enough time to travel the length of a football field. For that whole time you are basically driving with your eyes closed. Nothing even has to be wrong with your car. A little kid could step off the curb or the truck in front of you could brake. Thousands of people die every year because of distracted driving, and a lot of them were not the one on the phone. To me that is the most important part. If you don't wear a seat belt, you are mostly risking yourself. If you text while going forty, you are risking people who never got a choice.

Some people say they are good enough to do both. My cousin says this all the time, and he is honestly a good driver. But I think most people feel more focused than they really are, and you can't see yourself drifting over the line.

Still, I think banning phones completely would be too much. My family uses the map app every time we go somewhere new, and a voice saying "turn left in half a mile" is a lot safer than my dad trying to read directions off a piece of paper. A phone is also how you call for help if your car breaks down. Hands free calls are not perfect, because your mind is still partly on the conversation, but your eyes stay up and your hands stay on the wheel. It is pretty similar to talking to a friend in the passenger seat. Also, if a law is too strict, people just won't follow it.

So I think the rule should be about touching. Set up your directions before you leave the driveway, let the phone talk, and keep it somewhere you can't reach. A text can wait ten minutes. The person in the crosswalk matters more.
