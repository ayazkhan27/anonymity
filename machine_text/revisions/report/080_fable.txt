Our principal wants every student to join at least one extracurricular activity. I understand why. Clubs and teams can be the best part of school. Still, I disagree with making them mandatory, because a requirement would hurt the students it is supposed to help and make the activities themselves worse.

First, a lot of students just do not have the time. My friend Marcus leaves school at 2:45 and is at his job at a grocery store by 3:30, four days a week. His paycheck helps his mom pay rent. Other students watch their little brothers and sisters until a parent gets home, or ride a bus that leaves right after the last bell and does not come back. For them, a required activity is not a chance to try something new. It is one more thing to fail at, and the kids who got in trouble would be the ones whose families are already the busiest.

Second, forcing people into activities makes those activities worse. I played in the school band for two years, and I could tell who wanted to be there and who got signed up by their parents by about the second rehearsal. The ones who did not want to be there talked through practice and skipped concerts. Now imagine a yearbook staff or a soccer team where a third of the members only joined because of a rule. The students who care would have to drag everyone else along.

Third, plenty of students already do good things that the school never sees. One girl in my class takes karate at a studio downtown. Another volunteers at her church's food pantry every Saturday. A rule that only counts school activities tells them that what they do does not matter.

A better plan would be to make joining easier instead of mandatory. The school could hold an activity fair in September, run a late bus twice a week, and let some clubs meet during lunch. Students who join because they found something they like will stay, work hard, and bring their friends. Students who join because they were told to will count the days until they can quit.
