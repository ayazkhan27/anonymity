Phones and the Driver's Seat

Almost everyone I know has a phone, and almost everyone I know has looked at it while driving. I only have my permit, but I have already done it too. It was at a red light and I told myself it didn't count because the car wasn't moving. My mom was in the passenger seat and she did not agree. That little excuse is honestly why I think the rule should be simple. Drivers should not be holding a phone at all while they drive, and the only uses that should be allowed are the ones that let you keep your hands on the wheel and your eyes on the road.

My biggest reason is attention. Driving feels easy because most of the time nothing happens. My dad has driven the same road to work for like ten years and he says it is boring, which is good. But the real skill of driving is being ready for the one second when a kid runs out between parked cars or the truck in front of you slams on its brakes. Reading a text takes your eyes off the road for a few seconds. In driver's ed they told us that on the highway a car goes about the length of a football field in that time. For those seconds nobody is really driving the car. It is just moving, and everybody around is trusting that nothing changes.

A lot of people say they are good at multitasking, and I used to think I was great at it. The problem is that when you are distracted you can't notice what you missed. If I look down and nothing goes wrong, I feel like I handled it fine. I never see the guy on the bike I would have hit on a different day. So every safe trip makes me more confident, even though I didn't really prove anything. A law against holding your phone is helpful because it takes that decision away from the person who is worst at making it.

Another reason is who gets hurt. If texting only put the texter in danger, I could kind of understand people saying it is their own choice. But it doesn't work like that. The person in the crosswalk and the family in the next lane never agreed to any of it, and they deserve to get home safe. We already think this way about drunk driving. Nobody says a driver gets to decide for himself if four beers are okay. A phone in your hand is pretty similar, except people pick it up over and over in one trip.

I don't think every single thing on a phone should be banned, though. Phones are actually really useful in a car. Nobody uses paper maps anymore, and a GPS app that says the directions out loud is probably safer than a lost driver squinting at street signs. Calling 911 after a crash is a good use too. A phone on a dashboard mount, set up before you leave the driveway and controlled by voice, seems fair to me. Hands-free calls still take some of your attention, so I wouldn't say they are perfectly safe, but that rule is a lot easier to enforce. A cop can see a phone in your hand. He can't see what you are thinking about.

So my position is to set the phone up before the trip and then leave it alone until you get there. Most texts can wait twenty minutes, and your friends will still be there. If something really can't wait, you can pull over, which takes maybe two minutes. Compared to what can happen while you are looking down, that is a small price, and I think the law should make people pay it.
