Learning From Home Should Be an Option

When my cousin broke her leg in tenth grade, she was out of school for almost six weeks. Her teachers sent packets home, but nobody was there to explain the chemistry, so she spent the rest of the year catching up. If her school had let her join class by video, I don't think she would have fallen behind much at all. That is why I think students would benefit from being able to attend classes from home, as long as it is a choice and not something everybody has to do.

The biggest benefit is for students who can't get to the building. Kids are out for the flu, for injuries, for family emergencies, and for snow, and each day they are gone makes the next day harder. Usually by the third day of the flu you feel good enough to listen to a lesson, just not good enough to sit in a room with thirty other people and pass it to them. With a home option that student keeps up and everyone else stays healthy. It would also help students who live far out in the country. Some of them ride a bus for over an hour each way. That is two hours a day they could use for homework, sleep, or a job.

Learning from home also helps students who don't do well in a regular classroom. For some students a loud building is tiring because of anxiety. Others can't concentrate with classmates talking, or they get picked on in the hallway. At home they can pay attention to the actual material. Recorded lessons help too. If I don't get a math problem the first time, I can replay the explanation instead of nodding along like I understand.

I know there are problems with this. At home it is easy to open another tab, zone out, or just stop logging in. Students at home also don't get lunch with their friends, group projects, or sports. And not every family has fast internet or a quiet room. I think these are fair points, but to me they are reasons to set the option up carefully, not reasons to say no. Schools can require cameras on during discussions, check in with online students every week, lend out laptops and hotspots, and bring a student back into the building if their grades drop.

Finally, learning online is a skill we are going to need. A lot of colleges run courses online, and plenty of jobs have video meetings and expect you to manage your own time. It makes more sense to practice that in high school, while teachers are still keeping an eye on us, than to figure it out alone at eighteen.

Most students will still do best in person. But for the ones who are sick, who live far away, or who can't learn in a crowd, a seat at home is a lot better than an empty desk.
