Drivers should not use cell phones at all while they are driving. A phone is handy, but being handy is not worth the chance of a crash that could end someone's life.

Driving takes a person's full attention. Some people think hands-free calling is a good middle ground, but the research says it is not as safe as it sounds. Distraction comes in three kinds: visual, manual, and cognitive. A hands-free call gets rid of the manual part, yet the driver's mind is still on the conversation. Drivers on calls miss their exits, overlook people crossing the street, and slow down when they react to brake lights. Looking at the road is not the same as seeing it. Since the mental distraction is still there, a law that allows hands-free use just gives drivers a feeling of safety they have not earned.

Texting is even riskier. If you read a message at highway speed, you travel about the length of a football field without watching the road. Nothing on a screen is important enough for that. Most messages can wait ten minutes, and the ones that cannot, like an emergency, can be handled by pulling over first.

Some people say a full ban goes too far because phones also give us maps and music. I think there is an easy fix for that. A driver can pick a route and a playlist before leaving the driveway, and lots of newer cars have built-in systems made for use on the road. A phone that is mounted for directions and left alone is different from a phone someone is actually using. A clear rule that bans holding the phone and using it for calls or texts is also easier to enforce than a pile of exceptions, because a police officer can see a phone being used without having to guess what the driver is doing with it.

We already accept limits on how people drive, like speed limits and drunk driving laws, because everyone shares the road and one person's choices can affect many others. Phone use fits in the same group. No call or text is worth a life, so drivers should wait until the car is parked, and then they can answer everything they missed.
