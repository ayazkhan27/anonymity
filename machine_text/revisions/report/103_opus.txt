Phones and the Road

Pretty much everyone has a cell phone now, and a lot of us feel lost without one. But the worst place to use a phone is behind the wheel. I think drivers should not be allowed to hold or touch a phone at all while the car is moving. The only exception should be hands-free stuff like voice navigation that you set up before you leave.

The main reason is attention. When you drive you need your eyes, your hands, and your brain all at once. A text takes all three. If you look down for five seconds on the highway, you go about the length of a football field without seeing the road. In that time a little kid could run into the street, or the car in front of you could slam on its brakes. That is terrifying. Nobody is a good enough driver to react to something they never even saw.

A lot of people say they can handle it because they only check messages at red lights or on empty roads. The problem is habits don't stay where you put them. My cousin started out reading texts at stoplights, and a few months later he was texting while merging onto the freeway. A simple rule that says the phone stays down is easier to follow than a rule with a bunch of exceptions. It is also easier for police to enforce.

Some people argue that phones make driving safer. Maps keep drivers from getting lost and making sudden turns, and if you crash you can call for help. Those are fair points. That is why the rule should still allow a mounted phone with voice navigation and emergency calls. What it should ban is typing, scrolling, watching videos, and holding the phone up to your ear.

A call or a text can almost always wait ten minutes. A person who gets killed cannot come back. If a message really matters, you can pull over and answer it. Keeping phones out of drivers' hands is a small sacrifice, and it protects you, your passengers, and everyone else on the road.
