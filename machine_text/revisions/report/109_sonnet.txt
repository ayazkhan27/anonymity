Drivers Should Not Use Cell Phones While Driving

Almost everybody has a cell phone now, and most of us grab it dozens of times a day without even thinking about it. On a couch that is fine. In a car it is a real problem. I believe drivers should not be allowed to use handheld cell phones at all while they are driving, and the law should treat the phone as the danger it is.

The biggest problem is attention. Driving feels automatic, but it actually takes constant decisions. You have to watch the car in front of you, check your mirrors, notice someone standing at the curb, and guess what the other drivers are about to do. A phone uses up the same part of your brain. Experts talk about three kinds of distraction: visual, when your eyes leave the road, manual, when your hands leave the wheel, and cognitive, when your mind leaves the job. Texting does all three at once. If a driver looks at a message for five seconds on the highway, the car travels about the length of a football field with nobody watching the road. No safety feature in a new car can fix that.

The numbers show how serious this is. Distracted driving kills thousands of people in the United States every year, and the real number is probably higher, since drivers almost never admit they were on their phones after a crash. A lot of the people who get hurt were not even the distracted driver. They are passengers, bike riders, kids walking to school, and other drivers who were following every rule. That is why I do not think this is only about personal freedom. If someone wants to risk his own life, that is his choice. Risking the lives of strangers is a whole different thing.

Some people say hands-free calling is safe enough, so the ban should only cover texting. That sounds fair at first, but the research does not back it up. Studies with driving simulators and real traffic data found that the conversation is the distraction, not just holding the phone. A driver in the middle of a call misses more red lights and brakes later when the car ahead stops, even with both hands on the wheel and both eyes forward. The brain cannot give itself fully to a talk and to the road at the same time. It keeps switching back and forth, and every switch wastes a moment that a driver may not have. Hands-free fixes the problem with the hands, but the problem with the mind is still there.

Other people point out that drivers already talk to passengers or change the radio, so phones are no different. I disagree. A passenger is in the car and can see the road, so he or she usually stops talking when traffic gets tense. Someone on the other end of a phone cannot see the intersection and just keeps going. Also, a radio knob is quick and simple, but a phone has endless texts, alerts, and apps that were made to keep people looking. A phone is designed to be hard to ignore, and that is the worst quality for something inside a moving car.

Enforcement is a fair worry, because a law does not work if nobody follows it. Still, seat belt laws and drunk driving laws show that strong rules can change habits over time. When the fines are real and people understand why the rule exists, something that used to seem normal starts to seem careless. A simple rule that covers everything is also easier for police to enforce than a long list of exceptions. If an officer sees a phone in a driver's hand, there is no need to figure out whether the driver was dialing, reading, or doing something that was allowed.

There should be a few sensible exceptions. Someone facing a real emergency, like reporting a crash or calling for help, ought to be able to use the phone, and a map that was set up before the trip starts is reasonable too. Pulling over and then using the phone is always possible. These exceptions are small, and they do not change the main point, which is that a drive is not the time for calls, texts, or scrolling.

Nobody has to be reachable every single minute. Showing up a little late, or letting a message sit for ten minutes, costs almost nothing, and a friend will understand. The other choice could cost someone's life. Drivers should put their phones away until the car is parked, and the law should make them do it.
