Phones and the Driver's Seat

Pretty much everyone I know has a phone, and pretty much everyone I know has looked at it while driving. I have done it too. Last month I was at a red light on the way to practice and I checked a text from my mom, and I told myself it was fine because the car was not moving. People usually ask if drivers should be allowed to use cell phones "in any capacity" like the answer has to be all yes or all no. I think the best answer is in the middle. Drivers should not be allowed to hold, read, or type on a phone while the car is moving or stopped in traffic. But a phone in a mount that is giving directions, or a phone used to call for help, is a good thing and should stay legal.

The reason for keeping phones out of drivers' hands is pretty easy to understand. To drive safely you need your eyes on the road, your hands on the wheel, and your mind on what is in front of you. Texting takes all three at the same time. In driver's ed we learned that the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration says reading a text takes your eyes off the road for about five seconds. At highway speed that is about the length of a football field. I play football, so I know how long that field is, and I would never agree to drive the whole thing with my eyes closed. That is basically what a quick look at a message is. The same agency says thousands of people die every year in the United States because of distracted driving.

Phones also matter more than other distractions because the driver is not the only one on the road. Eating a sandwich or changing the radio takes some attention too, but a phone pulls harder, because a text or a call is a person who wants an answer back. The kid in the crosswalk, the guy on the bike, and the family in the next lane are all trusting the driver to pay attention. Protecting them is what laws are for. We already have speed limits and seat belt laws for the same reason, and most people agree those are fair and that they help. A phone law fits right in with them.

Some people say hands-free calling fixes everything. It fixes a lot. A driver on speaker keeps both hands on the wheel and both eyes forward, which is much better than holding the phone. But I have read that your mind still stays partly in the conversation, so you react a little slower. When my dad drives and I am talking to him, I stop talking when the traffic gets heavy because I can see it. Someone on the other end of a call can't see the road, so they keep going. I would still keep hands-free calls legal, since police could not really enforce a ban on them anyway. Drivers should just keep them short. New drivers like me, who are still learning how to handle the car, should wait until they have more experience.

There are also ways a phone makes driving safer, and a total ban would take those away. Directions are the best example. A phone in a dashboard mount that tells you out loud where to turn is a lot safer than a lost driver slowing down to read every street sign. Being able to call 911 after a crash, or to report something in the road, is another good example. A law against phones "in any capacity" would punish people for doing these helpful things. I think people respect a law more when it makes sense to them, and a law that allows the safe uses would be easier to follow.

So the rule I would support is simple enough to remember. Set the route before you pull out, put the phone in the mount, and leave it alone until the car is parked. If something really can't wait, pull over. The message will still be there in ten minutes, and so will everyone on the road. I think most drivers would be happy to follow a rule like that.
