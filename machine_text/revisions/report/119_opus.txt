Phones Belong Out of the Driver's Hands

Almost everyone carries a cell phone everywhere now, and the habit of checking it has followed people into the driver's seat. Because of that, there is a big debate about whether drivers should be allowed to use phones at all while the car is moving. I think drivers should not hold or mess with a phone in any real way while driving. The only exceptions should be emergency calls and hands-free GPS that you set up before the trip starts.

My first reason is attention. Driving seems simple once you get used to it, but it is always asking something of you. You have to judge how fast oncoming cars are going, keep an eye on a biker at the edge of the road, and watch for a little kid who might chase a ball into the street. Reading a text takes your eyes off the road for a few seconds. At highway speed, a car goes about the length of a football field in that time. Nobody would agree to drive that far with a blindfold on, but a quick look at a screen is basically the same thing.

Some people say hands-free calling solves the problem. It helps, but it doesn't fix it. Studies on distracted driving have found that a phone conversation pulls your mind away from the road even if both hands are on the wheel. Talking to a friend in the passenger seat is different, because they can see the road too and will usually stop talking when traffic gets bad. The person on the phone can't see anything, so they keep talking while the driver's focus slips. That is why I would limit even hands-free calls to short ones that really matter.

The other side does have a good point about GPS and emergencies. Phones have mostly replaced paper maps, and a driver listening to spoken directions is safer than one squinting at street signs or trying to unfold a map on the steering wheel. In an emergency, like a crash up ahead or someone having a medical problem, a phone call can save a life. A fair rule would allow these uses, as long as the route is typed in before the car moves and the phone sits in a mount instead of the driver's hand.

Everything else can wait. Texts, social media, playlists, and email don't need an answer in the next five minutes, and no message is worth a crash. Good laws, steady enforcement, and phone settings that silence notifications while driving would all help a lot. In the end, though, it comes down to each driver choosing to give the road their full attention, and that is a choice every one of us can make.
