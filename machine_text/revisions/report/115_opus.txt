Phones Belong Out of the Driver's Hands

Almost everyone has a cell phone with them all the time now, and that includes in the car. Phones give us directions, play our favorite music, and help us stay in touch with family and friends. Since they are so helpful, a lot of drivers treat their phone like it's part of the dashboard. Still, I think drivers should not hold or touch a phone at all while the car is moving. The only use that makes sense is hands-free and set up before you leave, like a GPS route that talks out loud.

The biggest reason is attention. Driving seems easy once you get used to it, but your brain is actually doing a lot. You have to watch the road, check your mirrors, figure out how fast other cars are going, and react to surprises, like a little kid running into the street after a ball. Reading a text takes about five seconds. At 55 miles per hour, a car goes about the length of a football field in that time. For those five seconds you are pretty much driving with your eyes closed. No text is worth that.

Some people say talking on the phone is the same as talking to someone in the passenger seat. Research says it isn't. A passenger can see the road too. When traffic gets busy, a good passenger usually stops talking or even helps by pointing out a car you missed. The person on the phone has no idea what's going on, so they keep talking right when you need to focus the most. Studies have shown that even hands-free calls slow down how fast drivers react, because the problem is in your head, not just your hands.

Other people say phones are really important now, especially for directions. That is true, and it's why I think there should be one exception. You can type in the address before you pull out of the driveway and let the phone give directions from a mount on the dash. What you shouldn't do is type, scroll, or pick up the phone while the car is in drive. If you need to change the route, you can pull over. Taking thirty seconds in a parking lot is easy and keeps everyone safe.

It's also about being fair to everyone else on the road. A driver who texts isn't just risking their own life. People walking, people on bikes, and other drivers never agreed to that. We already have laws against drunk driving because it puts other people in danger, and distracted driving is a lot like that. Clear rules against holding a phone while driving, with real consequences, show that the road belongs to all of us and we should look out for each other.

Cell phones are here to stay, and honestly they make life a lot better in many ways. But when you are behind the wheel, your job is to drive. Putting the phone down or turning on do-not-disturb until you get there is a small thing to do. When you think about all the lives it can protect, it's worth it every time.
