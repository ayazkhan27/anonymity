I agree with the principal that every student should have to do at least one extracurricular activity. I know some kids will complain about it at first, but clubs and teams teach you stuff you can't really learn in a classroom. They help you make friends, they teach you to be responsible, and they help you figure out what you're good at.

First, activities get kids together who would probably never talk otherwise. Our school is big, and it's easy to just hang out with the same three or four people all day. If a quiet kid joins robotics or the school play, he ends up working with people from other grades he never would have met. My cousin was really shy in ninth grade until she joined cross country. Running all those miles with her team gave her something to talk about, and by the end of the season she had a whole new group of friends. If the school makes everyone join something, then everybody gets that chance, not just the outgoing kids.

Second, activities teach responsibility in a way homework doesn't. If you're on yearbook and you miss a deadline, the whole book is late. If you're on the soccer team and you skip practice, you let the whole team down, not just yourself. You have to manage your time, show up when people are counting on you, and do what you said you would do. Colleges and jobs want people who can do that, and it's better to practice now while it doesn't matter as much if you mess up.

Third, activities help you find out what you like. A lot of people don't know they like debate or painting or coding until they actually try it. Someone who has a hard time in math might find out she's a good leader on student council, and that confidence could help her in class too. If it wasn't required, a lot of kids would never even try.

Some people say students are already too busy with jobs or helping their families. That's true for some kids, but the rule is only one activity, and the school could have clubs that meet once a week or at lunch. That's not asking a lot.

So I agree with the principal. Doing one activity helps students make friends, grow up, and figure out who they want to be.
