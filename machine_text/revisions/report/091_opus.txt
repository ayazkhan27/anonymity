I agree with my principal that every student should do at least one extracurricular activity. In class we learn facts. In activities we learn how to work with people, how to manage our time, and what we like to do.

First, activities teach teamwork. On a soccer team you can't score if nobody passes the ball. On the yearbook staff, the writers, photographers, and editors all have to meet the same deadline or the book doesn't get printed. My cousin says she uses these same skills at her job every day.

Second, activities help with time management. Last year I joined the debate club, and we had practice three afternoons a week. I thought my grades would go down. They actually went up, because I started doing my homework right after dinner instead of watching videos until ten. A lot of kids in my club said the same thing happened to them.

Third, activities help students figure out what they are interested in. A quiet kid might try out for the school play and find out they can act. Someone on student council might decide they want to study law someday.

Some people say students are already too busy. One activity is only a few hours a week, though. That is why I support the principal's decision.
