Dear Principal,

I am writing to argue against the proposed policy that would require at least a grade B average for students to take part in sports and other activities. I understand the goal of encouraging academic effort, but this policy would not help the students it is meant to help. It would hurt them.

First, many students who struggle in class do not feel like they belong anywhere until they join a team or a club. Those activities give them a reason to come to school and stay engaged. They also build friendships with adults and peers who support them. If we take that away, students with C averages may stop caring, and their grades could fall even further.

Second, sports and activities teach skills that grades do not measure. Teamwork, discipline, leadership, and time management are not learned by sitting alone in a classroom. They come from practice and competition. A student who is barred from these experiences does not get a second chance to learn them.

Finally, the policy would punish students for struggling instead of helping them. It is not fair to take away the thing that keeps a student motivated just because their grades are low. A better approach would be tutoring, study hall, or mentoring for students who are falling behind. That way they can raise their grades without losing the activities that keep them going.

For these reasons, I respectfully ask that the school keep sports and activities open to all students.

Sincerely,
A Concerned Student
