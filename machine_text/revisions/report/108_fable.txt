Phones and the Driver's Seat

Pretty much everyone I know has a phone, and pretty much everyone I know has looked at it while driving. I have too. A few months ago I was at a red light and I looked down to read a text from my friend. When I looked back up the light was already green and the guy behind me was honking. Nothing bad happened, and I was lucky, but it showed me how fast my mind can leave the road. So I think drivers should not be allowed to hold or use a phone while the car is moving. I would not ban every single use though.

The reason is pretty simple. To drive well you need your eyes, your hands, and your attention, and a phone takes all three. In driver's ed they told us that reading a text takes about five seconds, and on the highway that is like driving the length of a football field without looking. If you are typing, you are not really steering or checking your mirrors, and you are not ready to brake. The worst part is that the person who gets hurt is usually somebody else, like a kid crossing the street or a family in the next lane. A text can wait ten minutes. Someone's life is worth a lot more than that.

Some people say they are good enough to do both. I don't believe them. When you are distracted you don't feel distracted, and that is why it is so easy to keep doing it. Being confident is not the same as being good at it, and a law should not depend on what each driver thinks of himself.

I still think a total ban goes too far. Phones are our maps now, and honestly a phone on a mount reading the directions out loud is safer than a lost driver making a sudden turn. People should also be able to call 911 in an emergency. Hands-free calls are harder to decide on, because talking still takes some of your attention, but to me a short call is more like talking to a passenger than texting.

So here is the rule I would support. Set up your directions before you leave, put the phone on the mount, and don't touch it again until you are parked. Maps and emergency calls stay legal, and texting, scrolling, and holding the phone do not. That is not much to ask, and it keeps everybody on the road safer.
