Almost everybody has a phone these days, and most people check theirs more than they realize. That is why phones in cars is such a big deal. A car weighs about two tons and moves fast, and the driver has to give it all of their attention. I think drivers should not use cell phones at all while they are driving.

The main reason is how our attention works. A lot of people think they are good at multitasking, but studies on divided attention say they are not. The brain just switches back and forth between jobs, and every switch costs a little bit of awareness. If a driver on the highway looks at a text for five seconds, the car goes about the length of a football field with nobody watching the road. That is not safe, and having years of experience does not fix it.

Some people say hands-free calling is a good compromise because both hands stay on the wheel. But the danger is not only in the hands. A conversation takes up the mind too. Studies of drivers on hands-free calls found they reacted slower to hazards and missed things right in front of them, like a kind of tunnel vision. A driver can be looking at the road and still not see it. Another good thing about a full ban is that it is simple. If all phone use is against the rule, a police officer does not have to guess which kind of use is okay.

Other people say phones are needed for directions or for emergencies. I understand that, and I think there are easy answers. You can put in the route before you start the car, or mount the phone and let it give spoken directions without touching it. If there is an emergency, the driver can pull over somewhere safe and make the call. Waiting a few minutes to stop is a small price compared to a crash.

Also, driving is not just a personal choice. A distracted driver puts passengers, walkers, bikers, and other drivers in danger, and they never agreed to take that risk. We already have laws against things like drunk driving and running red lights because they hurt other people, and texting while driving belongs with those.

Changing a habit is hard, but it can be done. Drivers can turn off notifications, use do-not-disturb, or put the phone in the back seat where they cannot reach it. Every message can wait until the car is parked. A life is worth a lot more than a reply.
